Your Brain Replays the Picture to Remember It — in Bursts That Ramp Up for Seven Seconds

A study published in eLife on November 20 recorded directly from inside the brains of 13 neurosurgical patients holding images in mind. Hippocampal "ripples" — brief high-frequency bursts long tied to sleep and long-term memory — ramped up across the seven-second wait, but only on trials people got right. One reviewer calls the evidence compelling; another calls it weak. Both are in print.

Code-Breaking Math Found a Spinning Dead Star in 10 Hours. The Old Way Needed 600 Years — and Still Missed It.

A peer-reviewed paper published Thursday in Physical Review Letters borrowed lattice algorithms from post-quantum cryptography to find gamma-ray pulsar PSR J1622−7204 — spinning 261 times per second — in 15 years of public NASA Fermi data in about 10 hours of computing. The previous best method failed despite the equivalent of 600 years. One new pulsar so far; the method is the breakthrough.

NASA confirmed Thursday that Voyager 1 — launched in 1977, traveling at 38,000 mph — has become the first human-made object one light-day from Earth: 16.1 billion miles, so far that its signal, moving at light speed, took 24 hours to arrive.
