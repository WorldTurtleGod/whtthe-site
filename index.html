The House Passed a $1.7 Trillion Law Without a Quorum, the Fifth Circuit Says

On Friday, the en banc Fifth Circuit ruled 9–8 that the House violated the Constitution's Quorum Clause when it passed the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2023 by counting 226 proxy-recorded members toward a quorum. The judgment itself is narrow — one pregnancy-workers provision, unenforceable against Texas — but the reasoning points at everything else in that omnibus.

A Second Judge Stopped Trump's 250-Foot Arch. This Order Has No End Date.

Judge Leonie Brinkema blocked the Memorial Circle arch from the bench Friday. Her opinion says it was planned "without congressional approval" and in violation of several laws. A separate D.C. order runs only to Dec. 3.

Tech & AI

Claude and a Mathematician Claim the Riemann Hypothesis. The Code Checks. The Prize Hasn't Moved.

On Friday, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge announced he and Claude had proved the Riemann hypothesis, posed in 1859 — releasing the argument as Lean code, not a paper. On Saturday, independent researchers said they had recompiled and certified it. The Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the problem as unsolved, and this paper prints all three facts in the same breath.

Science

Code-Breaking Math Found a Spinning Dead Star in 10 Hours. The Old Way Needed 600 Years — and Still Missed It.

A peer-reviewed paper published Thursday in Physical Review Letters borrowed lattice algorithms from post-quantum cryptography to find gamma-ray pulsar PSR J1622−7204 — spinning 261 times per second — in 15 years of public NASA Fermi data in about 10 hours of computing. The previous best method failed despite the equivalent of 600 years. One new pulsar so far; the method is the breakthrough.

The Government Posted Its Bigfoot Files. Its Own Biologist Wasn't Buying It.

The U.S. Forest Service quietly put three "Bigfoot/Sasquatch Records" in its FOIA reading room on Oct. 15. They include a 1982 investigation of 15-inch tracks, a hoax warning from a Border Patrol tracker, and a 1949 elk carcass that went missing.

$3.79 Billion in a Day, 30 Frames on the Couch: GTA VI's Record Launch Meets Its Players

Grand Theft Auto VI launched November 19, and Take-Two announced an estimated $3.79 billion in day-one retail sales with 32.5 million units sold pre-release — led with dollars and units, not "reach." Strange

He Set Himself on Fire and Rode a Zip Line 568 Feet. The Record Book Says It Counts.

Polish stuntman Stanisław Madej, 43, performed the farthest distance full body burn on a zip wire — 173.3 meters, longer than an American football field — in Łódź on October 3, more than doubling the 2023 record. Guinness published the record Friday. Face exposed, gel only, hoses standing by. Do not try this at home; he very much did not improvise it.

For 12 years, Spitting Image put Britain's most powerful people on Sunday-night TV as grotesque latex puppets. Fluck died Saturday at home in Cornwall. Politicians, he once said, lined up to be mocked.

The Fire Took Three Barns. The Town Built the Christmas Shop Back.

In August 2025, fire destroyed three barns at Shawna and Chris Baldwin's home in Medina, New York — including the Christmas shop and years of handmade stock. Two wooden angels survived. This month the shop is open again, rebuilt with help from the local Amish community, her church, friends and family.

Dragos researcher HD Moore planted unique keys and links in AI requests and tracked who used them. He found three hacked AI hosts, resellers keeping customer prompts, and "Zero Data Retention" flags that were ignored, including by a Chinese AI lab whose models U.S. companies resell.

Just 28 light-years away, a red dwarf has been circling a white dwarf it should not have survived, while a second white dwarf orbits them both. Astronomers say no comparable system is known within about 100 light-years of Earth.
