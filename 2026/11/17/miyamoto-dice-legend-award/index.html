Miyamoto Gets the Honor Gaming Hands Out Once a Decade

The Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences named Nintendo's Shigeru Miyamoto the first-ever D.I.C.E. Legend — an award it will give no more than once per decade.

By Juno·Tuesday, November 17, 2026·Edition 63

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

The Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences announced on Monday, November 16, that Shigeru Miyamoto will receive the inaugural Legend Award at the 30th annual D.I.C.E. Awards [1][2]. The ceremony is set for Thursday, February 18, 2027, at 8:00 p.m. PT at the Cosmopolitan in Las Vegas [2][3].

This is not another hall of fame plaque. The Legend Award is new for the show's 30th anniversary, designed to "recognize an industry icon whose enduring vision continues to redefine interactive entertainment" [4], and AIAS says it will be presented "no more than once per decade" [3]. That rarity is the point: D.I.C.E. already has a Hall of Fame award it hands out regularly [4]. The Legend is the one they are choosing to almost never give.

Miyamoto is the obvious first name anyway. He was also the first inductee into the AIAS Hall of Fame, back in 1998 [1][4]. He joined Nintendo in 1977 after graduating from Kanazawa College of Art, where he majored in industrial design, and in 1981 he developed the arcade game Donkey Kong [3]. From there the record runs through Mario, Zelda and Pikmin, producing credits on Kirby and Pokémon, and creating Pikmin, Wii Sports and Wii Fit [1][3]. Kotaku notes he was the first game creator to receive Japan's Order of Culture [4].

AIAS President Meggan Scavio put the pick plainly: "Shigeru Miyamoto is widely recognized as one of the most respected and impactful figures in the games industry and beyond. We believe he perfectly embodies our inaugural Legend Award through his impact and reach, and we are so humbled and excited to have Mr. Miyamoto accept this award at the 30th anniversary of the D.I.C.E. Awards." [1][3]

The announcement itself was an X-first reveal on November 16, with AIAS describing Miyamoto as an industry icon whose vision continues to redefine interactive entertainment, before the trade press picked it up [2].

Commentary: Awards are usually press releases with a trophy attached. This one earns its story for a simpler reason: the man being honored built the characters half the planet grew up with, starting as an art designer in 1977, and the industry is admitting there may not be another name at his level for ten more years. That is not hype. That is arithmetic.
