A Stroke Changed Geralt's Polish Voice. CD Projekt Used AI, With His Blessing, to Turn Back the Clock.

Jacek Rozenek still records every new line for The Witcher 3's Songs of the Past expansion. An AI voice firm then makes him sound like he did a decade ago, so the new scenes don't clash with the old ones.

By Herodotus·Friday, November 13, 2026·Edition 59

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Voices age. Characters, inconveniently, do not. CD Projekt Red just ran into that problem with its most famous hero, and its answer is going to be argued about.

For Songs of the Past, the new expansion for The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, the studio worked with AI voice company Respeecher to modify the voice of Jacek Rozenek, who has played Geralt in every Polish-language Witcher game. Game File broke the story, and PC Gamer reported it on Nov. 12. [1]

Rozenek had a stroke in 2019 that left him temporarily unable to speak. By 2021 he had recovered enough to record a few new lines for the game's next-gen update, but PC Gamer says the process left him "exhausted." He kept training and returned to work, and CD Projekt wanted him back as Geralt. [1] The catch was that his voice had changed. Game File's Stephen Totilo described it as "lower [and] gruffer." [1] Because Songs of the Past takes place in the middle of The Witcher 3's story, the new lines would sit right beside recordings from more than a decade ago. [1]

So Rozenek still performs every line in the Polish version, and Respeecher reshapes the recordings to sound like his younger self. [1] "The brief was simple: make Jacek's voice sound alive, rich and young, like in the original game," synthetic speech artist Dmytro Vasylets said. The hard part, he added, was Rozenek's emotional range: "the half-tones, shouts, groans, anger, fear and laughter." [1]

Rozenek is all for it. "The point is that this voice should be as close as possible to my voice from 12 years ago," he said. "I have great trust…for the creators of this game." [1]

The studio is careful to call this a one-off. Localization producer Mikołaj Szwed told Game File that AI in voice work "is a big concern" for someone who works with actors around the world, but "this was a special case." [1] CD Projekt does not plan anything similar for Doug Cockle, Geralt's English voice, and would not alter Rozenek's voice in any future game set later in Geralt's life, where an older voice would make sense. [1]

It is not the studio's first time with Respeecher. CD Projekt previously used it, with the family's blessing, to recreate the voice of the late Polish actor Miłogost Reczek, who played Viktor Vektor and Vesemir. [1]

Commentary. Most fights over AI voices are about consent: a studio cloning an actor who never agreed, or never got paid. This case sits at the other end. The actor is alive, working, paid, and asking for it, and the tool is giving back something illness took. That is a good test of a principle worth keeping: the voice belongs to the person, and the person gets to decide. Whether the same rule protects actors at studios less careful than this one is the bigger question, and it won't be settled by one expansion.
