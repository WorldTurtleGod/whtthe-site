Rosalía Wins Album of the Year at the Latin Grammys for a Record Third Time

The Spanish star took five trophies in Las Vegas for Lux and became the first woman to win the top album prize three times. Argentina's Milo J, 20, beat her for record of the year, and Daddy Yankee brought Diljit Dosanjh on stage.

By Herodotus·Friday, November 13, 2026·Edition 59

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Some artists win an award and say thank you. Rosalía won one on Thursday night and thanked her doubters.

"To all the people who told me I wasn't good enough, or that my music was too weird, I'm thankful I didn't listen," she said while collecting the album of the year prize at the Latin Grammys in Las Vegas. "Now I'm winning these prizes." [1]

The award, for her album Lux, made her the first woman to win best album at the Latin Grammys three times, BBC News reports. She won it before for El Mal Querer in 2019 and Motomami in 2022. [1] She went home with five trophies in all: album of the year; song of the year and best pop song for La Perla; best alternative music album; and best short-form music video for Sauvignon Blanc. [1] She also opened the show with a medley of her biggest songs. [1]

She did not sweep, though. La Perla lost record of the year to Niño, by 20-year-old Argentine singer Milo J, who beat Karol G, Jorge Drexler, Carin León and Rosalía for the prize. He looked surprised, took home four trophies, and called Niño the song "I'm most proud of out of everything I've created so far." His sign-off: "Long live Argentine folk music." [1]

Puerto Rican reggaeton veteran Daddy Yankee was named person of the year and was presented a lifetime achievement award by Janet Jackson, who told him in Spanish that his music "has inspired an entire generation." [1] His set ran through Gasolina and Con Calma with LL Cool J and French Montana on stage. It also brought out Punjabi singer Diljit Dosanjh, his partner on the track Pokito, who paused his tour in India to become the first Indian artist to perform at the Latin Grammys. [1]

Other winners included ARIA VEGA for best new artist and Sara Schell for songwriter of the year. [1] BBC News adds that Lux is tipped as a favorite when nominations for the main Grammys are announced next week. [1]

Commentary. A Barcelona singer, an Argentine folk revivalist, a Puerto Rican reggaeton pioneer and a Punjabi star sharing one stage in Nevada is a nice reminder of how culture actually travels: not by decree, but because people choose to listen. Nobody planned that lineup from the top. Audiences built it.
