A Senator Wants 42% More for the Pentagon Before Jan. 3. His Own Leader Put It on Ice.

On Meet the Press, Sen. John Kennedy said he has spent 16 months failing to get Majority Leader John Thune to move a party-line, $350 billion defense bill. Thune and the Budget chairman told NBC the effort is shelved.

By Publius·Monday, November 23, 2026·Edition 69

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Republicans have six weeks left in control of Congress. One Republican senator spent Sunday morning on national television complaining that his own leader won't use that time to pass a big military spending increase.

What Kennedy said. On NBC's Meet the Press on Nov. 22, Sen. John Kennedy, R-La., said Congress is "not doing our job." "The president has his right to ask for 42% more spending on defense. The Democrats will never do that. The only way to do that is with Republican votes. And the only way to do that is through a reconciliation bill." [1] Then he went after Senate Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D.: "I have tried and failed, for 16 months, to convince Senator Thune, who has total control of the Senate and my Senate colleagues, to do that. I have failed, no disrespect to anybody. You can lead a man to Congress, but you can't make him think." [1][2]

Reconciliation is a budget procedure that lets certain spending and tax bills pass the Senate with a simple majority instead of the 60 votes needed to end a filibuster. [2]

What's in the request. The administration split its $1.5 trillion defense request into $1.15 trillion in the regular budget and $350 billion through reconciliation, Daily Beltway reported. The reconciliation part would pay for the Golden Dome missile-defense program, critical munitions and most of a $55 billion push for a new Defense Autonomous Warfare Group. [2] On Nov. 12, President Trump posted on Truth Social that Senate leaders should "IMMEDIATELY move on the 350 Billion Dollar Reconciliation Bill," and War Secretary Pete Hegseth has said $350 billion is needed "at a minimum," according to the same report. [2]

Thune's answer. Thune and Senate Budget Committee Chairman Ron Johnson told NBC News the effort is on ice and that Republicans are instead seeking Democratic support for military funding. [3] Not every Republican wants the money. Daily Beltway reported that Sen. Rand Paul, R-Ky., called the proposal "tone-deaf" and vowed to oppose spending that could prolong the Iran war. [2] Senate appropriators released a regular fiscal 2027 defense bill Nov. 16 at nearly $1.1 trillion, moving about $69 billion of the requested one-time money into ordinary discretionary spending, Daily Beltway reported, citing Breaking Defense. [2]

Democrats take control of both chambers Jan. 3, including a 56-seat Senate majority. After that, any party-line defense bill is dead. [2][3]

The rest of the interview. In the same interview he called Russia's Vladimir Putin and China's Xi Jinping "predators," said President Trump should use the Russia sanctions law Congress passed, and said he wants Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent under oath about Nvidia chips reaching China. [1]

Commentary. The founders put the power of the purse in Congress on purpose. In Federalist No. 41, Madison defended the power to raise armies while warning that "a standing force, therefore, is a dangerous, at the same time that it may be a necessary, provision." A 42% increase in one year, passed by one party in a lame-duck session through a procedure designed for budget housekeeping, in the middle of the Iran war, is not how you handle a dangerous provision. Kennedy is right that a majority has the right to try. Thune and Paul are right that $350 billion more for the Pentagon, rushed through with no Democratic votes in the last six weeks before voters handed power to the other party, deserves to wait for the Congress the voters just elected. Make the case in January and win the votes.
