She Stole a Jet in GTA VI Without Touching the Controller. Her Brain Pressed the Buttons.

Streamer Perri Karyal posted a clip Sunday of an EEG headset turning three trained thoughts into button presses. Her eyes and head did the steering, and she says the stunt took ages.

By Publius·Monday, November 23, 2026·Edition 69

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Grand Theft Auto VI has been out four days. Players have already stolen the military jet, a series ritual that goes back to San Andreas. Perri Karyal did it without using her hands on the controller.

The British streamer, who goes by Perrikaryal, posted the clip on X on Sunday, Nov. 22. "I STOLE A JET WITH MIND CONTROL IN GTA6," she wrote. "this took me AGES you have no idea." [1][3] French tech site Frandroid reported Monday morning that the video had passed 620,000 views within hours. [1]

How it works. Karyal explained the setup in the same post. "This electroencephalogram (EEG) records my brain activity and when I'm imagining something I've trained the software to recognise (like pushing a cube forward), a button will be pressed on my virtual xbox controller." [1][2][3] For this run she trained three mental images. She imagined pushing a boulder to accelerate, got angry to interact with vehicles, and imagined a cricket hopping to jump. [1][2][3]

The headset is not reading her mind, and she does not claim it is. The software learns an electrical pattern she can reproduce on demand. When it recognizes that pattern with enough confidence, a script presses the matching button, Frandroid explained. [1] The analog sticks, which aim the camera and steer the character and then the plane, run on other inputs. "I use eyetracking and gyro for joysticks," she wrote. [1][2] The gyroscope tracks the tilt of her head, so her eyes and head steer while her brain handles the buttons. [1][3]

So the honest headline is "hands-free," not "thought-only." Karyal said that herself, in the post that went viral, which is more candor than most tech launches offer.

Not her first game. Karyal holds a master's degree in psychology and first ran into EEG at university, according to Frandroid. She drew attention in early 2023 by playing Elden Ring with a headset and later finished it, using her brain for everything except movement. [1] Hong Kong tech site unwire.hk cited a 2023 IEEE Spectrum interview in which she said she trained the system 429 times to recognize a single "push forward" thought. [3] In a 2024 interview with the French outlet L'Atelier, she said her setup could press only one button at a time by thought, with about a one-second delay between thought and action, OnPoint 4 Gamers reported. [2]

The platform also had to be worked around. Her rig is built for PC, and GTA VI is so far available only on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Frandroid noted she had to route around similar limits to play Elden Ring Nightreign on PS5. [1] Her post did not say exactly how she connected this run to the console.

Commentary. Strip away the "mind control" headline and this is an accessibility story that one gamer built in public. A consumer EEG headset, eye tracking and a head gyro let someone fly a stolen jet in the biggest game of the decade without using her hands. One person with a psychology degree, a lot of patience and a willingness to show her failed attempts on stream got it done. Publishers that still treat accessibility as an afterthought should note how far one player got on her own.

She ended her post by asking her followers what she should try next. [3]
