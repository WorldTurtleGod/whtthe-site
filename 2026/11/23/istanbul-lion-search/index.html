Something Resembling a Lion Is Loose in Istanbul's Woods, and the Thermal Cameras Keep Losing It

On Sunday night, surveillance cameras in Istanbul's Sarıyer district caught an animal officials assessed as resembling a lion. Thermal cameras found it, a tranquilizer attempt reportedly failed, and by Monday morning the search had run more than 12 hours with no capture announced. Where it came from, nobody has confirmed.

Turkish security and wildlife personnel were searching on Monday for an animal believed to be a lion, roaming loose in woodland north of Istanbul, the country's national parks authority said [1]. Personnel at a police station in the Zekeriyaköy area, north of the European side of Istanbul, reported seeing "an animal resembling a lion" on surveillance cameras at around 9 p.m. (1800 GMT) on Sunday, November 22, the parks authority said in a statement posted on X [1]. Turkish television broadcast surveillance and thermal camera footage showing what appeared to be a large cat walking through undergrowth, and teams searching woodland in outer Istanbul's Sarıyer district, which borders the Bosphorus strait and the Black Sea [1].

"Teams equipped with thermal imaging cameras were dispatched to the area to allow more comprehensive searches, and residents living nearby were given the necessary warnings," the parks authority statement said [1]. "Following operations in the wooded area, the animal's location was identified. Efforts to capture it are continuing, with a team that includes specialist veterinarians on site" [1].

The longer account adds the overnight shape of the hunt, and this paper grades it as reported, not confirmed. The animal was first recorded around 6 p.m. Sunday in the Uskumruköy neighbourhood, according to reports citing the Istanbul Governorate [2]. Authorities reportedly located the animal and attempted to tranquilize it with a dart, but their efforts were unsuccessful, and although thermal cameras detected its heat signature, officials lost track of it during the night [2]. By Monday morning, November 23, the search had continued for approximately 12 hours without a reported capture [2]. Sarıyer Mayor Oktay Aksu, noting the animal moves fast and constantly changes location, called on citizens not to leave their homes [2]. The Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's Office launched an investigation into who owns the animal and how it reached the area; no owner has been identified and officials have not confirmed the animal's origin [2].

Print the identification exactly as officials printed it: an animal "resembling a lion," assessed in Turkish reporting as a lioness — an assessment, not a confirmed identification [1][2]. Lions are not native to Istanbul. As of Monday morning's reporting, it had not been caught [1][2].

Somewhere in the woods north of a city of millions, a large cat that nobody will claim is walking through the undergrowth on camera, found by heat and lost by dark, while gendarmes, veterinarians, and thermal drones work the tree line and the mayor tells everyone to stay home. The record book can wait. First, somebody has to catch it.
