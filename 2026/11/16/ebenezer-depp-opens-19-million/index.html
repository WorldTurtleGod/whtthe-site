Johnny Depp Played Scrooge and Came Out on Top. Not by Much.

Paramount's Ebenezer opened at No. 1 with $19 million in North America, Depp's first major movie role in eight years, just under the $20 million tracking had pointed to.

By Herodotus·Monday, November 16, 2026·Edition 62

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

A Christmas Carol is a story about a man who gets a second chance. It was a fitting choice for Johnny Depp.

Ebenezer, Ti West's PG-13 take on the Dickens story, opened at No. 1 this weekend with $19 million from 3,535 theaters in the U.S. and Canada, and $23 million from 63 overseas markets, for a $42 million global start, according to Variety. [1] It is Depp's first major movie role in eight years, since 2018's Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald. [1]

Deadline called it the best of times and the worst of times: a first-place finish, but under the $20 million that tracking had suggested. [2] The trade points to a few reasons. Everyone already knows the plot; Deadline called it "a 183 year-old story." And moviegoers often hold back the weekend before a big release, with The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping arriving next weekend. [2]

The crowd that showed up liked it. Audiences gave it an A- CinemaScore and a 90% audience rating on Rotten Tomatoes, Deadline reported, while critics were cooler. [2] Half the audience came as Depp fans, and enthusiasm climbed with age, peaking at a 78% "definite recommend" among viewers over 55. [2] Depp took a $5 million upfront fee, well below his past average, plus a share of the back end, on a film that cost a net $38 million. [2]

The rest of the chart was soft. The Cat in the Hat was second with $11.4 million, Godzilla Minus Zero fell 73% to $8.3 million in third, and David Leitch's How to Rob a Bank opened to about $5 million in seventh. [2]

Christmas movies are a long game. Deadline noted they pay off over years in a studio's library, and Ebenezer has Thanksgiving and December still ahead. [2] Scrooge, after all, didn't change in one night. Well, he did. But it took three ghosts.
