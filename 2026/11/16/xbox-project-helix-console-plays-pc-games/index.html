Xbox's Next Console Is a Console That Plays PC Games — Not the Other Way Around

In its free 25th Anniversary Magazine, published Nov. 15, Xbox finally defined Project Helix in one sentence — and the sentence is a promise about which machine serves which.

By Juno·Monday, November 16, 2026·Edition 62

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Every next-generation announcement is usually a fog of teraflops. Xbox chose a definition instead.

In the Xbox 25th Anniversary Magazine, published free on Xbox Wire on Nov. 15, Jason Ronald, Xbox's VP of Next Generation, gave the clearest description yet of Project Helix, the successor to the Series X|S: "Helix is designed to be a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC. It is not a PC that can also play console games." [1][2]

The order of those clauses is the whole product. Console first: instant, easy access the moment you sit down, with deeper customizability and performance settings available for players who want them. PC library second, as an addition — not a Windows desktop wearing a controller. Ronald framed the ambition the same way: "When we think about Project Helix, it's really designed to be the ultimate gaming machine–one that removes all the barriers of play that have historically existed." [1]

The library promise is the part players will hold them to. "My entire library is accessible to me. I'm able to bring four generations of my Xbox library and progression with me and have it available instantly. And we remove the arbitrary constraints between console and PC games." Four generations means the original Xbox, the 360, the Xbox One and the Series X|S — plus progression carrying over. And Ronald explicitly waved off the spec-sheet arms race: "We don't think the next generation is defined by 8K, or 240 hertz, or some of the traditional metrics–it's really about gameplay innovation." [1]

What Xbox did not announce matters as much as what it did. The magazine gave no price, no release date and no final specifications. The ROG Xbox Ally handheld and the Xbox mode for Windows were cited as the direction — a unified Xbox interface across living room, handheld and cloud — not as a template being copied directly. Earlier this year, at GDC on March 11, Microsoft had confirmed a custom AMD chip co-designed for next-generation DirectX and FSR, with alpha development hardware from 2027. [1][3]

So discount the noise accordingly. Prices between $1,200 and $1,800, a Holiday 2028 launch, specific chip specs and confirmed Steam or Epic storefront access are circulating on X and in rumor coverage — none of it was confirmed by Microsoft in the magazine. What is confirmed is a philosophy, stated on the record by the man building the machine, on the brand's 25th anniversary: the living room box stays a living room box, and the PC library comes to it, not the reverse.

Players have heard "best of both worlds" before, usually right before a machine that is good at neither. Helix's test is simple and public: turn it on, and your four generations and your progression are just there. Xbox wrote the test itself. Now it has to pass it.
