The Fire Took Three Barns. The Town Built the Christmas Shop Back.

In August 2025, fire destroyed three barns at Shawna and Chris Baldwin's home in Medina, New York — including the Christmas shop and years of handmade stock. Two wooden angels survived. This month the shop is open again, rebuilt with help from the local Amish community, her church, friends and family.

Shawna's Barn, at 3502 Fruit Ave. in Medina, New York, is a seasonal Christmas shop run by Shawna and Chris Baldwin with their family friend and partner Richard Monica [1]. In August 2025, a fire destroyed three barns at the Baldwins' family home — including a 250-year-old structure they had spent considerable time restoring, and the building that housed the Christmas shop, along with the merchandise inside, stock they had spent years collecting and creating [1].

What survived is the detail everyone in Medina will remember. Two wooden angels came through the fire and remain on the property today; a plastic M&M character now greets customers from the front porch, and a Fisher-Price Little People Buffalo Bills set also made it through [1]. Shawna Baldwin: "It's amazing and strange some of the things that survived." "Those two angels were definitely watching over us" [1].

When she decided to reopen, the help arrived the way it does in places that still work. Members of the local Amish community, her church, friends and family stepped in to help the family rebuild [1]. "We had so much love and support from the community, which helped make reopening possible," she said [1]. The grand reopening was announced for November 6, and the shop is open this weekend [1][2]. This paper prints no hours: the November 21 report and the shop's own flyer describe the November weekends differently, and an unverified schedule helps nobody drive to Medina.

The shop itself started the way common-man culture usually does — with a cancelled table and a barn. In 2020, COVID-19 forced the cancellation of Niagara-Orleans BOCES' annual craft show, where Shawna Baldwin, a secretary at BOCES, had sold her handmade items [1]. She welcomed shoppers to her own barn instead, following the state guidelines then in place, and word spread — customers came from Syracuse, Canada and Dansville [1]. The Baldwins expanded the barn with a porch and more shopper space, finished and decorated with family and friends [1].

Almost everything on the shelves is made by the three of them. Much of the merchandise is handmade by the Baldwins and Monica, a retired art teacher who spent 34 years teaching in Spencerport [1]. Chris Baldwin, retired, makes many of the wooden items and maintains the property; scrap wood left over from the barn's construction became miniature villages, churches, lanterns and ornaments [1]. Monica, who keeps eight Christmas trees in his own home, helps create items and displays [1]. The shelves hold one-of-a-kind creations, vintage ornaments and antiques, handmade decorations, repurposed items, angels, snowmen, Santas, fresh wreaths and swags [1]. A favorite piece is an antique cradle dating to the 1800s, displaying dolls and bears of the late Nancy Berger and her mother, the late Mildred Wilson Baker [1]. Preparing for Christmas is year-round work: they begin making merchandise in January, some pieces taking nearly a year to complete [1].

Monica recalled one customer who came in saying she did not like Christmas, looked around, left — and came back to tell them they "know how to do Christmas" [1]. "This is why we do what we do," he said. "It's a magical season" [1].

Commentary: No program absorbed this shop and no organisation took it over. A school secretary lost her craft-show table in 2020 and opened her barn instead; a retired husband turned construction scrap into miniature churches; and when fire took three barns and years of handmade stock, the Amish, the church, and the neighbors helped build it back in time for the season they make all year for. Two wooden angels survived the fire. So did the shop. That is common-man culture in its working clothes.
