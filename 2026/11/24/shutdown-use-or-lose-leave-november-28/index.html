Federal Workers: Schedule Your Use-or-Lose Leave by Saturday. A December Shutdown Could Eat It Otherwise.

Senate Republicans now say they're open to Democrats' demand to cut off new money for the Iran war, but there's no deal and the Dec. 11 deadline is 17 days out. OPM's rules set a Nov. 28 deadline for protecting leave that a shutdown could wipe out. Christmas Day would bring no holiday pay during a lapse.

By Publius·Tuesday, November 24, 2026·Edition 70

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

If you're a federal employee with more annual leave than you can carry into next year, the date that matters this week isn't Dec. 11. It's Saturday, Nov. 28.

First, the news. Senate Republican leaders have opened the door to the trade Democrats want. The Washington Post reported Monday, Nov. 23, that key Senate Republicans are willing to consider funding the Pentagon while cutting off money for the Iran war, a conflict Congress never authorized. [1] "I'm looking at language that might be able to do that," Appropriations Chair Susan Collins (R-Maine) said, and Majority Leader John Thune (R-S.D.) said he's open "to anything that keeps the government funded and makes sure that we've got our military and national security requirements and needs met." [1][2]

There's still no deal. "I think there are some good conversations happening. No deals yet," Thune said before the Thanksgiving recess, The Hill reported Tuesday. [3] Negotiators are sketching a bill that would fund the government through mid-April and carry the annual defense authorization, disaster relief and farm aid. Talks may not move until Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer gets past his Dec. 1 leadership election against Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-Md.), who is pushing from the left for Iran limits. [3] Trump has demanded a $350 billion Pentagon budget resolution instead. GOP sources told The Hill the votes aren't there. [3] "A government shutdown is a greater probability every day that goes by that Republicans and Democrats aren't talking to each other," GOP strategist Ron Bonjean said. [3]

Now the part that hits your pay stub.

Leave. The 2026 leave year ends Jan. 9, 2027. OPM's deadline to schedule "use-or-lose" annual leave is Nov. 28, 2026. [4] OPM's shutdown guidance, dated Jan. 30, 2026, says a lapse in appropriations "qualifies as an exigency of the public business," so agencies "must restore any annual leave that was forfeited because of the lapse," whether you were furloughed or excepted. That only applies if the leave "was properly scheduled in advance," in writing, before that deadline. OPM says it will not waive the scheduling rule during a shutdown. [5] One catch: leave that was already restored once and expires this year cannot be restored again "unless Congress enacts legislation providing otherwise." [5] The sources don't fully agree here. FedTools, a federal-employee site, wrote that nothing it read says a lapse counts as an exigency. OPM's own guidance says it does. [5][6]

Christmas. A lapse that starts Saturday, Dec. 12, would run through Dec. 25. OPM says that during a lapse, "no lapse-affected employee may receive holiday time off pay." Excepted employees who aren't needed on the holiday are put in furlough status for that day. [5]

Health insurance. Your FEHB coverage continues even if your agency can't pay premiums on time. Your share piles up and gets withheld from your pay once you're back in pay status. You can't cancel coverage to save money during a shutdown furlough unless it's Open Season or you have a qualifying life event. [5]

Paychecks. FedTools, matching the GSA and NFC payroll calendars, says the paycheck for Nov. 29 to Dec. 12, due around Dec. 23 or 24, covers fully funded work. The first check at risk would be for Dec. 13 to 26, due Dec. 31 on GSA's calendar or Jan. 7, 2027 on NFC's, if the lapse is still going at the payroll cutoff. [6] That's the site's own reading of the calendars, so check with your payroll provider. Back pay rules are the same as we reported Nov. 21.

Commentary. The power of the purse belongs to Congress. Madison called it in Federalist No. 58 "the most complete and effectual weapon" the people's representatives have. Using it to rein in a war Congress never voted for is exactly what it's for. But a shutdown fight shouldn't be paid for by the GS-7 who spends Christmas without a paycheck, waiting on Congress for back pay, and loses leave she earned. Congress can wage this fight and protect the workforce in the same bill. Until it does, put your leave request in writing before Saturday.
