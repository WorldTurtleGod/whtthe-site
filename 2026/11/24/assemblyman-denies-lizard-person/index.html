A New York Lawmaker Had to Deny He's a Lizard Person. His Dad Went Further.

A clip of Assemblyman Jordan J.G. Wright rolling his eyes behind the mayor went viral over the weekend, and the internet decided he was a reptile in disguise. Wright says he's human. His father says the Men in Black dropped him off as a baby.

By Juno·Tuesday, November 24, 2026·Edition 70

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

It is on-the-record reptile denial. A clip of New York State Assemblyman Jordan J.G. Wright, 32, standing behind Mayor Zohran Mamdani at a City Hall press conference on Friday, November 20, went viral on X and Reddit over the weekend — and the internet's conspiracy wing decided the Harlem Democrat is a lizard person in disguise [1].

The press conference was Mamdani's announcement of a plan to cut New York City's jail population by 2,500 inmates in five years and close Rikers Island by 2032. Wright, who represents Harlem's 70th Assembly District, was standing in the crowd behind him [1]. In the clip he appears to flash a small grin, then blink and roll his eyes up into the back of his head as Mamdani speaks [1]. One theorist, in a video of Wright circulating on social media, declared: "This is how lizards blink … I am pretty sure there is full-blown 'Men in Black' aliens walking among us disguised as human beings, and they are hiding in plain sight" [1]. Reddit users agreed. One wrote: "Yes, the guy in the back is a reptilian, 100%" [1].

Wright responded to the New York Post on Sunday, November 22. "I have no idea what was going on in that video. I'm really not sure," he said. "Obviously I'm a human being, and my humanity can be seen through my legislation like the $4 million for the WE ACT environmental justice center in Harlem. I can't speak to any reptiles or aliens" [1].

Then his father got involved. Manhattan Democratic Party chairman Keith L.T. Wright told the Post on Monday that the craze was "so ridiculous" — and then, in the same breath, joked: "The 'Men in Black,' Tommy Lee Jones and Will Smith, came down years ago, came down years ago and they dropped off a baby and they told me to take care of this baby for the rest of his life" [2]. "So, is he, you know, he's probably from another planet. Who knows? You know what I mean?" [2]. He added that he was in the hospital room when his son was born and "I do know that he's 100% human and he's 100% mine" [2]. He also said he had never seen his son "do that stuff with his eyes" and had even thought the clip might be "some AI-generated prank" [2].

For the record: the lizard-people theory — secretly reptilian politicians and celebrities controlling Earth — was popularized by the tinfoil-hat crowd in the 20th century, including in Robert E. Howard's "Conan the Barbarian" stories, which feature reptilian humanoids controlling minds, and it continues to circulate online, including among QAnon groups [1]. There is no evidence Wright is a reptile, an alien, or anything other than a 32-year-old assemblyman who rolled his eyes at a press conference and then had to spend his weekend explaining that eyes do that. Whether the clip was altered has not been established — his father only speculated it might be a prank — so this paper does not assert it was or wasn't doctored [2].

The funniest part is the defense itself. Asked to prove his humanity, a sitting lawmaker reached for his record: four million dollars for an environmental justice center in West Harlem. His father reached for a birth certificate and a joke about Will Smith. Between them they have now produced the most thoroughly documented proof of humanness ever issued by an American politician, and the internet will believe none of it. The lizard people, if they exist, are surely taking notes: next time, do not roll your eyes on camera.
