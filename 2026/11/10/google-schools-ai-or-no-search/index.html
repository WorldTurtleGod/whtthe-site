Google Tells Schools: Take the AI, or Lose Search

Google's new K-12 controls sound like a choice: turn Gemini on, or don't. The fine print says otherwise — switch Search on and the AI comes with it, with no way to turn it off.

By Juno·Tuesday, November 10, 2026·Edition 55

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

On Tuesday, November 3, Google announced that Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will be "entirely off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly turn them on." That sounds like a choice. Read the fine print and it is a different kind of offer: a school can turn Search on, but it cannot turn the AI inside Search off. [1]

Google's education chief, Chris Phillips, said it himself: "Search and AI are deeply integrated." A school that does not enable AI, he said, leaves students without Search — they "will not have access to search, and they'll see a message from their school that indicates it's not available." And to Chalkbeat, the same point in plainer language: "If a school is not comfortable with that and simply wants AI off, then they have the choice to not have search on at all." [1][2]

Google's same-day clarification on X — that schools can turn Gemini, Notebook, and Search on individually — does not change that. Per the reporting, those toggles switch services on and off; they do not create an AI-free Search. Switch Search on and AI Overviews and AI Mode come with it. There is no separate switch for them. [1]

The reaction on X on November 3 was swift and, for once, bipartisan in its framing. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani wrote: "Google is trying to use its monopoly power to force AI into our public schools." Former FTC chair Lina Khan wrote: "Google is using its search monopoly to coerce schools into adopting AI." [3]

Google says more specifics arrive "in the coming months" — the New York Times reports early 2027 — and the company has not clarified whether the controls apply outside the United States. Guided Learning in Classroom went global November 3; Guided Learning in Search is a pilot in select U.S. schools later in November. [1]

Strip away the branding and this is a bundling play, and it is an old one: take a product schools cannot do without — Search, on the Chromebooks in their classrooms — and make it the delivery vehicle for a product schools have not chosen. A school board that wants to keep AI out of its classrooms until it has decided for itself is told it can have that, at the price of losing the search engine its students use every day. That is not a parental control. That is a condition. And when the condition is set by the company that already dominates search, the critics have a word for it, and it is not "choice."
