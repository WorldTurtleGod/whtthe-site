He Walked Home for 28 Years. On Monday, He Arrived.

Karl Bushby left Chile in 1998 with a tent and a cheap camera. On Monday he knocked on his mother's door in Hull — 31,000 miles later.

By Juno·Tuesday, November 10, 2026·Edition 55

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

On Monday, November 2, Karl Bushby walked up to his mother's front door in Hull, England, and finished the walk he started as a 29-year-old. It had taken 28 years and a day. [1]

Bushby set out from Punta Arenas, Chile, on November 1, 1998, on what he called the Goliath Expedition: walk home to Hull, unbroken, under two rules — no motorised transport to advance the journey, and no going home until it was done. The Associated Press puts the distance at about 31,000 miles, roughly 50,000 kilometres, across four continents and about 30 countries. Other estimates run higher, toward 36,000 miles, and no record body had confirmed a record as of Monday — so take the AP figure as the reported distance, not a certified record. [1]

The route is the story. He walked the length of the Americas and on through Europe, through deserts and mountain ranges and the Darién Gap, over decades of setbacks. When Eurotunnel refused permission to walk the Channel Tunnel's service tunnel, he swam the English Channel instead, landing at Shakespeare Beach near Dover on October 5, 2026 — and then walked the last stretch north to Hull, pushing the same cart that had accompanied him for much of the journey, to a crowd, a "Welcome Home Son" banner, and his mother, Angela. [1][3]

He left as a soldier with a tent, a few hundred dollars, and a cheap plastic camera. He came back at 57, to a son and a granddaughter he had never met in person, and a mother who had waited 28 years for the knock on the door. Most journeys are measured in miles. This one is measured in a lifetime: the man who left is not quite the man who arrived, and the door he knocked on is the same one he left from. He walked around the world to get home. On Monday, he did. [1][2]
