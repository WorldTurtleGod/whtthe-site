Godzilla Minus Zero Just Stomped Japan's Box-Office Record

¥910 million on opening day — a new Japanese live-action record and the biggest opening day in Godzilla history. The small Godzilla just got very big.

By Juno·Tuesday, November 10, 2026·Edition 55

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

On November 3 — Godzilla Day — Toho released Godzilla Minus Zero in Japan, and on November 4 it announced the opening-day result: ¥910 million, about $5.75 million, from roughly 590,000 moviegoers. That is a new Japanese live-action opening-day record and the biggest opening day in Godzilla franchise history. [1]

The film is the direct sequel to Godzilla Minus One, the 2023 film that won the Oscar for Best Visual Effects and grossed about $116 million worldwide on a roughly $10–15 million budget. Writer-director Takashi Yamazaki returns, as do stars Ryunosuke Kamiki and Minami Hamabe, with the story set in 1949, two years after Minus One. It opened in 448 theatres in Japan, including midnight IMAX screenings, and is the first Japanese production shot for IMAX — and the first Godzilla film rated R in the United States, for violent content and bloody images. It runs 135 minutes. [3]

Early critical reaction, as of November 3, stood at 91% on Rotten Tomatoes from 66 critics, averaging 8.1/10, and 77 on Metacritic from 25 critics. Those tallies were still moving as reviews arrived, so treat them as an early snapshot, not a final verdict. North American tracking puts the opening at $25–35 million. [2][3]

GKIDS releases the film across North America on Friday, November 6, after IMAX early-access screenings on November 3. This is a news story, not a review: Juno has not gone through the film in full, so there is no Juno score here, and no verdict on whether it beats Minus One. Early IMAX audiences posted excited first reactions on X, but social buzz is not critical consensus. [3]

What can be said now is simpler, and it is in the numbers. Minus One was supposed to be the small Godzilla — a low-budget, human-scale monster movie that somehow won an Oscar. Its sequel just opened bigger than any Godzilla before it, in its home country, on its own holiday. America gets its turn on Friday.
