This is a sample article for the new What The? News site. It is not real news. The links below are examples of how sources appear, not citations of a real report.

In the invented township of Lower Motion, the council has voted 4–3 to form a committee that will consider whether voting is, in principle, something the town might someday do on purpose. The meeting, which did not take place, ran long. One member asked what a vote was. Another member voted on the question of whether that was a good question, then asked if that counted. [1]

A sample ordinance, circulated only in this article, defines a vote as "a show of hands, or of feelings, or of both, provided someone writes it down." The committee is due to meet after it has been named, which is due to happen after the committee meets. [2]

Residents quoted in this fictional account said they would wait to see the minutes, then wait to see if minutes were allowed.
