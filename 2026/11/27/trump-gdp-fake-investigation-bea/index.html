The Economy Grew Faster Than First Reported. Trump Wants His Own Statisticians Investigated for It.

On Thanksgiving morning the president called the government's pre-election growth estimate "FAKE" and demanded "an immediate investigation" after the Bureau of Economic Analysis revised third-quarter GDP up from 3.4% to 4.3%. The bureau's own release calendar, its revision history and the White House's own October press release cut against every part of the charge.

By Publius·Friday, November 27, 2026·Edition 73

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

The government told President Trump the economy was even stronger than it first thought. He responded by accusing the government of lying.

On Wednesday, November 25, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis said real GDP grew at a 4.3% annual rate in the third quarter, revised up 0.9 percentage point from its advance estimate of 3.4%. [1] Reuters called it the fastest pace in three years. [2]

On Thanksgiving morning, Trump went after the first number. "Why wasn't the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation," he wrote on Truth Social, calling it a "massive, and purposeful, error," according to Axios and Newsweek. [3][4] In a longer holiday post he wished a happy Thanksgiving "to even those who issued fake Economic Numbers, just prior to the Election, and corrected them yesterday, three weeks late," calling the revision "A RECORD SETTING ERROR, and one so grave that it should be immediately investigated." [5][6] CBS News noted he offered no evidence. [6] The same post said Somalis who don't work or who receive government assistance "should be thrown out." [5][6]

Claim one: "three weeks late." The BEA publishes three estimates of each quarter about a month apart. Newsweek reported that the October 29 advance release ended with "Next release: November 25, 2026," and that the date was already on the schedule in February. [4] Axios also reported the revision came out on the previously announced schedule. [3]

Claim two: "record setting." The revision was big, but not a record. Axios reported that first revisions have averaged about 0.5 point either way, and that BEA revised second-quarter 2020 growth up by 1.2 points during Trump's first term. [3] Newsweek cited a larger swing: the first estimate for late 2008 went from a 3.8% decline to 6.2%, a 2.4-point move. [4]

Claim three: "purposeful." BEA's technical notes say the investment revision was led by software, "based primarily on revisions" to a Bureau of Labor Statistics price index, while consumer spending on goods was revised up using revised Census and new Energy Department data. Health care spending was revised down on "newly available" Census survey data. [1] And as Newsweek pointed out, Trump's White House hailed the 3.4% figure on October 29 as "the strongest pace in a year." [4] A number already being sold as a boom is a strange choice for a sabotage plot.

The bureau answers to Trump's own Commerce secretary. The BEA sits inside the Commerce Department, run by Howard Lutnick, whom Axios called one of Trump's closest political allies. [3] Newsweek reported Trump later reposted Lutnick crediting record exports. BEA's release says exports fell in the quarter. [1][4]

This is the second time Trump has turned on his own data shops. In August 2025 he fired Bureau of Labor Statistics Commissioner Erika McEntarfer after a weak jobs report. [7] Trump did not say who should run the investigation. [4] BEA's third estimate is due December 23. [1]

Commentary. Madison wrote in Federalist No. 51: "If men were angels, no government would be necessary." That's exactly why the people who count the economy have to be insulated from the people who get graded by it. Revisions aren't scandals. They're the system working: more data comes in, the estimate gets better. A president who wants an investigation every time a number moves, up or down, is telling every career statistician that the safe move is to guess what the boss wants. That is how you end up with numbers nobody, investors, the Fed or voters, can trust. If Trump has evidence of fraud, show it. Otherwise, leave the counters alone.
