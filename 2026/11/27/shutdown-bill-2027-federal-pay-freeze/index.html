Your 2027 Raise Is Zero Unless the Shutdown Bill Says Otherwise. It Happened Once Before.

Trump's pay plan freezes base and locality pay for most civilian federal workers in 2027 while law enforcement gets 3.8% and troops get 5% to 7%. Nearly 40 unions want at least 3.8% for everyone written into whatever Congress passes by Dec. 11. In 2019, a shutdown-ending deal overrode a Trump freeze, retroactively.

By Publius·Friday, November 27, 2026·Edition 73

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

If you're a federal employee, the bill Congress has to pass by December 11 to avoid a shutdown is also the likeliest place for Congress to change your 2027 paycheck.

What the president decided. In an August letter to Congress, President Trump wrote: "I have determined that for 2027, base pay and locality pay for civilian Federal employees will not change from the 2026 rates." [1] He used the exception in Title 5 for "national emergency or serious economic conditions affecting the general welfare." [1] He said current law would otherwise raise locality pay by an average of 20.6%, costing $26 billion in the first year, plus 3.1% across the board for the General Schedule. [1] In the same letter he directed OPM to raise law enforcement pay 3.8%, and set military raises at 7% for E-1 to E-5, 6% for E-6 to O-3 and 5% for O-4 and up. [1]

OPM has since widened the law enforcement group. Some newly covered positions, including 0083 police, some 0080 security staff and 1811 criminal investigators, could see 6.7%: the 3.8% plus a catch-up for missing this year's raise, Federal News Network reported. [2] Everyone else gets zero.

The squeeze. Most civilian workers got a 1% raise this year, the unions note. [4] Now the average enrollee share of FEHB premiums rises 10.9% in 2027, OPM's open season materials show. [3] FEHB Open Season ends December 14. [3]

The ask. On November 19, nearly 40 public- and private-sector unions, with signers including AFGE, AFSCME and the AFL-CIO, wrote to the top four appropriators, Tom Cole, Rosa DeLauro, Susan Collins and Patty Murray. They asked for "at least a 3.8% pay adjustment" in "whatever spending measure you advance." [4][5] "Enrollee premiums have thus increased by more than 41% since the start of 2025, while most civilian pay has risen only about 3%," the letter says. [4] Rep. James Walkinshaw, D-Va., is pushing his FAIR Act's 4.1%, Government Executive reported. [5]

The precedent. It has happened before. After the 2018-2019 shutdown, the February 2019 deal gave civilian workers 1.9%, overriding a freeze Trump had ordered months earlier. The raise was paid retroactively to the first full pay period in January, Federal News Network reported. [6] The pay plan isn't final until Trump signs an executive order, usually in December, and Congress can override it. [2][6]

One more change to watch. FEDweek compared OPM's January 2026 shutdown furlough guidance with the September 2025 version. Besides dropping the back-pay guarantee, the newer guidance replaced a section saying agencies could run RIFs during a shutdown with one saying they can generally begin or continue disciplinary actions for poor performance or misconduct. [7] Translation: a furlough doesn't pause a pending adverse action against you.

What to do now. Check your FEHB plan before December 14. Watch the text of whatever passes, a CR or full-year bills, for a pay section. If it's not there, the freeze stands. [1][4]

Commentary. Hamilton wrote in Federalist No. 30: "Money is, with propriety, considered as the vital principle of the body politic." [8] The founders gave that power to Congress, not the president. The law already sets a raise formula. This president skipped it again by citing "serious economic conditions," and the economy just grew at a 4.3% rate last quarter. [1][9] Maybe the federal workforce is too big; Congress can cut it in the open. But freezing civilian pay while handing raises to the agencies the White House likes best is the executive picking winners with the purse. Asking people to work through a shutdown without a paycheck, then to eat a premium hike with no raise, is no way to run anything.
