100 Parades Since 1924. Not One Postponed. Until Now.

Macy's pushed its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday at 8:30 a.m. ET as a nor'easter brought forecast gusts of up to 65 mph to New York. It is the first postponement since the parade began in 1924. The balloons went back to New Jersey for a weeks-long prep job crammed into about a day.

The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade has survived the Depression, a world war and a pandemic. It did not survive this week's forecast, at least not on Thursday.

Macy's moved its 100th parade from Thanksgiving Day to Saturday, November 28, stepping off at 8:30 a.m. ET, after the National Weather Service issued a high-wind warning for New York. [1][2][3] Mayor Zohran Mamdani said the city expected sustained winds of 30 to 40 mph and gusts as high as 65 mph. [1] "The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority, and after close consultation with our City and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year's celebration," Macy's said. [1]

A real first. Deadline and ABC7 both reported it is the first postponement since the parade began in 1924. [2][3] It was canceled outright from 1942 to 1944, when materials for the giant balloons were needed for the war effort. [1][2] In 1971, high winds grounded the balloons, but the parade still marched. [2][3] In 2020, Covid shrank it to a TV-only event around Herald Square. [2]

The rules disagree a little. NBC News reported city rules bar the balloons when sustained winds exceed 23 mph. [1] Deadline put the limits at sustained winds of 25 mph or gusts over 35 mph. [2] Either way, Thursday's forecast blew well past them.

Twenty-four hours to do weeks of work. Executive producer Will Coss said the balloons already in Manhattan had to come down and go back to a facility in New Jersey. "They have to go back and be parade readied again, so they have to be prepared, which is a weekslong process that's going to get condensed to about 24 hours," he said, according to NBC News. [1] There will be no second public inflation event; the Friday reinflation is closed to the public. [2][3] Coss called the lineup "an evolving conversation," since some marching bands, including the University of Alabama's, had plans to leave town for football games. [2]

NBC, which airs the parade, re-ran last year's broadcast on Thursday morning and will carry Saturday's live from 8:30 a.m. [1] Deadline reported the network will run the same ads both days, and noted last year's parade drew 34.3 million viewers across NBC and Peacock. [2] (NBC News, one source for this story, is owned by the same company as the broadcaster, NBCUniversal.)

NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch said there was "no specific, credible threats to the parade at this time" and that the department will call in over 1,000 officers to work Saturday. [1][3] Mamdani, recovering from an Achilles tear, said he will miss it because of upcoming surgery. [2][3] Asked about the balloons, he joked: "It's the only kind of inflation we support in New York City." [2]

Not everyone could wait two days. ABC7 talked to a Kansas family who had held off visiting New York until their kids were old enough for the parade, and who were flying home Friday. "I'm very disappointed," Melissa Welsh said. [3]

Commentary. A private company, a city and a TV network looked at a 65-mph forecast and 100 giant balloons over packed sidewalks, and made the call a day early. Nobody needed a federal agency to tell them. Tradition matters, but so does not dropping Snoopy on a crowd. Saturday's forecast calls for sun. [2] Go see it.
