Tidal Is Deleting the Robots: 29 Million AI Songs Headed for Removal

Tidal says more than 43% of tracks uploaded in the last 90 days were fully AI-generated. Starting December 9, it will ban and remove them — a private platform cleaning its own house, no government ban required.

By Juno·Wednesday, November 25, 2026·Edition 71

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Music streaming service Tidal said Tuesday, November 24, that it will start banning and removing songs generated fully with AI [1]. The company said that over the past 90 days, more than 43% of tracks uploaded to its platform were generated by AI [1].

Tidal said it will start removing fully AI-generated music on December 9, and that the process will result in more than 29 million songs being deleted from the platform by the end of the year [1]. With the new rules in place, the platform said it will be rejecting roughly 80,000 to 85,000 tracks per day [1]. Tidal is also banning distribution services from delivering 100% AI-generated tracks and will reject submissions whose metadata says they are made fully using AI [1]. Songs made with AI assistance remain allowed; only fully generated tracks are banned [1].

The company separately put fully AI-generated tracks at 27 million, or 6.3% of tracks on the platform, with 96% of those having no streams [1]. This paper prints both figures as Tidal stated them — the expected deletion total and the catalog share — without merging them into one number.

Tidal started de-monetizing songs made using AI in June, but that did not prove enough of a deterrent, Tidal Music's lead Robert Anderson said on X [1]. "A few months ago, @Tidal tagged and demonetized all wholly AI-generated tracks to protect the authenticity of real artists. But that's enough, as 43% of tracks distributed to us in the last 90 days have been detected as AI-generated. Something more drastic needs to be done…" the post said [1].

"It's true that not all songs catch on immediately, and artists should have time to build their fanbases. But this situation is different – at this scale, the authentic music and artists that Tidal subscribers are looking to listen to and discover get buried," the company said [1].

The context matters. Deezer plans a toggle to let users filter out AI music, while larger platforms like Spotify and YouTube Music have chosen to label AI-generated music instead of taking stronger measures [1]. Tidal chose the harder line on its own platform, for its own subscribers, without waiting for a regulator to write the rule. Whether the detection holds up — and whether real artists get wrongly swept out with the robots — is the question December 9 will start answering.
