Police Saw Benjamin Franklin on Hanover Street. They Recognized a Man With Two Warrants.

A 66-year-old Boston tour guide who works as a Franklin impersonator was arrested in full colonial costume in the North End — and showed up to his arraignment still wearing it.

By Publius·Wednesday, November 25, 2026·Edition 71

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Benjamin Franklin once wrote that "an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure." Boston police were running a crime prevention sweep Monday when they found him.

Officers on a warrant sweep downtown noticed "a man dressed head to toe as Benjamin Franklin" on Hanover Street in the North End at about 2:15 p.m., the Boston Police Department said. "On closer inspection, officers recognized him not as the Founding Father but as Timothy Kingman, 66, of Beacon Hill." [1] He had two active warrants out of Boston Municipal Court: a default warrant for shoplifting and a warrant for assault and battery. [1] Police arrested him at Cambridge and Staniford streets and booked him at the Nashua Street Jail. [1]

An onlooker filmed the arrest; the video shows two plainclothes officers handcuffing the costumed man, NBC Boston reported. [2]

Kingman was arraigned Tuesday morning still in his colonial clothes, according to NBC Boston and CBS Boston. [2][3] Prosecutors asked for $500 cash bail on the assault charge. His lawyer, Nicholas Santiago, asked that he be released on personal recognizance, and the judge agreed. [2] A not guilty plea was entered on his behalf, and the judge ordered him to stay away from his accuser, CBS Boston reported. [3]

The costume was not a stunt. Santiago told the court Kingman has run his own Boston tour business as a Franklin impersonator for six years, and before that worked as an electrician and served eight years in the Coast Guard. [2] "He is truly dedicated to service and working," Santiago said. [2] He also offered a review of the costume: "It takes guts. He's committed. He wears the outfit." [3]

The assault charge has a tour-guide twist of its own. According to the police report described by NBC Boston, the March 2 incident involved a dispute with another man leading students along the Freedom Trail. That guide told police Kingman appeared intoxicated, interrupted him, shoved him several times and swung at him, then followed and verbally harassed the group until a Faneuil Hall security guard stepped in. [2] Santiago said his client had his phone knocked from his hand in the incident and suffered shoulder and thumb injuries that needed treatment, while the alleged victim was not hurt. He said the altercation stemmed from Kingman's issues with alcohol and that Kingman has been sober since — "for the last eight months," he told the court. [2][3]

The charges are accusations, not convictions. Kingman is due back in court January 13, 2027, for a pretrial hearing. [2]

Franklin, for the record, also wrote that "time is money." Warrants, it turns out, keep time too.
