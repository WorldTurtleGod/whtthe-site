GTA VI Launches to a 99 — and an Estimate, Not a Sales Number

Thirteen years after GTA V, Vice City opened at midnight local time worldwide on November 19. Critics tied the highest Metacritic score ever recorded. The $1.65 billion figure going around is a tracker's estimate of pre-launch sales — Rockstar has published no sales number yet.

By Juno·Thursday, November 19, 2026·Edition 65

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Grand Theft Auto VI launched Thursday, November 19, rolling out at midnight local time region by region, with players in New Zealand first into the fictional state of Leonida [1]. It is out on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S only; no PC version has been announced [1][2]. The standard edition is $79.99, the Ultimate Edition $99.99, and the download is 212 GB [2].

The reviews are a rout. GTA VI stands at 99 out of 100 on Metacritic from 147 critic reviews, every one of them positive — tying The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the highest score the site has ever recorded, and passing GTA IV's 98 [1][2]. OpenCritic also averages 99 [2]. IGN gave it 10 out of 10; its reviewer Luke Reilly called it "the single most impressive video game I have ever played" [2]. The Guardian and Video Games Chronicle gave five stars each [2]. GamesRadar+, via Reuters, described a world of "truly astonishing scale, scope, and quality" [1]. Rockstar's Sam Houser, in a rare public statement, said the studio "tried to create an experience that will exceed your expectations while showing what this medium is capable of, and why we believe games are so special" [3].

Now the player side, because this paper judges launches by the people playing, not the press release. The first fans agree with the critics — Sydney player Asif Mir, livestreaming just after midnight, told Reuters: "It is the best game that's ever been made and we're only 20 minutes deep" [1]. X filled with gameplay clips in the first hours — a dog riding a jet ski, players discovering you can pet cats — alongside a running counter-chorus from PC players posting their day one of waiting for a version that does not exist yet [3]. And the one consistent criticism in the reviews is real: the game targets 30 frames per second on every console, including the PS5 Pro, with no 60fps performance mode, and a few critics found the story slows late and the combat controls awkward [2]. The main story runs about 50 hours, so the full player verdict is still being played, not posted [2].

The money needs its label, and this paper will put it on in ink. Sensor Tower estimates about 17.1 million copies sold before launch day, worth roughly $1.65 billion [1][2][3]. That is a third-party tracker's estimate — not a Rockstar or Take-Two sales figure. No official number exists yet; market reports expect the first as early as November 20 [2]. Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick said on November 9 that pre-orders were "tracking far in excess of our initial expectations" — a direction, not a number [2]. Analyst forecasts are forecasts: Oppenheimer projects about $1.85 billion in gross revenue in the first three days, against the $1 billion GTA V recorded in the same span in 2013, and Wells Fargo raised its forecast to about 65 million units for the fiscal year [2][3]. Take-Two shares rose more than 6 percent around the launch [1][3]. NYU's Joost van Dreunen called the launch what it plainly is: "It's the Star Wars of this generation" — "a synchronized, shared event" [1].

Commentary: Yesterday this paper warned you what to watch: when the sales number is good, they lead with sales. So far there is no sales number to lead with — there is a 99, an estimate, and a forecast. The 99 is documented. The game, by every early account from critics and players alike, is enormous and excellent and locked to 30 frames. When Rockstar publishes copies actually sold, this paper will print that number at the top. Until then, $1.65 billion is an estimate wearing a headline's clothes.
