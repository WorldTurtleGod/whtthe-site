America Bombed an Iranian Island Tuesday. Congress Holds the Purse and Can't Decide Whether to Use It.

U.S. jets hit a missile site on Qeshm Island, Iran answered with drones at Kuwait and Bahrain, and Senate Democrats are split over whether ending the war is worth a December shutdown fight.

By Publius·Thursday, November 19, 2026·Edition 65

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

The war with Iran that the administration says has cooled off got hot again this week.

On Tuesday night, U.S. fighter jets struck a missile launch site on Iran's Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, "with precision munitions," according to U.S. military officials quoted by the BBC. The officials called it a response to Iranian "aggression targeting US personnel and assets." [1] Al Jazeera and the AP reported that the U.S. said the strike answered an Iranian attack earlier Tuesday on a U.S. radar installation in Bahrain. [2] Iranian media reported explosions on the island but quoted a local official saying no infrastructure was damaged. [1]

Iran hit back within hours. Its army said drones targeted U.S. Patriot air defense systems and drone and missile launchers at Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait, which hosts U.S. forces. [1][3] Kuwait said its air defenses engaged the drones and reported no damage or casualties. It was the first attack on Kuwait in more than two months. [1] Bahrain, home of the U.S. Navy's 5th Fleet, said it intercepted "several Iranian aerial attacks." It has reported four attacks in six days. [1][2] The same day, a tanker owned by the UAE's state oil company was hit near the Strait. One sailor was missing and 11 crew members were getting medical care. [1]

Iran's army commander, Maj. Gen. Amir Hatami, warned of a "stronger, more crushing" response "without geographic limitations" if the U.S. strikes again. [1] A third U.S. aircraft carrier has arrived in the region, according to Iran's Fars news agency as reported by Al Jazeera. [2]

This is the same war the White House has argued is legally over. The Globe and Mail, reporting on the House war powers fight earlier this year, noted the administration's position that because a ceasefire had been declared, "the hostilities have ceased" for purposes of the War Powers Resolution's 60-day clock. [4] Tuesday's bombing run is hard to square with that.

Congress has two tools. The first is a war powers resolution. The Senate agreed to pull H.Con.Res. 93, which directs the President to remove U.S. forces from hostilities against Iran, out of committee no later than Friday, Nov. 20, with up to six hours of debate, according to the Senate's own floor log. [5] Sen. Tim Kaine, D-Va., told USA Today that Congress has already voted to end the war and "the president's ignored it." [6]

The second tool is money. Government funding runs out Dec. 11. Four Senate Democrats wrote last week that "any short-term package must include language that restricts the Trump Administration from using appropriated funds to continue his war against Iran." [6] Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who is challenging Chuck Schumer for majority leader, said current leadership is "not willing to use the most important power we have to end the war, which is to defund it." [6] Sens. Peter Welch, D-Vt., and Catherine Cortez Masto, D-Nev., told USA Today they oppose forcing a shutdown over it. Schumer has said the war must end and a shutdown must be avoided, without saying which wins if he can't have both. [6] On the other side, Sen. John Kennedy, R-La., said cutting off war money is "not gonna happen. Not in your or mine or our natural lifetimes." [6]

Commentary. In Federalist No. 58, Madison called the power over the purse "the most complete and effectual weapon with which any constitution can arm the immediate representatives of the people, for obtaining a redress of every grievance." [7] He didn't write that it was a weapon to be used only when it is painless. A resolution the President can ignore is a press release. An appropriation that says "no funds for this war" is law. Senators who say they want the war over but won't touch the money are telling you how much they want it over.
