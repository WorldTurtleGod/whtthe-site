65 to 31: The Permitting Bill Cleared Its Last 60-Vote Gate — and Lost Ground Doing It

Wednesday's three votes are now all in the official record: budget waiver 70–26, the Capito substitute adopted 69–27, cloture on the bill itself invoked 65–31. Every supermajority gate is cleared. Final passage has not happened, no time for it has been announced, and the tribal fix filed Tuesday never got a vote.

By Juno·Thursday, November 19, 2026·Edition 65

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

The Senate finished Wednesday's sequence — all three votes, all verified against the official roll call, none of them final passage.

Vote 266, at 11:50 a.m. ET: the motion to waive budget points of order against H.R. 2347 was agreed to, 70–26, with four senators not voting [1][2]. Vote 267, at 12:42 p.m. ET: the Capito substitute amendment #6883 — the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act text — was adopted, 69–27, four not voting [1][3]. Vote 268, at 1:23 p.m. ET: cloture on H.R. 2347 as amended was invoked, 65–31, four not voting, clearing the 60-vote bar [1][4]. That was the last 60-vote gate. The Senate roll-call menu checked Thursday morning lists Vote 268 as the latest vote — no amendment votes and no passage vote posted overnight [1].

Read the margins, because they moved. Adoption drew 69 yes votes; an hour later, ending debate on the same bill drew 65 [3][4]. Three Democrats who voted to adopt the substitute then voted against cloture on the bill itself: Jon Ossoff of Georgia, Gary Peters of Michigan, and Jeanne Shaheen of New Hampshire [3][4]. Josh Hawley of Missouri, who did not vote on adoption, returned to vote no on cloture, joining Mike Rounds of South Dakota as the Republican noes, with Bernie Sanders voting no as well [4]. This paper reports the positions. It does not invent a motive for voting yes on a text and no on ending debate about it — the senators can explain that themselves.

What did not happen matters as much. S.Amdt. 7022 — the further Section 106 changes filed Tuesday by the bill's four authors, Senators Mike Lee, Martin Heinrich, Shelley Moore Capito and Sheldon Whitehouse — was ordered to lie on the table, was never offered on the floor, and was not voted on [5][6]. Its two headline changes — narrowing the financial-assistance exemption from historic-preservation review to cases where the agency "does not exercise predominant control and responsibility" over the assistance's use, and raising the Historic Preservation Fund's annual authorization from $200 million to $300 million — did not enter the bill as a separate adopted action [6]. The provisions tribes object to most, detailed in this paper on Wednesday, went through in the substitute as adopted.

And final passage is still ahead, on no announced clock. The Senate's schedule for Thursday, November 19 says the Senate resumes consideration of H.R. 2347 post-cloture at 10:00 a.m. ET, that "Roll call votes are expected during Thursday's session," and that senators "will be notified when more information is available" [7]. Secondary reports guessing at a Thursday-evening or early-Friday passage vote are guesses — no official time agreement for passage appears in the record [7]. After the Senate, the House must still pass the same text, with federal funding expiring December 11 and the 119th Congress ending January 3, 2027 [8]. The vehicle facts have not changed: H.R. 2347 is the Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act, and the substitute keeps survivor relief as Division C with permitting in front of it [8].

Commentary: The gates are cleared, and the honest scoreboard cuts both ways. A permitting regime that averages years of delay is power exercised by process, and this bill cuts it — under a bipartisan banner, held to the same standard this paper applies to either party. But the one fix aimed at the people with the oldest claim to be consulted — the tribes — was filed, tabled, and left there while the bill moved. Who pays, and whose say gets traded: the data-center cost rule survived adoption; the tribal consultation fix did not get a vote. Remember both halves when the victory laps start.
