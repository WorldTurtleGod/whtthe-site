The Man Who Built PlayStation Says Game Machines Cost "the Price of a Car." He Thinks Consoles Could Die.

Ken Kutaragi, the engineer who fathered the PlayStation, told The Game Business that hardware prices are "not healthy" and that "the age may be coming where we don't need any consoles." He now runs an AI company, which is worth knowing when you weigh the prediction.

By Publius·Thursday, November 26, 2026·Edition 72

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Ken Kutaragi, the Sony engineer who built the original PlayStation, says the box under your TV may be on its way out.

The Game Business published its full on-stage interview with Kutaragi on Tuesday, November 24, recorded in October at the Game Republic New Horizons conference in the UK. [1] GamesRadar picked up the console remarks on Wednesday, November 25. [3] Asked where consoles go from here, Kutaragi went straight to price.

"Currently the machines are super expensive," he said. "It's not a healthy situation. You spend huge money on [them, because] graphics boards are super expensive right now." [1]

He tied part of the problem to a choice his own old company made. Starting with the PlayStation 4, Sony moved its console design toward PC-style architecture so developers could build for console and PC at the same time, The Game Business reported. [1] "It worked, but right now PC is super expensive– it's the price of a car, it's not healthy," Kutaragi said. [1]

Then the prediction. "The age may be coming where we don't need any consoles," he said. "It's a [real] danger." [1] In his view, AI that runs on servers rather than on the device in your hands will shrink what players need at home to "some lightweight platform." "But it will not be a console, it will not be a smartphone," he said, then added, laughing: "This is not the official comment from Sony Interactive Entertainment!" [1][2]

Kutaragi gave the interview in English, and The Game Business said it was hard to follow, so it posted the video with subtitles instead of an audio-only version. [1] The quotes above are the outlet's cleaned-up versions. The raw video is rougher but says the same thing: "we don't need any console." [2] A short version of the console comments first ran in The Game Business newsletter in October, and Insider Gaming wrote it up then. [4][5] The full interview is what is new this week.

The interview held more than the console forecast. Kutaragi told The Game Business he once met "the head of Microsoft one-to-one," who offered Sony free hard drives and license rights. "He was so arrogant," Kutaragi said, laughing. He said he answered with one word, "No," hoping Microsoft would then build its own games machine, which it did with the Xbox. "That was very intentional," he said. [1] That account is Kutaragi's alone. The outlet did not name the executive, and Microsoft's side of the story is not in the piece.

He also brushed off the old story that PlayStation was revenge on Nintendo, which dropped its CD deal with Sony. "Many of the media said, 'Sony suffered a betrayal, they will take some revenge.' No. Totally no. We are all friends," he said. "I love Nintendo." [1]

Kutaragi is no longer at Sony. He is now president and CEO of Cyber AI Entertainment, according to The Game Business and GamesRadar. [1][3]

Commentary. Know who is talking. A man who runs an AI company is telling you AI will make the hardware you own obsolete. He may be right. He also has a stake in it. A console is a thing you buy once and keep. A server is a thing somebody else owns and rents back to you, on their terms, for as long as they keep it switched on. If Kutaragi's "lightweight platform" future comes, the price of a car will drop off your living room floor and turn into a monthly bill, and the game you paid for will live on a machine you will never touch. Expensive hardware is a real problem. Owning nothing is a worse fix. Players should keep buying physical games and machines while they still can, and make companies earn the switch rather than force it.
