The Band Served 5,000 Turkey Dinners and Said Goodbye. Fifty Years Later, Thanksgiving Eve Still Belongs to The Last Waltz.

On Thanksgiving Day, November 25, 1976, the Band played its farewell at San Francisco's Winterland with Dylan, Joni Mitchell, Neil Young and Muddy Waters. For the 50th anniversary, musicians in Toronto and Denver lined up tribute shows, and fans kept a yearly ritual of watching the film.

Fifty years ago yesterday, a rock band ended its touring career by feeding its audience first.

The Last Waltz, the Band's farewell concert, took place on Thanksgiving Day, November 25, 1976, at Bill Graham's Winterland Ballroom in San Francisco. [1][3] The group picked Winterland because it was where it had made its debut as the Band in 1969, the Bay Area Brief reported. [1] Graham staged the night with more than 300 workers. Starting at 5 p.m., 5,000 people were served turkey dinners, with ballroom dancing to the Berkeley Promenade Orchestra and readings by poets including Lawrence Ferlinghetti, Diane di Prima and Michael McClure. [1]

Then came the guests: Bob Dylan, Joni Mitchell, Neil Young, Van Morrison, Muddy Waters, Eric Clapton, Dr. John, Neil Diamond, Paul Butterfield, Ronnie Hawkins and more. [1][2] Martin Scorsese filmed it, added soundstage performances shot later on the M-G-M lot, and released The Last Waltz in 1978. [1][3] The Library of Congress added the film to the National Film Registry in 2019. [1] Winterland itself is gone. It closed on New Year's Eve 1978 and was razed in 1985. [1]

The anniversary. Toronto's Massey Hall scheduled "LW50: A Celebration of Bob Dylan and the Band," with a lineup that included the Weather Station, Basia Bulat, Born Ruffians, Joel Plaskett, Whitehorse, Jeremie Albino and Julian Taylor, for Wednesday, November 25, Exclaim! reported. [2] "It is literally a dream come true to be asked to honour their legacy in this iconic room, around the corner from where they met Bob Dylan," musical director Aaron Goldstein told the magazine. [2] "I rewatched The Last Waltz not long ago, and it still gives me goosebumps," Albino said. [2]

In Denver, the 22nd annual Last Waltz – Revisited booked more than 60 Colorado musicians and a horn section into the Fillmore Auditorium the same night, paired with a canned-food and hygiene drive for the Denver Rescue Mission, according to the organizers. [4] In San Francisco, SFFILM marked the anniversary earlier this fall with a 4K screening moderated by former Rolling Stone music editor Ben Fong-Torres, who covered the original concert, the Bay Area Brief reported. [1]

The tradition. Writing in The New Yorker on Wednesday, Hanif Abdurraqib said he has watched the film every Thanksgiving Eve for almost half of those 50 years, a habit that began when he was 19 in a mostly empty college dorm. [3] He calls a soundstage take of "The Weight" with the Staple Singers one of the greatest live performances on film, and he names the part fans tend to forget. Robbie Robertson was done with the road and the others were not. The Band re-formed without him in 1983. Levon Helm stayed home from the group's 1994 Rock & Roll Hall of Fame induction. Richard Manuel was dead less than eight years after the show. [3] "The film itself was an antidote to loneliness, a vision of a life lived well," Abdurraqib wrote. [3]

The night is often remembered as one big party. It was also a breakup, played in public with the best guest list in rock.

Commentary. No ministry of culture made this. A promoter rented an old ice rink, five musicians called their friends, a director brought cameras, and 5,000 strangers ate turkey together. Fifty years later, people in two countries are still putting it back on stage and collecting canned food for their neighbors while they do it. That is what free people do with a holiday when nobody plans it for them.
