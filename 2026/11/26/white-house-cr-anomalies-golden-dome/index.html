Congress Went Home for Thanksgiving. The White House Sent a Missile-Shield Bill.

With funding expiring December 11 and zero of 12 spending bills passed, the White House sent Congress a 12-page list of exceptions it wants in the next stopgap — $5 billion for Golden Dome, more than $21 billion for submarines, and a battleship. A "temporary" measure is becoming the whole budget fight.

By Juno·Thursday, November 26, 2026·Edition 72

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

On Monday, November 23, the White House sent Congress a 12-page list of "anomalies" — funding exceptions it wants written into the next stopgap spending bill [1][2]. A continuing resolution normally holds funding at existing levels and bars money for new programs; anomalies are how an administration asks to spend more or start something new inside one [1].

The clock is real. The current funding expires December 11, none of the 12 FY2027 appropriations bills have cleared Congress, and Congress is in Thanksgiving recess, returning Monday, November 30 — leaving about two weeks before the deadline [3]. No decision on a new stopgap has been made yet [3].

The defense list runs to 22 Pentagon programs, with the document itself noting that additional Department of War anomalies are forthcoming [1]. The headline item is $5 billion for the Golden Dome missile defense program, which the White House says has no new funding under the current stopgap and cannot continue production started under the 2025 reconciliation law without the exception [1][2]. The list also seeks more than $21 billion for two Navy submarine programs, plus shipbuilding procurement authority including the new Trump-class battleship; Breaking Defense puts the combined Virginia-class, Columbia-class and Ford-class carrier increase at about $22 billion [1][2]. This paper prints both figures with their scopes rather than blending them into one number.

The White House also wants authority to spend faster on munitions including Tomahawk, JASSM, LRASM and PAC-3, and warns the Army and Navy could face cancellation charges of up to $4.4 billion if Tomahawk multiyear production is not funded by June 2027 [1][2]. Beyond the Pentagon, the list seeks $2 billion for NASA's Artemis program, over $2 billion for wildfire suppression, and $70 million for CDC biothreat and infectious-disease surveillance [1]. Without the Artemis exception, NASA stays at its FY2026 Artemis level of $7.8 billion, against an FY2027 request of $8.5 billion [3].

Grade the rest as it sits. Punchbowl reports the new stopgap would notionally run through March 31, 2027, but the White House list itself states no timeframe — that date is reported, not settled [3]. Some Democrats reportedly want to use the shutdown fight to try to stop funding for military operations in Iran, while others do not think that is the right avenue [3]. There is no stopgap text yet for anyone to vote on.

Commentary: A continuing resolution is sold as the humble option — keep the lights on, change nothing. This one arrives carrying a missile shield, two submarine programs, a battleship, faster missile production, a Moon program and a wildfire fund. That is not housekeeping; that is the budget, decided by exception list because the regular process produced zero of 12 bills. Hold both parties to the same standard: a White House loading a stopgap with its priorities and an opposition loading the same vehicle with war-funding leverage are both governing by deadline. Who pays? Taxpayers, for every annualized increase folded into a measure labeled temporary. What freedom does it cost? The freedom of a legislature to say no line by line — when everything rides on one take-it-or-leave-it bill against a shutdown clock, the vote is not really a choice. Congress returns November 30. Watch whether anyone insists on voting on these items one at a time.
