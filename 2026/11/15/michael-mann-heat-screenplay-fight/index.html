Michael Mann Wants His "Heat" Screenplay Back. New Regency Says He Made a Deal With Himself.

The director is using a copyright-termination right to reclaim the script for his 1995 crime classic. The studio sued Friday, calling it a work made for hire. Both sides say Heat 2 is safe.

By Herodotus·Sunday, November 15, 2026·Edition 61

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Heat is a movie about two professionals who respect each other and still end up across a runway with guns drawn. Thirty-one years later, its writer and its studio are doing the legal version.

New Regency, which produced Heat in 1995, filed a federal lawsuit on Friday, November 13, asking a court to declare that Michael Mann's attempts to reclaim the copyright to his original screenplay are void. [1][2] Earlier this year, Mann's lawyers sent three termination notices. [1][2]

The fight turns on a piece of U.S. copyright law. Authors can terminate a transfer of rights after 35 years, Variety explains, but a "work made for hire" belongs to the company that commissioned it and can't be clawed back. [1] New Regency says that's what this was. According to the suit, Warner Bros. acquired the screenplay in 1994 in a deal with Mann's loan-out company that was explicitly a work-for-hire agreement. [1]

Mann's side points to an earlier arrangement. Per the suit, his lawyers said Mann had assigned his rights to his loan-out companies in 1988, and when New Regency asked for a copy, they were told it was an oral agreement. [1] The complaint does not let that pass quietly. It calls the deal "an oral agreement that Mann allegedly made with himself, in which Mann, like a Shakespearean actor playing multiple parts, simultaneously spoke on his own individual behalf" and on behalf of his two companies. [1]

New Regency also says that if the notices stand, they would strip it, "beginning in 2028, of rights it has held for more than three decades." [2]

Mann's camp says it will fight. "After 40 years Michael Mann is exercising the right of authors to reclaim their work and in this case Michael Mann's original creation," a spokesperson said, adding that the writing began on "multiple screenplays during the 1980s." [1][2]

As for the sequel: Heat 2, now at Amazon MGM Studios, starts shooting January 19. Variety reports that Mann and New Regency both have deals with Amazon that account for the dispute, with Mann assigning any rights he reclaims to Amazon. "This is unrelated to production of Heat 2," his rep said. [1][2]

Commentary. The Constitution gives Congress the power to secure to authors "for limited Times" the rights to their writings. [3] The termination right is Congress's way of giving an author a second shot at a bargain struck when he had little leverage. Whether a man can sign a binding deal with himself is a question for the judge.
