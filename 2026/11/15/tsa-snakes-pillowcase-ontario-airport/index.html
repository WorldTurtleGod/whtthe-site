He Packed His Pets in a Pillowcase. One of Them Was a Rattlesnake.

TSA officers at Ontario International Airport thought they were handling a bundle of rope in a checked suitcase. Then it moved. The story was released Nov. 13 — the passenger said the snakes were his pets and he had flown with them before.

By Juno·Sunday, November 15, 2026·Edition 61

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

The X-ray said rope. The hand said otherwise.

At Ontario International Airport in California, TSA Officer Benjamin Andrade-Carillo was screening checked baggage when he picked up what he thought was a bundle of rope inside a suitcase. Then it moved. Inside a pillowcase in that suitcase were two live snakes — including a rattlesnake. TSA released the story and the press picked it up on Nov. 13; the screening itself happened earlier, so this is a November release, not a November incident. [1][2]

"When I realized the item in my hand was alive, I told myself, 'I need to escalate this immediately,'" Andrade-Carillo recalled. [1]

He called in Supervisory TSA Officer Suggey Flores, who has clearly had a different life than the rest of us. "I felt it tense up and start moving," Flores said. "I have held snakes before, so I wasn't too spooked. I was more impressed, because it's not something you see every day." Screening paused in the immediate area while officers worked out what they were holding. "Safety was a concern because we didn't know what kind of snakes they were," Flores said. "They could have been venomous." One of them, it turned out, was — a rattlesnake, confirmed by ONT TSA Manager Francisco "Frank" Calderon. [1]

Airport police and the passenger's airline were notified. Police brought the bag to the ticketing area, where the passenger removed the snakes himself and handed them to a family member who was not travelling. His explanation, per the Los Angeles TSA Coordination Center: the snakes were his pets, and he had previously travelled with them on another airline. TSA rescreened the bag, cleared it, and let him fly — without the reptiles. No injuries, no bites, and airport-wide screening was not disrupted. [1]

Andrade-Carillo, two years into the job, put it in perspective: "In my two years of service, I had not found anything like this. In San Diego, I experienced a gentleman trying to bring live lobsters as a carry-on." His advice to the travelling public is the quote of the week: "We ask the traveling public to be mindful of what they bring in their bags, because if we don't know what it is, we will find out." [1]

TSA's official guidance is to check with your airline before arriving to learn the rules for flying with pets, including reptiles. Nowhere in that guidance, one assumes, is there a pillowcase exception for rattlesnakes.
