A Sheep Outran an Ohio Town for 45 Days. Then a 13-Year-Old and Her Horse Showed Up.

Drones, decoy animals and a sheriff's office couldn't catch the runaway blackbelly sheep of Wadsworth, Ohio. A teen roper on an 18-year-old horse named Catalena did it in an afternoon.

By Herodotus·Sunday, November 15, 2026·Edition 61

Every frontier town needs a legend. Wadsworth, Ohio, got one in wool.

An American blackbelly sheep was first seen roaming Wadsworth in September, and for about 45 days nobody could catch her. [1][2] She turned up in yards and vanished again. One woman kept finding her in her yard and started feeding her. Searchers tried luring her with another sheep and a goat, tried cornering her, and flew a drone to spot her. Nothing worked, and she kept crossing state Route 94, which worried everyone. [2]

Janine Smalley, founder of Whispering Acres Farm Animal Sanctuary, and volunteer Rebecca Roxberry led the hunt, which spread on social media with a "Catching the Wadsworth Sheep" Facebook event. [1] When the usual methods failed, they put out a call for a roper. Shannon Crumpler saw it. "If anybody can catch the sheep, it's my daughter," Smalley remembers her saying. [2]

On Wednesday, November 11, 13-year-old Reagan Crumpler of Litchfield walked down to her family's pasture and asked her horse, "Catalena, you want to go catch a sheep today?" [2] The chase through a field along U.S. Route 224 took hours. Every time they got close, the sheep turned and Reagan missed her throw. Then she told the horse, "Come on, Cat. We got this. Keep going." [2]

"It felt like she knew what I was saying, and she immediately kicked in the high gear, and we got right in front of it," Reagan said. The rope landed. Neighbors came out of their houses hollering and filming. [2][3]

The sheep has been named Gerri Reagan, after her captor. She is staying in what Smalley calls the "princess palace" at the sanctuary on a stray hold, with a vet visit on the calendar. If no owner comes forward with proof, she stays for good. Nobody knows where she came from. [1][2] The rescue also brought the sanctuary six new volunteers. [2]

Would Reagan do it again? "Yes, in a heartbeat." [2]

Commentary. The founders liked to praise the self-reliant citizen. They would have enjoyed this one: when the town's machinery stalled, a neighbor's kid saddled up and solved it.
