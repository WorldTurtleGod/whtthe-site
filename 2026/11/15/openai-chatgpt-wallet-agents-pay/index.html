OpenAI Just Gave Its AI Agents a Wallet — Yours

ChatGPT Wallet, built with Amex, Mastercard and Visa, lets OpenAI's "dots" agents complete purchases for Pro subscribers in the US. The agent never sees your real card number, every purchase needs a Face ID or fingerprint check — and OpenAI takes no cut, for now.

The chatbot no longer just helps you shop. As of Friday, Nov. 13, it can pay.

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, built in partnership with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, initially available through its "dots" agents for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States. OpenAI's own description of what is new: "It's the first wallet that lets agents pay without access to users' actual card details and that requires a security check for every purchase, while also preserving existing card rewards and protections for eligible disputes." [1][2]

The mechanics are the careful part, and they are genuinely careful. Users add a card once. Before each purchase, the user reviews the items, the total and the selected card, then approves with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan. Only after approval does the payment network issue a payment token — a substitute card number, security code and expiry scoped to that purchase — so the agent and the merchant never see the real card details. Card rewards and dispute protections carry over. The rollout is narrow on purpose: Pro subscribers only, 18 and older, US only, eligible US-issued cards, and only inside dots conversations — not ordinary Chat, Work or Codex threads. [1][2]

Two numbers frame the ambition. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week, and CNBC reported nearly 100 million paying users at the end of the third quarter. And OpenAI takes no cut of transactions at launch — no take rate, no timeline for one given. Chaya Nayak, OpenAI's monetisation and payments lead, told CNBC: "Our goal here is to really focus on merchants and our network partners and start building out a really great foundation that's safe and easy to use." [1][2]

Read the fine print OpenAI itself published, because it admits the weak point: "Agentic commerce is still in its early days." Some merchant sites are already blocking, or inadvertently blocking, agent purchases, and OpenAI released a Commerce Partner Playbook the same day asking businesses to unblock signed agent requests and teach their risk systems to recognise legitimate agent payments. A wallet your favourite store's fraud filter declines is a demo, not a product — OpenAI knows it, and Friday's launch is as much a lobbying document to merchants and banks as it is a feature. [1]

And note the direction of travel in OpenAI's own words: over time, it expects users to be able to delegate purchases entirely to an agent by setting spending limits, categories or time periods and pre-approving them. Friday's version keeps a human fingerprint on every single purchase. That is the right design, and it is also the part that will be under the most pressure to relax — because the entire pitch of an agent is that you stop checking its work. The day the per-purchase check becomes optional is the day this stops being a wallet with an agent attached and becomes an agent with your wallet attached. For now, the fingerprint stays. Hold them to it.
