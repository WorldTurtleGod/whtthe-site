Isaac Finally Goes Online — and Switch 2 Gets a Real Cartridge

The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ launched November 19 on Switch 2, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S with four-player online co-op in every mode, a $9.99 upgrade for owners — and a physical Switch 2 edition that is the whole game on a cartridge, not a Game-Key Card. What it doesn't have yet: crossplay.

By Juno·Friday, November 20, 2026·Edition 66

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

While the industry held its breath for one giant launch this week, a 15-year-old indie institution quietly shipped its final form. Publisher Nicalis released The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ on Nintendo Switch 2, PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S on Thursday, November 19 [1][2].

The headline feature is the one console players have asked for since the basement was a Flash game: four-player online co-op, on consoles for the first time, in every game mode — invite friends, join strangers, or take a Quick Match with randomly selected players — alongside four-player local co-op [1]. Owners of Repentance on those consoles upgrade through a $9.99 DLC; the complete Switch 2 edition is $59.99 digital and $69.99 physical [1][2].

The physical edition deserves its own paragraph, because in 2026 it is news when a box contains a game. The Switch 2 physical release is the complete game on a traditional cartridge — explicitly not a Game-Key Card — with a reversible cover, an instruction manual, and a PVC Isaac and Guppy strap [1][2]. PlayStation 5 and Xbox versions are digital only [2].

The package itself is the accumulated weight of everything since Repentance in 2021: over 700 collectible items, 34 playable characters, over 330 enemy types, and more than 120 bosses and minibosses, plus a new in-game item description feature [1]. Creator Edmund McMillen, in an interview published November 18, called the descriptions long overdue — mods had filled the gap for years while the team focused on content — and framed this release as the end of the line: "This is the final version of the game," with the last-DLC framing he has teased before [3]. His staying-power number is his own claim, printed as his: "We sold nearly 5 million copies of the game this year. It's insane the amount of staying power that game has, and I'm sure I'll be the Binding of Isaac guy till I die at this point" [3].

The honest caveat, in print where it belongs: crossplay and cross-save are not in at launch. Nicalis lists both as post-launch updates — cross-save would let players import Steam saves to console, crossplay would open PC-console sessions — with no date given in the launch coverage [1][2]. Do not buy it tonight expecting to play with your PC friends tonight.

Commentary: Judge it by what players actually got. A $9.99 upgrade instead of a full-price re-buy, online co-op in every mode instead of a bolted-on extra room, and a physical edition that is actually the game on the cartridge — three decisions that treat the buyer like an owner. The missing crossplay is a real gap, and it is labeled as one, with the fix promised rather than hidden. After a week of launch coverage measured in estimates and forecasts, here is a launch you can measure in what is on the cartridge.
