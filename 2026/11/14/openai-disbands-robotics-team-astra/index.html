OpenAI Just Fired Its Robot Team — Because the Chatbot Learned to Drive the Robot

OpenAI disbanded its stand-alone robotics team after a general model beat the best specialized robot brains 74.8 percent to 51 percent — in simulation. The empty conference booth told the story first.

By Juno·Saturday, November 14, 2026·Edition 60

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Fortune found the story in an empty booth. OpenAI had registered booth #208 at CoRL, a robotics conference in Austin this week. There was no OpenAI logo on it and nobody home — because, as OpenAI confirmed to Fortune on Friday, Nov. 13, the company has disbanded its stand-alone robotics team. [1]

The researchers are not being laid off; they are being absorbed. The robotics research team moves into OpenAI's main research organization under Chief Research Officer Mark Chen, and the applied robotics and hardware teams move into the Applied organization under President Greg Brockman. OpenAI says robotics remains a core part of its long-term vision and that folding the team in is how it accelerates toward general-purpose robots. [1][2]

The number behind the decision: in an Nvidia-run study, an agent built on GPT-6 Astra — OpenAI's general-purpose frontier model, fed camera images, robot information, and a control interface — scored 74.8 percent on a simulated manipulation benchmark, against 51.0 percent for the best specialized robot models. Read that again. The generalist did not just compete with robots built for one job. It lapped them. Carnegie Mellon's Guanya Shi told Fortune he reads the move as a signal OpenAI now sees robotics as "no longer a special problem." [1]

Now the caveats, because they matter. That 74.8 percent happened in simulation — a digital robot in a digital room, not a machine on a factory floor. Shi himself notes that big models are slow, live in the cloud, and need a constant connection, while a real robot often needs a small brain that runs on board. MIT's Russ Tedrake makes the other half of the point: companies with robots actually deployed are gathering the one thing a benchmark cannot give — real-world work experience, site by site. [1]

This is also the second time OpenAI has wound this team down — the first was in 2020, before it rebuilt the unit in 2024 — and it lands while the company is reportedly carrying $10.9 billion in quarterly operating losses and preparing a possible IPO. Sam Altman has called efforts outside the core "side quests" before. The humanoid robot OpenAI has promised — first public demonstration targeted for 2027 — now has to survive as a feature of the main model, not a department of its own. [1]

The bet OpenAI is making is the bet behind everything it does: one big general mind, applied to everything, beats a hundred narrow specialists. On a simulated bench this week, the bet paid. Warehouses will grade the real exam.
