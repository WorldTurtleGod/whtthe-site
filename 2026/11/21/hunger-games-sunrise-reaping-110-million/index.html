Critics Shrugged. Fans Didn't. The New Hunger Games Is Headed for $100 Million-Plus.

Sunrise on the Reaping" made $21 million in previews and was tracking near $110 million for the weekend by Friday midday, more than double the last prequel's opening, despite middling reviews.

By Publius·Saturday, November 21, 2026·Edition 67

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Three years ago, the Hunger Games looked tired. "The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes" opened to $44.6 million, the series' worst start. [1] This weekend, the franchise came roaring back.

Lionsgate's "The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping" made $21 million in Thursday previews and Wednesday early screenings, both Variety and Deadline reported. [1][2] Deadline put the exact figure at $21.25 million, with $5 million from Wednesday early-access premium-format shows. [1] That's the second-best preview haul in franchise history, behind "Catching Fire's" $25.2 million in 2013. [1]

By Friday midday, Deadline projected a $110 million domestic opening at 4,108 theaters, after a Friday of about $51 million including previews. [1] That would rank fourth among the series' openings, and Deadline said it has a shot at third if the weekend isn't front-loaded. [1] Studios and trackers don't agree on the number, though. Variety reported that Lionsgate itself was "more cautiously predicting an $85 million debut," while some estimates ran as high as $125 million. [2] Saturday and Sunday will settle it.

The movie is adapted from Suzanne Collins' novel and follows a young Haymitch Abernathy, the mentor played by Woody Harrelson in the original films and now played by Joseph Zada, as a tribute in the 50th Games, which sends twice the usual number of children into the arena. [2] Ralph Fiennes, Elle Fanning, Jesse Plemons, Glenn Close and Kieran Culkin are in the cast. [1][2] Francis Lawrence directed, his fifth film in the series. [1] Variety reported a $150 million budget. [2]

Critics were lukewarm. Deadline cited a 68% Rotten Tomatoes critics score, against a 95% audience score that it said is the best in the franchise. [1] Opening-night audiences polled by PostTrak gave it five stars and a 79% "definite recommend," with women making up 67% of the crowd, according to Deadline. [1]

If it clears $100 million, Variety reported, it would join "Spider-Man: Brand New Day," "Toy Story 5," "The Super Mario Galaxy Movie" and "The Odyssey" as one of five 2026 films to do it. [2] Deadline noted that the jump from "Songbirds" to "Sunrise" would be about 147%, which it called "unheard of" for a sixth film in a series. [1]

The rest of the chart looked thin by comparison. Deadline projected Amazon MGM's Stallone biopic "I Play Rocky" second at about $10 million in its first wide weekend, and Paramount's Johnny Depp "Ebenezer" third at about $8 million, down 58% in its second weekend. [1]

Commentary. A story about a government that makes children fight to the death on television for the entertainment of the capital still sells out theaters 18 years after the first book. Collins never hid what she was writing about: what happens when power gathers in one city and the districts are left to pay for it. The founders had a word for that arrangement, and it wasn't "republic." Audiences keep buying tickets to that warning.
