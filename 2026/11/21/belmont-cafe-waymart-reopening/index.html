A Car Through the Window, a Town Through the Door: The Belmont Café Plans to Reopen Tuesday

On Halloween, a car rammed the front window of Dawn and Joe Lepkowski's Belmont Café in Waymart, Pennsylvania, fifteen minutes before the trick-or-treaters came out. Nobody was seriously hurt. The town was at the café before the owner was — and she split the donations with the staff the closure put out of work. Reopening is planned for 6 a.m. Tuesday, November 24.

The Belmont Café, at 249 Belmont Street in Waymart, Wayne County, Pennsylvania, was opened on Christmas Eve 2011 by Dawn and Joe Lepkowski — Dawn a Brooklyn native, Joe originally from Peckville — and it runs breakfast and lunch only [1]. On Saturday, October 31, about 4:45 p.m., after the café had closed for the day, Dawn's mother stopped by in Dawn's car to pick up a cheesecake and apparently stepped on the gas rather than the brake; the car rammed the front window left of the doorway and came to rest partly inside the dining room [1]. Waymart Police Assistant Chief Harry Shaffer confirmed a call at about 4:45 p.m. to the 200 block of Belmont Street; two apartments in the building were evacuated, the road was closed about two hours, and residents could not return until a structural engineer inspected the building [2].

Dawn, on arriving: "There was my car in the dining room window. My mom was in the ambulance by this time… She luckily only got a real cut on her hand. She was shaken, but she's fine, thank God" [1]. Joe put the timing plainly: "Two good things, we weren't open and they weren't trick or treating." "It could be a lot worse" [1]. The car crossed the sidewalk about fifteen minutes before trick-or-treaters were out in force for Waymart's Halloween party at the park a block away [1].

Then the town did what the town does. "By the time I got here the town was already coming down here for us. It's an amazing, amazing place to live" [1]. Organizations and individuals donated funds to cover what insurance did not; Waymart Church offered the use of its kitchen if the Lepkowskis wanted to run the café from there temporarily; their oldest son, Rob Solimini, set up a GoFundMe; someone even offered a car, since Dawn's was totaled [1]. Dawn divided the donations to help her staff thrown out of work by the closure — a cook and three waitresses [1]. Robert Grimm and Grimm Construction did the repair work, and by the November 18 interview only painting remained [1].

The reopening is planned, not done — this paper prints it as planned. The building inspector was expected the day after the interview and the state inspection soon after, and Dawn Lepkowski was announcing a reopening at 6 a.m. Tuesday, November 24, two days before Thanksgiving [1]. A check on November 21 found the November 24 date still being announced, with no report of a postponement — and no report yet that the inspections have passed [1][3]. Regular hours once reopened: 6 a.m. to 2 p.m. Monday through Friday, 6 a.m. to 3 p.m. Saturday and Sunday [1].

What Dawn wanted the place to be, in her own words: "I remember saying I wanted a restaurant that everyone is going to feel comfortable and be like family, not just customers." "We really have achieved it. So many friendships have come out of this restaurant" [1]. On what keeps them going — Joe: "We're a team." Dawn: "Probably the people. And I do love what I do but's mostly it's the people" [1]. Joe again: "It's kind of like a Hallmark moment." "You finally realized you got the answer you were looking for" [1]. And Dawn: "I really am a very blessed woman. The Lord has watched over us and brought us here" [1].

Commentary: No program absorbed this café and no organisation took it over. A church lent a kitchen, neighbors gave past what insurance covered, a son raised money for his mother's restaurant, and the owner shared what came in with the cook and three waitresses the crash put out of work. That is common-man culture in its working clothes — a family place carried by its own town — and it is why this desk keeps telling these stories.
