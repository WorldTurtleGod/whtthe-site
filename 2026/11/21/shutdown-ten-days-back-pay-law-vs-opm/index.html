Congress Left Town With 10 Working Days Before a Shutdown. The Law Says Furloughed Workers Get Back Pay, but Trump's OPM Says Congress Decides.

Lawmakers scattered for Thanksgiving Friday with no funding plan, while Senate Democrats pushed Iran war limits. A 2019 law says furloughed workers "shall be paid," but OPM's current guidance says that's up to Congress, and Congress has settled it bill by bill.

By Publius·Saturday, November 21, 2026·Edition 67

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Federal funding runs out after December 11. When the House and Senate left Washington on Friday for Thanksgiving, they had no agreed plan to keep the government open, and only 10 scheduled session days left before the deadline, two of them Fridays, according to MS NOW. [1]

The fight is over the Iran war. Senate Democrats, whose votes are needed to reach 60 in the Senate, are split on whether to demand that any funding bill cut off money for it. [1][2] Sen. Tim Kaine of Virginia wants a provision ending war funding in the stopgap bill. "If you want to keep waging war against Iran, there is a path to do it," Kaine told NPR. "Come and seek a congressional authorization, which they should've done from the first day." [2] Sen. Michael Bennet of Colorado proposed specific language Thursday to bar war funds, MS NOW reported, joined in calls by Sens. Chris Van Hollen, Tammy Duckworth, Kirsten Gillibrand and Bernie Sanders. [1]

Republicans say cutting funds mid-war endangers troops. Sen. Thom Tillis of North Carolina told NPR Congress must refill stockpiles like Patriot missiles and that the GOP may use party-line reconciliation if Democrats force a shutdown. [2] Senate Republicans shelved that plan Wednesday, MS NOW reported. Sen. John Kennedy of Louisiana said he sees "better than a 50-50 chance" of a shutdown. [1]

The law and the agency say different things. The 2019 Government Employee Fair Treatment Act, codified at 31 U.S.C. 1341(c), says each furloughed employee "shall be paid for the period of the lapse in appropriations," and excepted employees for their work, "at the earliest date possible after the lapse in appropriations ends." A 2019 amendment added "subject to the enactment of appropriations Acts ending the lapse," and that clause covers both groups in the same sentence. [3] The U.S. Code, current through Oct. 15, 2026, shows no later change. [3]

OPM's current guidance, dated Jan. 30, 2026, reads it more narrowly. It says excepted workers "will be paid after Congress passes and the President signs" a funding bill, but for furloughed workers it says only: "Congress will determine via legislation whether furloughed employees receive pay for furlough periods." [4] The September 2025 version had answered that same question "Yes," citing the statute, FedSmith and Government Executive reported. [5][6]

The change matches the White House line. During the 43-day shutdown last fall, OMB Director Russell Vought began insisting the 2019 law doesn't pay furloughed workers automatically and that Congress must authorize it, Government Executive reported. [6] OMB's guidance now says furloughed workers get retroactive pay "when specific appropriations for such payments are enacted," according to Federal News Network. [7]

Congress has overruled that reading twice. The law ending the fall shutdown on Nov. 12, 2025, says pay funds "shall be available for payments pursuant to subsection (c) of section 1341" and "such payments shall be made." [8] The Feb. 3, 2026, law ending a four-day partial lapse did the same; OPM's own memo says it covers employees "who were furloughed." [9] Neither changed the statute itself, so the dispute returns with every lapse. FedSmith advises workers to watch the language of any December deal. [5]

Before 2019, nothing was guaranteed. Furloughed workers had no legal right to back pay and got it only when Congress passed a law for it, the Congressional Research Service notes. [10]

Other pieces. FEHB health coverage continues in a shutdown, with the employee's premium share collected afterward, and Open Season ends December 14, FedSmith reported. [5] Separately, the president's pay plan freezes 2027 General Schedule rates, with a 3.8% bump for covered law enforcement. [5] A Lankford-Hassan bill, S. 4632, would roll funding forward in 14-day blocks, but it isn't law. [5]

Commentary. The Framers gave both war and money to Congress so no president could fight without the people's representatives paying for it in the open. Kaine is right that this war never got that vote. But Congress already passed a law saying furloughed workers "shall be paid," and the President signed it. An administration that rewrites that promise in a guidance document is legislating without a vote. Congress should vote on the war and fund the government as two separate votes, and write back pay in again.
