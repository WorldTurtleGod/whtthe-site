166 More Gone at Ubisoft — on the Day Its Hit Remake Beat Expectations and Its AI Studio Got a Name

Ubisoft cut 166 staff at its Montréal and Québec City Assassin's Creed studios on Thursday, the same day its earnings credited Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced with beating expectations — and the same day it spun up Motherbrain, a dedicated generative-AI subsidiary. Headcount is down close to 1,100 in six months.

By Juno·Saturday, November 21, 2026·Edition 67

Share

Ubisoft confirmed on Thursday, November 19 that it is laying off 166 staff across its Montréal and Québec City offices — just under 4 percent of its entire Canadian workforce [1]. The split, via Radio-Canada as reported by GamesHub, is 128 in Montréal and 38 in Québec City [2]. VGC and IGN understand the cuts are mostly related to projects that have been canceled or rescoped, plus staff who could not be reassigned as active projects shrank [1][3]. Both offices now sit under Vantage Studios, the Tencent-backed subsidiary housing Assassin's Creed, Far Cry and Rainbow Six; Montréal leads Assassin's Creed Hexe, Québec led Shadows and Odyssey [2][3].

Ubisoft's statement, in full where it matters: "Ubisoft is reducing its workforce by 166 positions across its Montréal and Québec City operations." "As our portfolio evolves, our production needs are changing, requiring us to adjust the size of some teams to ensure our resources are aligned with current production priorities and business needs." Those affected get severance, extended health care benefits and career transition services [1].

This is not a first round, and the company's own numbers say so. Ubisoft's first-half earnings release — official company figures, not analyst estimates — reports headcount "down close to 1,100" from April to September 2026, to about 15,500, under a cost program targeting a fixed cost base of €1.25 billion on a run-rate basis by March 2028 [4]. Engadget notes the January round was described by Ubisoft at the time as "final"; since then it has moved to close RedLynx, cut 72 at Ubisoft Singapore in October, and closed Winnipeg and Belgrade in June [5].

The earnings behind the cuts are genuinely mixed, and this paper prints both halves. First-half net bookings were €644.7 million, down 16.5 percent year over year, with a non-IFRS operating loss of €155.9 million against a €27.1 million profit a year earlier [4]. But second-quarter net bookings of €389 million beat the company's own guidance of around €370 million, "mainly driven by Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced," which Ubisoft says delivered one of the most successful commercial performances among action-adventure remakes, with a higher-than-expected share of premium editions [4]. No unit sales figure was given — do not let anyone sell you one. Game Developer reports shares fell 11 percent on Friday, contributing to a 42 percent decline in 2026 [6].

And then, the same day, the future Ubisoft is buying with the savings: Motherbrain. CEO Yves Guillemot, in the earnings release: "We have also established Motherbrain, the team behind our first playable generative AI experience Teammates, as a dedicated subsidiary developing proprietary technologies for gameplay to empower creators and open up new possibilities for richer, more immersive and engaging player experiences" [4]. Its brief is smart NPCs with memory and AI game masters managing large-scale systemic worlds, supporting all Ubisoft studios and aiming to partner beyond Ubisoft [4][5]. The player and press reaction, reported as reaction and not as fact: social coverage pairing the 166 cuts with the Black Flag success claim drew predominantly negative, frustrated comments asking how a successful remake ends in layoffs, and Engadget cited a GDC survey in which 1 in 3 industry workers reported a layoff in the past two years and 55 percent said generative AI is having a negative effect on the industry [5].

Commentary: Judge it by what players and workers actually got. A remake players bought, in premium editions, above the company's own forecast — and 166 of the people at the two studios that make the flagship series are gone the same week, while the savings are announced alongside a new AI subsidiary named, apparently without irony, after a rogue AI boss. Companies may cut; that is their right and sometimes their necessity. But a January promise of "final" that wasn't, followed by six months of quieter rounds, is a credibility number, and unlike unit sales, Ubisoft published that one itself: close to 1,100.
