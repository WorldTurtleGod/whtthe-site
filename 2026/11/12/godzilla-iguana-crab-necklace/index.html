The Iguana That Looks Like Godzilla — Wearing a Crab Necklace

A Galápagos marine iguana went viral this week with a Sally Lightfoot crab draped across its neck like jewelry. The photographer swears it's real. It is.

By Juno·Thursday, November 12, 2026·Edition 58

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Wildlife photographer Dale Morris posted photos on Instagram on Oct. 31 of a Galápagos marine iguana standing on a rock with bright red Sally Lightfoot crabs climbing all over it — in one shot, a crab sits across its neck like a necklace. [1][2] Complex posted the photos to its X account on Wednesday morning, Nov. 11, and they went viral, amassing over 3.2 million views in just a few hours. The caption: "Photographer captures iguana that looks like Godzilla." [1]

The resemblance is the whole joke, and it is a good one. The iguana's spiky crest, its posture, the way it stares down the camera — social media users saw Godzilla immediately, and they were not wrong to. The original 1954 Godzilla was modeled after three dinosaurs (T. rex, Iguanodon, Stegosaurus), and marine iguanas genuinely did influence the 2014 Godzilla design: Weta Workshop's Christian Pearce said the team "did look at the natural world, including marine creatures" because "Godzilla spends 99 per cent of his life underwater so we looked at marine iguanas." [1]

And the crab necklace is not a trick. The interaction is a real cleaning behavior — the crabs pick parasites and debris off the iguana's scales — and Morris has replied to commenters confirming the photos are real, not AI. [1][2] The original Oct. 31 post drew 78,000 likes; Complex's Instagram version on Nov. 11 drew 47,000. [1]

Parade, which covered the viral moment, put it best: "No movie-adapted Godzilla has worn a crab necklace." [1] True, and a shame. The King of the Monsters has had seventy years of movies, and not one of them thought to accessorize.
