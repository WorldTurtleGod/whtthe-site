The Kennedy Center Is Switching to Ticketmaster. Its Former President Sat on Ticketmaster's Parent Board.

NPR reports that the nation's performing arts center is dropping the nonprofit ticketing system it used for decades. While the deal was being negotiated, its then-president was drawing a $260,205 board salary from Live Nation.

By Herodotus·Thursday, November 12, 2026·Edition 58

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

The Kennedy Center Honors are on Dec. 7. As of Nov. 11, you could not buy a ticket. The center offered only a form to sign up for information about how tickets would eventually go on sale, and it did not tell NPR how or when that would happen. [1]

Current and former staffers told NPR the delay comes from a change of plumbing. For decades the Kennedy Center has sold tickets through Tessitura, a not-for-profit platform the Metropolitan Opera developed in 1995. The center was one of its first users. Now it plans to move to Ticketmaster. [1]

Here is the part NPR focused on. While that relationship was being negotiated, the center's president, Richard Grenell, was also a paid board member at Ticketmaster's parent company, Live Nation. [1] Grenell became Kennedy Center president in February 2025, when President Trump became the institution's chairman, and joined Live Nation's board that May. Live Nation's SEC filings show he was paid $260,205 for his board work in 2025 and given 102 shares of stock, worth about $14,200, when he joined. The Kennedy Center's latest tax filings say he was paid nothing as its president. [1] He left the presidency in March. [1]

"This is literally conflict of interest," Columbia Law School government-ethics professor Richard Briffault told NPR, explaining that sitting on one board while holding a financial stake in a company doing business with it is "a problem." [1]

The Kennedy Center tells it differently. Spokesperson PJ Elliott said the center began evaluating Ticketmaster in the spring of 2024, during the Biden administration and before Grenell arrived, after deciding it "had outgrown its previous ticketing system," and that "the agreement followed standard procurement review." [1] Ticketmaster said it "categorically" denies that anyone pressured the center and that center staff first approached it in February 2024. [1] Two unnamed current and former employees told NPR that Ticketmaster was never under serious consideration in 2024. NPR's attempts to reach Grenell went unanswered. [1]

There is a second name in the paper trail. NPR obtained a March email in which Rick Canny, then a consultant and now the center's artistic director, told the chief financial officer: "I've shaped many or most of the terms of the deal and I'm reviewing the final agreement tonight." [1] Canny, a longtime talent agent for 1980s and '90s rock acts, did not respond to NPR. [1]

The partner matters too. A jury found in April that Live Nation and Ticketmaster operated as a monopoly that overcharged ticket buyers, in a suit brought by 33 states and the District of Columbia after they rejected the Justice Department's $280 million settlement. Live Nation has denied operating a monopoly. [1]

Commentary. James Madison put the principle in one sentence in Federalist No. 10: "No man is allowed to be a judge in his own cause, because his interest would certainly bias his judgment, and, not improbably, corrupt his integrity." [2] The Kennedy Center is a public institution, and its chairman is the sitting president. [1] Whether or not the Ticketmaster deal was good business, the man running the institution should not have been collecting a paycheck from the other side of the table while it was being worked out. The founders didn't trust virtue to police itself. That's why they built rules, and the rules apply at the theater too.
