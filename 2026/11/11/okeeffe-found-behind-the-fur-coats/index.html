A Lost O'Keeffe Spent 40 Years Behind the Fur Coats. Next Week It Goes on the Block.

Known for decades only from a single 1931 photograph, Georgia O'Keeffe's "Near Abiquiu, New Mexico I" turned up in an East Village closet. Christie's sells it November 17, estimated at $3 million to $5 million, and you can see it free until then.

By Herodotus·Wednesday, November 11, 2026·Edition 57

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

Every age leaves things in closets. Most of it is umbrellas. Now and then it is a masterpiece.

Earlier this year, a woman in Manhattan's East Village asked her niece to help sort her belongings and settle her affairs. She mentioned, casually, that there was a Georgia O'Keeffe in the front closet. Behind dozens of coats the niece found an old wooden box. Inside was "Near Abiquiu, New Mexico I," a 10-by-24-inch oil from 1930, with O'Keeffe's "OK" signature on the original backboard, Christie's says. [1]

The painting had been a graduation gift when the owner earned her master's degree in the 1960s. Her mother bought it for $3,800 on April 15, 1963, at The Downtown Gallery on 51st Street, O'Keeffe's dealer in Manhattan. [1] Smithsonian magazine puts that at roughly $42,000 in today's money. [2]

She hung it in her apartment until the mid-1980s. Then, her family told Christie's, the fiercely private owner got nervous that a guest at a bridge game was "eyeing it a bit too closely." She put it back in its original box and stored it in the closet, where it stayed, still in the gallery's packing, until she told her niece about it this year. She died shortly afterward. The family has asked to stay anonymous; Christie's describes the owner as a Foreign Service veteran fluent in at least four languages and a world-ranked bridge master. [1]

For the art world, the painting had been officially "unlocated." It appears only in an appendix of O'Keeffe's catalogue raisonné, known from a 1931 photograph, which Smithsonian reports was taken by her husband, Alfred Stieglitz. [1][2] Christie's says it is likely the first of three O'Keeffe paintings of Lobo Peak, north of Taos Pueblo. The second hangs in the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and the third is in a private collection that once belonged to David and Peggy Rockefeller. [1]

The painting will be offered in Christie's 20th Century Evening Sale in New York on Tuesday, November 17. The lot page lists an updated estimate of $3 million to $5 million. Until the sale it is on free public view at Christie's, 20 Rockefeller Center. [1][3]

"Rediscoveries are rare in the well-established market for academically researched and highly revered historic American artists," said Tylee Abbott, Christie's international director of American art. [1]

There's a quiet moral in it. One sharp-eyed guest at a card table kept an O'Keeffe out of sight for four decades. The painting survived because someone who loved it chose to hide it rather than sell it. Next week a room of bidders will decide what that care was worth.
