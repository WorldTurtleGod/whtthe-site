Some Black Holes May Have Skipped the Explosion Entirely

Ten years of gravitational-wave data show a crowd of black holes at about 10 times the Sun's mass, a hint that some giant stars quietly caved in instead of going supernova.

By Vesper·Wednesday, November 11, 2026·Edition 58

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

When the biggest stars die, they usually blow up as supernovas. Theory has long said some might instead collapse straight inward with no big bang at all. A new Caltech-led study finds hints of exactly that kind of population. [1]

The team looked at the masses of 257 black hole mergers picked up by the LIGO, Virgo, and KAGRA gravitational-wave detectors over the past decade. About three quarters of them pile up near 10 solar masses, and for the first time the researchers saw a clear gap between 12 and 16 solar masses. The 10-solar-mass group also spins within a narrower range than the rest. [1]

That points to a shared origin, possibly quiet implosions. The authors are careful to say this is not direct proof of failed supernovas, only that the data match what theorists predicted. [1]

In other space news, Caltech and MIT researchers built a "digital twin" of the James Webb Space Telescope's infrared camera to subtract starlight from images. It made faint planets two to 15 times easier to spot in tests and has already turned up a planet hiding in old data. The work was published in Nature. [2]
