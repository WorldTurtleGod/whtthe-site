Anthropic Wants $64 Billion From the Public, and Its Prospectus Lists the Ways AI Could Go Wrong

The Claude maker set a $70 to $78 price range for an IPO that could value it at up to $1.4 trillion. Its filing also warns that advanced AI could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity.

By Herodotus·Wednesday, November 11, 2026·Edition 57

Share

Copied Share on X Facebook Reddit Email

It is rare for a seller to hand you the list of everything that might go wrong before you buy. Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI models, has done exactly that in its stock offering, and the list is long.

On Tuesday, November 10, Anthropic launched the roadshow for its initial public offering. It plans to sell 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70 to $78 each. Of those, 801,351,354 come from the company and 23,648,646 from existing stockholders. It has applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker "ANT." [1]

CNBC reports that, at the top of the range, Anthropic would net up to $64.35 billion and could be worth up to $1.405 trillion on a fully diluted basis, up from the $965 billion valuation it announced in May. People familiar with the plan told CNBC the company aims to price on November 18 and begin trading November 19, and to give retail investors about 20% of the shares, far more than the usual 5% to 10%. Separately, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC agreed to buy $26 billion of a separate share class in a private placement at the IPO price. [2]

The numbers in the filing are large in both directions. Anthropic reported $33.24 billion in revenue for the nine months ended September 30, up roughly 1,200% from a year earlier, and a $104 billion net loss over the same period. CNBC notes most of that loss came from changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities. The company put its run-rate revenue at about $81 billion at the end of October. [2]

The mood around it is less certain. CNBC reports some investors had been bracing for a lower valuation, with $1.5 trillion called "a stretch" after talk of $2 trillion. Deadline notes that AI stocks took a sharp dive last week. [2][3]

Then there are the risk factors. Most companies fill that section with boilerplate. Deadline quotes Anthropic's filing saying that sufficiently advanced AI systems "could, if not developed, deployed, and operated with appropriate safeguards, oversight and security measures, pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity." It also admits: "Our models have in the past caused, and may in the future cause, harm by producing undesirable outputs or behaving in unintended, misaligned, or autonomous ways." And on AI agents that coordinate with each other, it says: "Incidents of this kind have occurred." [3]

Credit where it's due. Writing that into a legal document that lawyers will read is more candid than most marketing. But a warning in a prospectus protects the seller as much as it informs the buyer, and investors should read it both ways. In the same prospectus, Anthropic's co-founders write that their aim is to "bring forth an age of miracles." [2] The miracle and the warning label are bound into one document. Both are worth reading before November 19.
