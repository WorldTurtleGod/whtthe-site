Lift a lid in southern Poland in November and you may find the neighbors have moved in for the winter.

The wildlife rescue center Leśne Pogotowie got a call that venomous vipers had taken refuge in an underground water meter chamber. [1] TVP World reported that a worker froze at the sight of them. [2] What rescuers found were 19 slow worms. [1]

Slow worms look like snakes, but they are legless lizards. They are not venomous, pose no threat to people, and earn their keep in gardens by eating snails, earthworms and other small invertebrates. [1] Poland has two species, the common and the eastern slow worm, and many eastern males carry blue spots on their backs. [1]

The chamber was a fine place to spend the winter: cool, damp and shielded from freezing by concrete. [1] It was also a trap. Its smooth vertical walls let the lizards slip in easily, with no way to climb back out. [1] Rescuers said the hideout could have become a death trap. [2]

They couldn't simply be let go either. The slow worms were already in winter torpor, and releasing them into the cold would leave them little time to find new shelter. [1] So they went to the rehabilitation center, where a stable, low temperature will let them keep sleeping. They will be released in spring. [1]

Nineteen lizards, one pit, no legs among them. A reminder that the scariest thing in the hole is often just whatever you expected to see.
