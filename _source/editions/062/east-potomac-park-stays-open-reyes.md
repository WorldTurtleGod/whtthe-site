On October 18, President Trump announced on Truth Social that East Potomac Park's three links-style golf courses would become one 18-hole championship course. [1] The park was set to close on Sunday, November 15. On Saturday, the day before, a federal judge said not yet.

U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes of the District of Columbia ordered that East Potomac Park, its golf course and the surrounding bike and walking paths stay open to the public while the lawsuit proceeds, "except for routine, temporary closures consistent with the Course's historical operating practices, and except as needed to address articulated, imminent threats to public safety." [1] Bloomberg Law reported that the order keeps the park open for two weeks. [2] It does not force the government to run the golf operation, the pro shop or the concession stand, but the government "cannot use the absence of golf operations as a reason to exclude the public from the Course." [1]

The government said the closure was about safety, chiefly a seawall in need of repair, and that the course no longer had an operator. [1][2] Reyes found both reasons likely pretextual. The real reason, she wrote, was to carry out the president's renovation plan, and the government likely broke environmental and administrative laws in its "haste to meet an arbitrary deadline." [2] She pointed out that safety concerns had been raised for years, including in a May assessment, while the course stayed open, and that the Park Service had managed to cut down more than 400 healthy trees within ten days. [1][2] "If public safety were at risk, NPS would have taken a step — any step — to address the safety issues it knew about," she wrote. [1] She called the decision to end the prior contractor's management an "entirely self-inflicted and self-serving injury." [2]

The plaintiffs are the DC Preservation League and two Washington-area golfers. [2] Reyes has also said she is opening an inquiry into whether Justice and Interior Department lawyers made false statements or withheld information in the case. [2]

The administration is not backing down. A Justice Department spokesperson said "nothing in the Court's order precludes planning for the development of a world class golf course to continue." [1][2] Trump called the park "Trash Island" before the ruling and, on Sunday, promised to "immediately appeal, on an expedited basis, this highly destructive and political decision of the judge." [1][2]

**Commentary.** This is a fight about a golf course, and it is tempting to shrug. Don't. Madison wrote in Federalist No. 47 that "the accumulation of all powers, legislative, executive, and judiciary, in the same hands" is "the very definition of tyranny." [3] Congress wrote laws that say how public land is closed and changed: with notice, a record and real reasons. When an executive branch announces the outcome on social media first and then assembles a justification to fit the date, it is quietly doing the legislature's job and asking the courts not to look. A judge asking for the true reason is the system working as drawn. If the plan is good, it will survive an honest process.
