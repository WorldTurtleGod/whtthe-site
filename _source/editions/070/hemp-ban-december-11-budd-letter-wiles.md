Two deadlines land on the same day. Government funding runs out Dec. 11. So does the last reprieve for the intoxicating hemp industry. Fourteen Republican senators want to make sure the second one doesn't get traded away to fix the first.

Sen. Ted Budd (R-N.C.) released a letter Monday, Nov. 23, that he and 13 Republican colleagues sent to President Trump urging him to let the federal ban on intoxicating hemp products take effect on Dec. 11 "as the law now provides." [1][2] The signers besides Budd: Pete Ricketts, Tom Cotton, Kevin Cramer, Marsha Blackburn, James Lankford, Joni Ernst, John Cornyn, Mike Rounds, Mitch McConnell, Rick Scott, Chuck Grassley, Jim Banks and Cynthia Lummis. [1] Budd's office says the letter went out Friday, Nov. 20. [1] A copy posted by the Eagle Forum is dated Nov. 18, so the sources differ on the date. [3]

"The choice is simple: stand with American families who don't want candy-flavored drugs sold in gas stations or with the cartel who profits from addiction and devastation," the senators wrote. "There can be no last-minute 'framework' that keeps these drugs on store shelves." [1][2]

Trump signed the ban last year in Section 781 of Public Law 119-37, the bill that reopened the government after the record-long 2025 shutdown. [1][2] Under the new definition, legal hemp products are limited to 0.4 milligrams of total THC per container, far less than most popular products contain, The Hill reported. [2] The ban was set to start in November. Then a one-month delay was written into a later stopgap funding bill, moving the date to Dec. 11. The Hill says that funding bill passed in August, while the senators' letter says Trump signed the extension in September. [1][2] The senators' letter says the administration "made clear at the time that there would be no further extensions." [1]

Who wanted that delay? NBC News reported in August, citing four people with direct knowledge, that the White House was actively pushing it, and that the ban would hit a business run by a member of Susie Wiles' family. Wiles was White House chief of staff at the time. [4] The Hill reported this week that the delay was inserted "reportedly at the behest of" Wiles, who was replaced as chief of staff earlier this month. [2] Budd and Ricketts tried to strip the delay from that bill with an amendment. It failed, and leadership passed the bill without it. [2]

Now there is a competing plan. Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) and Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) introduced a bill last week to regulate hemp instead of banning it. It would bar sales of consumable hemp products to anyone under 21, raise the legal THC threshold and ban "gas station" marijuana products made from hemp. [2][5] The letter calls any such framework "a permanent legal license for an industry that has spent years marketing drugs to kids." [1] Budd has already said on X that he "will not support any year-end package that extends, delays, or builds a regulatory framework around" these products, and Ricketts said he agrees. [5]

That puts hemp squarely in the year-end funding fight. If leaders try to fold a delay or the Sheehy-Klobuchar framework into the bill that keeps the government open, they will be doing it over the public objection of 14 senators in their own party.

**Commentary.** You can think adults should be free to buy a THC gummy and still see what's wrong here. A law passed by Congress was quietly postponed in a must-pass spending bill, and NBC and The Hill tie the push to a White House official whose own family business, according to NBC, stood to lose. That is the kind of private interest the Founders feared. Madison warned in Federalist No. 62 about laws that "undergo such incessant changes that no man, who knows what the law is to-day, can guess what it will be to-morrow," and about the "moneyed few" who profit from that instability. The date is already in the law. If Congress wants a ban, a framework or nothing, it should vote on that in daylight, not slip it into a shutdown deal.
