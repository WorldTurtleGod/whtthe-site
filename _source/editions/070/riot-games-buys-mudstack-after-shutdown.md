Riot Games announced Friday, Nov. 20, that it has acquired Mudstack, a digital asset and workflow management platform built for game studios, and that Mudstack's team has joined the company. [1] The League of Legends developer did not disclose a price. [1][3] Riot is a U.S. subsidiary of China's Tencent, which lists it as 100 percent owned, on an outstanding basis, in its 2025 annual report. [5][6][7]

That announcement landed about two months after Mudstack told its customers the product was finished.

On Sept. 14, founder and CEO Jordan Stevens posted a notice on the company's website: Mudstack was shutting down, and its agreements with customers and users terminated that day. Workspaces would stay readable until Sept. 28, "the last day to get your files off the platform," after which access would end and the public API would be disabled. Hosted customer data would be "permanently destroyed within 90 days of the platform shutting down," Stevens wrote, and "none of it is being sold or transferred to any third party." The notice gave no detailed reason for the closure. [2]

Mudstack was a cloud tool for managing game-art files, so artists could find, manage and share their work. Stevens founded the company in Atlanta in 2019. In August 2024, GeekWire reported that it had raised $4 million led by Anthos Capital, with Khosla Ventures, a16z Games, Pioneer Square Labs and Hyperplane participating, and that its customers then included Metacore and Paradox Studios. [4]

Riot's LinkedIn post framed the purchase as a way to improve its own pipeline. Mudstack's platform, the company said, "will help us better connect the different parts of our content creation pipeline, making it easier for artists to find, manage, and share their work while reducing time spent on repetitive tasks." [1] Stevens, identified in the same post as founder and CEO "and now officially a Rioter," said Riot had shared his vision "from our first conversations" and that "there's no better home for Mudstack." Laura DeYoung, Riot's senior vice president of Central Creative and creative director for League of Legends, said easier collaboration would show up "in the worlds and experiences we build." [1]

The post does not mention the September shutdown. It does not say whether the public service will reopen for the studios that used to pay for it. And it does not say when the deal was agreed.

Vertical Slice, a games-industry trade, reported the acquisition on Monday, Nov. 23, and described the fee as undisclosed. According to Vertical Slice, Stevens will become tools and pipeline technical art director at Riot's U.S. studio. The outlet wrote that it is "unclear whether Riot saved Mudstack at the death or the platform was shut down as a result of the acquisition." [3]

Tencent's ownership is not a rumor. Tencent announced a deal to acquire a majority stake in Riot in February 2011. [6] In December 2015, Tencent told TechCrunch it had "purchased the remaining equity of Riot" after owning "around 93 percent" beforehand. [7] Tencent's 2025 annual report lists Riot Games, Inc., established in the United States, as a 100 percent-owned subsidiary on an outstanding basis as of the year ended Dec. 31, 2025. [5]

**Commentary.** A specialized tool that studios trusted with their work vanished on two weeks' notice, and the first public explanation was a hiring announcement from a Tencent-owned publisher that now gets the talent and the technology for itself. Nobody has shown that Riot took customer files; Mudstack said those would be destroyed, not sold. The problem is the silence around everything else. When the deal was struck, whether the shutdown was the price of the sale, and whether anyone besides Riot will ever use this software again are basic questions. A company that asks artists to hand over their pipelines can at least tell them who the tool is for now.
