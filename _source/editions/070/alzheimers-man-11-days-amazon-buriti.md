The animals had been eating the buriti fruit too. The trackers could tell which ones a man had bitten.

Janarino Ferreira dos Santos, 74, has Alzheimer's disease. He disappeared into a forested area in Amapá, a state in Brazil's Amazon, and was missing for 11 days, Brazilian news site g1 reported. Family, firefighters, park rangers and volunteers searched for him. [1] Rescuers found him alive on the afternoon of Friday, Nov. 20, about 10 kilometers from where he went missing. [1][2] He is recovering in a hospital in Macapá, the state capital. [2][3]

He almost wasn't found. His granddaughter, Sara Montes, posted a video on Instagram pleading with firefighters to keep looking, because the search was going to be suspended that Sunday, RTÉ reported. "Don't forget about us. Don't forget about Grandpa," she said. [2]

How he lived is the strange part, and the trackers pieced it together from what he left behind.

First, the fruit. Searchers found buriti, the fruit of an Amazon palm, on the trail. "There were several buritis eaten by agoutis, but we also found fruit eaten by a person. The bite is different. And we found his footprints nearby," tracker and first responder Aniel Cardoso told g1, in Portuguese. [1] Rescuers believe he drank from small natural springs in the area. [1][2]

Second, the beds. Following his footprints, the team found resting spots he had made from açaí palm leaves. "We found a bed that looked like it had been used three or four days earlier, and another more recent one," park ranger Nerivan da Silva told g1. [1]

Third, the circles. Da Silva noticed that the footprints passed the same spots over and over. "He was walking in a circle. The footprints showed he went back and forth. That's when we were sure he was still in that area," he said. [1] The rescuers told g1 the pattern may be tied to Alzheimer's, which disrupts a person's sense of direction, and they narrowed the search to that patch of forest. [1]

The last clue was a smell. At one of his sleeping spots, searchers noticed a strong odor of urine. His family had told them he had urinary problems. A few meters past the last bed, they saw him. "He was sitting, only in his underwear and very weak. He no longer knew where he was," da Silva said. [1] Video posted by g1 shows men carrying him out of the forest on a makeshift stretcher, RTÉ reported. [2]

His past may have saved him. Searchers learned he had worked for years as a garimpeiro, an informal gold miner, and knew the forest. "He was already a woodsman. A garimpeiro spends days and days walking in the bush," Cardoso said. [1] Even so, the rescuers, who are used to working in harsh parts of the Amazon, told g1 that finding an elderly man with Alzheimer's alive after 11 days is a rare ending. [1]
