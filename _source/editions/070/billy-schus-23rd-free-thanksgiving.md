On Thursday, November 26, Billy Schu's restaurant at 15 Seneca Street in Hornell, New York, will serve free turkey dinners with all the trimmings to anyone who asks — homebound deliveries and pickups, no questions asked. It will be the 23rd consecutive Thanksgiving the restaurant has done it [1][2].

The program was started by the restaurant's namesake, Billy Schu, in 1987. Ian and Lori Cramp, married 32 years, bought the restaurant in 2004 and kept it going from their first year [1]. More than 500 dinners are expected in 2026; the restaurant provided more than 500 in 2025, and as of November 16 it already had requests for 384 meals [1]. Deliveries run 11 a.m. to 1 p.m., pickups 1 to 3:30 p.m. at 15 Seneca Street on Thanksgiving Day [1]. Reservations were taken through Monday, November 23.

Lori Cramp does the planning; Ian does the cooking. "I have always wanted to help people, so, I was all onboard," Lori recalled. "I didn't know what I was doing at all, but we gave it a whirl and it was a success. And it has grown every year" [1]. "I do all the planning. He does all the cooking. And I do anything else he asks me to do, like slice the pumpkin pies," she said. "It works out well that way. He doesn't want to get on my turf, and I don't want to get on his" [1].

This year the family changed its own holiday to serve longer. "We are changing our family Thanksgiving dinner to (the Saturday after the holiday) so we can be here a little longer on Thanksgiving," Lori Cramp said [1]. Her mother, Liz Perry, joins the volunteer dishwashing crew this year [1]. Their daughters — Danielle, Amber and Victoria — have volunteered since day one. "They have been volunteering since day one," Lori said. "And back then they used to make turkey hands (for table decorations) and help with the butter and rolls. And they still make turkey hands at their ages because I make them" [1]. "There are always many hands needed to make this all happen," the Cramps said [1].

The 2026 dinners are dedicated to Matthew Nelson, 25, the son of a close family friend and a colleague of Billy Schu's, who died November 10, 2026, and Adam Cramp, 18, Lori and Ian's nephew, who died July 16, 2025 — both described by the source as young people who "died after struggles with mental health" [1]. Lori Cramp said the dedications are an attempt to bring attention to the mental health crisis and to remind people who are struggling that help is available [1]. If you are struggling, or know someone who is: call, text or chat 988, or visit 988lifeline.org [1].

The work reaches beyond Hornell. Alfred University and Saxon Dining are partnering with the restaurant again this year to deliver meals to students staying on campus over the break — to The Community Table in Powell Campus Center at 4 p.m. on Thanksgiving Day [2]. The Cramps' daughters Amber and Tori are Alfred graduates [2].

No agency runs this. No grant, no program office, no press release from a foundation. It is a family restaurant, a donation box, and volunteers who have shown up for twenty-three years — the same people, the same street, the same promise that anybody who asks gets fed. That is the whole story, and it is enough.
