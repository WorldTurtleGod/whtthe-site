President Trump's 250-foot "triumphal arch" at Memorial Circle, the traffic circle at the Virginia end of Memorial Bridge, is on hold again. This time the order has no expiration date.

U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema of the Eastern District of Virginia ruled from the bench in Alexandria on Friday, Nov. 20, in a lawsuit brought by the Arlington County Board. Plaintiffs' lawyers said her injunction keeps construction stopped indefinitely, according to the Associated Press. [1] JURIST reported that her opinion found the county likely to win on the merits. [2]

**What the judge wrote.** "The record shows that the planning and proposed construction of the Arch was done in an arbitrary and capricious manner, without congressional approval, and in violation of the CWA [Commemorative Works Act of 1986] and [National Park Service's] Organic Act among other laws, and therefore in violation of the [Administrative Procedure Act]," Brinkema wrote, as quoted by JURIST. [2] The administration had relied on a 1925 law, Public Law 68-463, which authorized a memorial bridge across the Potomac. JURIST reported that Brinkema rejected that argument. She also found flaws in the required economic and environmental reviews, including the effects on nearby Reagan Washington National Airport. [2]

**The second order.** On Wednesday, U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan in D.C. extended her temporary restraining order against the arch to Dec. 3. That case combines suits by three Vietnam veterans and an architectural historian with a joint suit by Virginia and the District of Columbia. [1][2] Members of the Kennedy family have filed a separate suit over the arch's effect on Arlington National Cemetery. [1] Chutkan first blocked construction on Nov. 5. That came shortly after the National Capital Planning Commission, whose members the AP describes as mostly Trump appointees, approved the arch 8-4 even though dozens of witnesses spoke against it. [1]

**The scale.** The arch would be twice the height of the Lincoln Memorial, about half a mile from the cemetery's main entrance, the AP reported. [1] A Washington Post analysis found its shadow would touch burial sites on 284 days a year and pass over more than 75,000 graves, the AP reported. [1] Arlington County says first responders answered about 200 calls in the area in 60 days and argues that traffic and visitors would make safety worse, according to JURIST. [2]

"It's a billboard for an ego," Bart Collart told the AP. His son, Marine Cpl. Spencer Collart, is buried in Section 52 after dying in a 2023 Osprey crash. [1] Kate Talmor of Democracy Forward, a lawyer for the county, called the arch a "massive vanity project." [1][2] Supporters disagree. Jared Craig of Veterans for America First said the arch "was always supposed to be a gateway to connect D.C. to Arlington." White House spokesman Davis Ingle said it would serve as "a visual reminder of the noble sacrifices borne by so many American heroes." [1]

Brinkema was nominated by President Bill Clinton and Chutkan by President Barack Obama, the AP noted. [1]

**Commentary.** The Commemorative Works Act is not a technicality. It is Congress deciding, in public, what gets built on the nation's ground. Madison warned in Federalist No. 47 that "the accumulation of all powers, legislative, executive, and judiciary, in the same hands ... may justly be pronounced the very definition of tyranny." [3] A president who builds what Congress never approved is taking on Congress's job. A president who wants a monument should ask Congress for one. If the arch honors veterans as the White House says, it should have no trouble getting a vote. Building it by planning-commission vote and a stretched reading of a 1925 bridge law is building it without permission.
