Grand Theft Auto VI launched Thursday, November 19, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, with no PC version announced [1]. On November 20, Take-Two Interactive and Rockstar Games announced the numbers, and they need no adjectives. In the press release's own words: "Grand Theft Auto VI day one retail sales* reached $3.79 billion, setting a new record for entertainment across any medium, and becoming the biggest entertainment launch in history" [2]. And: "Achieving a near perfect score of 99 on Metacritic and resounding critical acclaim, Grand Theft Auto VI sold 32.5 million units pre-release, a new record across all platforms" [2]. CNN, reporting the same announcement, notes Sensor Tower's independent pre-release estimate was lower — $2.36 billion before the November 19 release — and that more than 80 percent of preorder buyers chose the $100 Ultimate Edition over the $80 standard edition [3].

Print the caveats in the same breath, because they decide what the numbers mean. The $3.79 billion is a company estimate of worldwide retail sales — what shoppers spent through the end of November 19 — not Take-Two revenue [2][3]. No launch-day unit total was given beyond the 32.5 million pre-release figure, and this paper does not invent one by adding the two together [2][3]. Rockstar president and co-founder Sam Houser, via GameSpot: "It is with immense gratitude that we enter a new chapter of Rockstar Games with the release of Grand Theft Auto VI." "We are incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world and we couldn't be more excited to share this moment with you" [4].

The critics' half: a 99 Metascore on PlayStation 5 from 150 critic reviews, 99 on Xbox Series X, and 99 on OpenCritic [1]. The players' half starts with a number Rockstar chose: the game targets 30 frames per second on every console including PS5 Pro, with no 60fps performance mode and no 40fps balanced mode at launch [1][5]. Player-reported launch problems, collected by iGame Magazine and labeled by that outlet as player-reported rather than confirmed by Rockstar, include falling through the map, clipping through walls, crashes and lost progress, a mission softlock, and PlayStation Network sign-in errors; Rockstar had posted no known-issues list or patch notes at that outlet's time of writing [1]. On X, in posts collected by PlayStation LifeStyle and printed here as reaction, not benchmarks: "GTA 6 has HORRIBLE performance. It drops down to 20 FPS in some areas," wrote @ExoGhost [5]. Another player reported drops to 15–20 FPS during fights on a new PS5 [5]. One player's claim is one player's claim — but the volume of identical complaints is itself the player atmosphere this desk reports.

Then the score war, which says more about the scorers than the game. Metacritic's user score for the PS5 version was reported at 5.0 by Dexerto and 5.4 by Push Square, both on November 21 — a moving snapshot, not a settled number — amid waves of zero-score reviews, some from accounts with little other history, and retaliatory zeros against another game's remake [6][7]. Metacritic requires no proof of ownership. The PlayStation Store rating, which does require ownership, stood at 4.84 out of 5 [6][7]. Print both or print neither; this paper prints both. Meanwhile, players hunting for the 60fps Rockstar did not offer found an Xbox Quick Resume glitch that unlocks the frame rate while speeding up game timing — demand, demonstrated [8].

Commentary: Judge it by what players actually got, and by this desk's standing rule: when the sales number is good, the publisher leads with sales. Take-Two led with $3.79 billion and 32.5 million units. That is not the "reach" tell; that is a record, stated in dollars. The other half is equally real: thirteen years of waiting bought a 30fps ceiling on the strongest consoles ever sold, launch bugs Rockstar had not publicly catalogued, and a user-score fight in which the only ratings tied to ownership say 4.84 out of 5. A 99 from critics, a fortune in a day, and a couch experience players are already glitching their way around. All three are true at once, and any review that prints only one of them is selling something.
