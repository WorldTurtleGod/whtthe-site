Some records are set with a stopwatch. This one was set on fire.

Stanisław Madej of Poland performed the farthest distance full body burn on a zip wire over 173.3 meters — 568 feet 6.8 inches, longer than an American football field — in Łódź, Poland, on October 3, more than doubling the 61.45-meter record Josef Tödtling of Austria set while on fire in 2023 [1]. Guinness World Records published the record on Friday, November 20 [1]; Parade covered it Saturday, November 21 [2]. Print the dates exactly: the stunt was October 3; the record's publication is this week's news.

Madej, 43, is a professional: stunt credits going back to 2005, including the TV shows Barbarians and The Witcher and the Netflix film Paradise [1][2]. He chose Tyrolka w Manufakturze, a 180-meter zip line at the Manufaktura complex, which he has run for the 10 years it has been open, and prepared for two months — with all practice runs done without fire, because the site sits in a shopping centre [1]. The safety plan was not an afterthought: teams with high-pressure fire hoses were posted along the route, and they practised extinguishing a dummy the day before [1][2].

His own account of the method is the best copy in the story. "I wore specialized protective clothing and was coated in a special water-based gel, which insulated my body from excessive heat. It was important to me to attempt the record with my face exposed - without a mask, covered only in the gel - as I believe that direct exposure to fire during such a feat elevates its significance" [1]. And on the element itself: "Personally, I believe that any contact with fire is dangerous. It is an elemental force that is often unpredictable; our experience allows us to react in advance to its erratic behaviour." "A situation that appears to be under control can very quickly escalate into a serious threat. That is why a high degree of awareness, concentration, and, above all, respect for fire are essential" [1]. No injury is reported in either source, and this paper adds none.

Five hundred sixty-eight feet. Most of us cannot stay calm for 568 feet of anything. A man from Łódź rode it fully engulfed, face uncovered, on a line he has operated for a decade, with hose teams rehearsed on a dummy the day before — and the trick, as with every record worth printing, was preparation arranged so the fire was the only surprise left. Guinness, for once, understates it: "a cool – but very dangerous – world record" [1].
