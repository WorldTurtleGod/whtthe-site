Detroit's buses carry about every kind of passenger. Last Saturday, two of them had four legs apiece and saddles on.

Ellessia Drew was driving near 7 Mile and Strasburg Street when she filmed two men leading two horses onto a Detroit Department of Transportation bus stopped at the curb. The horses walked down the aisle and were led back off through the rear door. [1]

The man holding the reins was Jordan Williams, co-founder of Urban City Cowboys. He told WXYZ-TV he had been helping at a fundraiser giving children horseback rides, then spotted a bus pulling up to a stop and decided to make a quick video. He and the driver spoke through the bus door before he walked the horses on. [1] It was not his first time, he said. "We've been on the Jefferson, we've been on the Chalmers, we've been on the Conners and now on the 7 Mile," Williams said. "So we did, we walked on, and I must tell you the buses are a lot smaller than they used to be, or I may have been a little bigger." [1][3] He has said the point was to draw attention to Black cowboys and urban equestrians. [2]

The city was less charmed. DDOT first said the stunt was "not consistent with DDOT policy and potentially creates an unsafe and uncomfortable ride for our passengers." [1] In an updated statement after reviewing the video and the bus's own camera footage, the department said it welcomes service animals that meet the Americans with Disabilities Act's requirements, but "it appears that these horses were brought on the bus for other reasons." [2]

DDOT did give Williams some credit. It said it recognized the video "was meant to draw awareness to a positive cause," while it "presented safety concerns for other passengers on public transit and for the horses themselves." [2] The driver has been "spoken to," and every DDOT bus driver will get additional training on the rules. [2] The city also offered to connect local entrepreneurs with "programs and opportunities that safely increase awareness of their organizations." [2]

Williams, for his part, hopes it all goes over with a laugh. "I hope DDOT looks at it like a joke like everyone else, and hopefully they come out to a rodeo," he said. [1]

**A wry note.** Somewhere in Detroit there is now a training slide that says, in effect, "no horses." Every rulebook gets its oddest lines this way: someone tried it first.
