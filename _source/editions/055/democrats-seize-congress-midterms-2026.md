On Tuesday, November 3, voters did the thing this paper reported the White House was planning for: they handed both chambers of Congress to the Democrats. The Associated Press called the Senate late Tuesday night and the House at about 1:28 a.m. ET Wednesday, ending two years of unified Republican rule and setting up divided government for the final two years of President Donald Trump's term. [1]

The Senate flipped on seven Republican-held seats: Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina, and Texas. The names tell the scale of it. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico defeated Republican Ken Paxton, and Democrat Gina Hinojosa defeated three-term Governor Greg Abbott — the party's first statewide wins in Texas since 1994. In Maine, Democrat Troy Jackson defeated five-term Senator Susan Collins. In Kansas, Democrat Adam Hamilton won the state's first Democratic Senate seat since 1932. Democrat Sherrod Brown won in Ohio, Democrat Annie Andrews defeated Senator Darline Graham in South Carolina, Democrat Roy Cooper won the open seat in North Carolina, and Democrat Josh Turek's win in Iowa secured the majority. In Michigan, Democrat Abdul El-Sayed won to become the nation's first Muslim U.S. senator. [2][3]

Two cautions, stated plainly. First, final seat margins are still being counted — outlets differ on the exact totals, and results remain unofficial until certification. What is settled is control, not the final margin. Second, control does not change hands today. The new Congress is seated on January 3, 2027; until then, Republicans still run the chambers. House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries is in line to become Speaker when it does. [4]

Trump's overnight reaction, as reported across outlets, was to accept the results and say he would work with Democrats, while insisting the outcome was not a verdict on him personally. On Truth Social just after 10:30 p.m. Tuesday, he wrote: "I wasn't on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE!" Democrats, meanwhile, wasted no time signaling what comes next — Senator Ron Wyden posted a single word on X, "Subpoenas," and Representative Robert Garcia posted "LAWYER UP." [1][5]

That is the constitutional shape of the next two years, and it is the oldest one in the book. The voters did not elect a new president. They elected a check on the one they have. The House's two-year cycle exists for exactly this: a midterm is the people's scheduled chance to revise the mandate without waiting four years. They used it. Come January, the president keeps the executive branch, the veto, and foreign policy — and Congress, under new management, keeps the purse, the confirmations, and the subpoenas. That is not gridlock as a failure. That is the system working as designed.
