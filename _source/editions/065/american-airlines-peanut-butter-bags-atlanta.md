Some travel stories end with a lost bag. This one ends with a bag you wish you'd lost.

American Airlines Flight 2609 from Miami landed at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport around 8:30 p.m. Monday. [1][2] Passengers told WSB-TV in Atlanta they waited more than two hours at baggage claim before their luggage finally came out on carousel 8. [1]

When it did, dozens of bags were coated in a brown substance. [1]

"Now they're bringing the bags out. There is doo-doo on the bags," a passenger can be heard saying in a TikTok video of the moment. [1][2] Derick McCallum, who shot the video, described the scene: "As soon as they come out, you see everybody just yelling, 'Oh my God. Oh my God.' Like, what is going on?" [1] Another passenger, Billy Johnson, said, "Everybody started saying, you know, 'This is poop. This is poop.' I'm like, surely to God not, right?" [1]

American Airlines says it was not. In a statement, the airline said the substance was peanut butter that had liquefied and leaked out of a passenger's checked bag during the flight, getting on other luggage. [1][2] "We're very sorry for the experience, and our team will work directly with the affected customers to make it right," the airline said. [1][2]

Not everyone is buying it. "Even if somebody did pack peanut butter? How much peanut butter does somebody pack to cover that many bags?" McCallum said. [1][2] Passengers told WSB-TV they ended up cleaning their own luggage with disinfecting wipes and tissues. [1]

The airline did not say how much peanut butter was involved, what it was packed in, or why the bags took more than two hours to reach the carousel. Neither report says whether any bags were replaced or any passengers compensated. [1][2]

There is a reason someone might have checked a jar instead of carrying it on. The Transportation Security Administration lets travelers bring peanut butter in a carry-on only in containers of 3.4 ounces or less, the same limit it applies to liquids. Checked bags have no such limit. [3] So a family-size jar has to ride in the hold, alongside everyone else's clothes.

**Commentary.** The federal government treats peanut butter like a liquid at the checkpoint. [3] A jar of Jif goes under the plane because a checkpoint rule says it can't go in the cabin, and then a hold full of strangers' suitcases pays the price. Nobody in Washington will lose sleep over a smeared duffel bag. But it's a small, perfect picture of how a rule written for one problem quietly creates another, and the people who wrote it never see the mess.
