Three days after it set Japan's opening-day record, Godzilla Minus Zero did the same thing in North America — bigger. The film took an estimated $31.6 million over its November 6–8 opening weekend, topping the North American box office and setting the record for the biggest opening ever for a live-action foreign-language film there, beating the $17.8 million record held since 2002 by Zhang Yimou's Hero. [1][2]

It played in 3,254 theaters, earned about $14.5 million on Friday including previews, and finished nearly triple the North American opening of its predecessor, Godzilla Minus One. Worldwide the opening reached about $63 million. Toho said it was the first time a Japanese film had topped the opening-weekend charts in Japan and North America simultaneously. [1][3]

The audience numbers are as strong as the grosses. The film earned an A CinemaScore and a 96% Popcornmeter, with the Tomatometer settling at 90% from 162 reviews. Premium formats did the heavy lifting: IMAX alone brought in $12.7 million domestically — about 40% of the total — and premium large formats overall made up 74% of ticket sales. Warner Bros.' animated The Cat in the Hat finished second with $20.5 million. [2][3]

One note on precision: $31.6 million is the Sunday estimate; Monday's final count settled slightly lower, at $30.5 million. Either way the record stands — and so does the scale of it. Minus One opened to about $11.4 million in North America in December 2023 and finished its entire domestic run at roughly $57 million. Its sequel made more than half that in three days, on a reported budget under $20 million, with marketing spend in the region reportedly below $10 million. [1][3]

This paper said last week that Minus One was supposed to be the small Godzilla. Its sequel just proved the point in reverse: a Japanese-language monster movie, released by a small distributor, beat every Hollywood studio on the biggest moviegoing weekend of the fall — because audiences wanted to see it on the biggest screen they could find.
