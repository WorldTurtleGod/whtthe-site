Paleontologists have described a previously unknown extinct species of pygmy sperm whale with the most extreme facial asymmetry known in any cetacean — and possibly in any mammal. Its name is Scaphokogia huachafo, and the name is the joke: huachafo is Peruvian Spanish for something extravagant, bizarre, tacky, or over-the-top. [1][2]

The animal lived 6.3 to 5.6 million years ago, in the Late Miocene, off what is now southern Peru. It was about 2.7 meters long — roughly 9 feet, a little smaller than a modern pygmy sperm whale. Its skull, found in the mid-1990s in the Pisco Formation at Aguada de Lomas in the Sacaco basin of Arequipa, sat unnamed for decades until lead author Aldo Benites-Palomino examined it while working on his bachelor's thesis in 2015. The description was published November 9 in the journal Communications Biology. [1][3]

The asymmetry is not subtle. Both of the animal's bony nostrils are shifted entirely to the left side of the skull's midline, with the left one considerably larger than the right, and the bones around the eye sockets differ markedly in shape. The snout itself angles downward by 31 degrees relative to the rest of the skull, and the neck joint suggests the animal could flex its head down by as much as 55 degrees — the whole structure built to point its snout straight at the seafloor. "The skull looks completely crooked," Benites-Palomino said. [1][2]

It was not an injury and not bad preservation. The skull's sutures were fully closed, with no fractures, pathologies, or deformation elsewhere — the crookedness was genuine biology. The researchers read it as a specialized way of feeding: sweeping a downturned snout along the shallow seabed, a way of life that disappeared as ocean conditions changed at the end of the Miocene. Across a 31-species comparison, the genus Scaphokogia ranked as the most asymmetric whale group of all. [2][3]

Six million years later, the fossil gets the last laugh. It waited thirty years in a museum drawer for someone to notice it was new — and when they did, they named it for exactly what it looks like: completely over the top.
