The most anticipated game of the decade is now sitting, locked, on players' hard drives. Rockstar opened digital pre-loading for Grand Theft Auto VI today, Tuesday, November 10 — nine days before launch — and the file needs up to 212 GB, depending on the console. [1][2]

The timing splits by platform. On PlayStation 5, the pre-load opens at midnight local time in each player's time zone. On Xbox Series X|S, it opens once worldwide at midnight GMT — which was 7 p.m. ET / 4 p.m. PT on Monday, November 9. The files stay locked until the game releases at midnight local time on Thursday, November 19, in all regions on both consoles. There is still no announced PC version. [2]

This is also the first GTA with no disc at all: the boxed edition holds a download code, not a disc, and boxed copies reach retailers from November 12, so physical buyers can start their download then. Rockstar moved the pre-load forward from November 12 to November 10 on November 3. [1]

The business side arrived a day earlier. Take-Two reported its fiscal second-quarter results on Monday, November 9, and said GTA VI pre-orders continue to reach "unprecedented levels" — chairman and CEO Strauss Zelnick said pre-orders are "tracking far in excess" of the company's initial expectations, ten days out from launch. Net bookings were down 18% to $1.60 billion for the quarter, but Take-Two held its full-year guidance at $8.0–8.2 billion. [3]

The standard edition is $80 in the US; the Ultimate Edition is $100. For that money you get, this week, a 212 GB download you cannot open and nine days of waiting. Rockstar is betting that is exactly what its audience wants.
