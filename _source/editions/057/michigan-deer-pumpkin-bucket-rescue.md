The deer was first spotted in Livonia, Michigan, near Detroit, with a bright-orange plastic Halloween pumpkin bucket covering its nose and mouth, preventing it from eating or drinking. The Detroit Animal Welfare Group (DAWG), a nonprofit based in Romeo, Michigan, searched for it for 10 days, with metro Detroit residents sharing sightings on social media. [1][2]

The rescue ended on Sunday, November 8, 2026, in Hines Park, Wayne County — a 2,300-acre park the deer had reached after travelling from Livonia. It was reported November 9 by the Detroit Free Press and the New York Post. [1][2]

Rescuers were about to leave for the day when a final sighting came in, as darkness was setting in and darting in the dark was not ideal. They waded through a 20-foot-wide river four times — in thigh-deep, freezing-cold water — and climbed tall muddy banks and through fallen branches and thick brush before they could safely dart the deer. [1][3]

The animal was first thought to be a doe; up close it proved to be a young button buck, a young male. It was moderately dehydrated, was given subcutaneous fluids, and had its tranquilizer reversed before being released back into the wild. The Michigan Department of Natural Resources permitted DAWG to dart the deer, and the buck was given a legally required ear tag because of the tranquilizing medication. The tag reads "Do Not Eat Until 12/8/26" — a human-safety warning should the deer later be hunted for food. [1][2][3]

After being freed, the buck paused for a few minutes to groom himself and look back before walking away, according to DAWG — the group said he paused "for a few minutes to groom himself and look back to say thank you!" [1]

"Happy tears! We will finally get some sleep tonight even though we are cold, wet and smell like swamp. We want to thank everybody who came out to search for the last 10 days," DAWG said after the rescue. [2]
