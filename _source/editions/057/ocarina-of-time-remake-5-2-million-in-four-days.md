Some stories get told once and forgotten. Others get told again every generation, a little polished each time, and the crowd still turns up. Nintendo learned this week which kind Ocarina of Time is.

Nintendo said on Wednesday, November 11, that The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for Nintendo Switch 2 sold more than 5.2 million units worldwide in its first four days after launching on November 5. That figure includes 810,000 copies in Japan and counts both physical and digital sales. Nintendo says it is the highest four-day global total for any Nintendo-published Switch 2 game. [1]

The game is a full remake of the 1998 Nintendo 64 original. Link still travels between two eras, as Young Link and Adult Link, using different abilities and items in each. Nintendo says the Switch 2 version has reimagined visuals and better playability "while preserving the experience of the original game." [1]

Read the claim carefully. VGC points out that Nintendo can only call it the fastest-selling first-party Switch 2 game, because not every third-party publisher shares per-console sales. It is likely the fastest-selling Switch 2 game overall, but Nintendo can't prove that. VGC also notes that chart trackers will report lower numbers, since Nintendo doesn't share digital data with them. [2]

The comparisons are still striking. In four days, the remake passed the lifetime totals VGC lists for Echoes of Wisdom (4.09 million after seven months), A Link Between Worlds (4.26 million), Phantom Hourglass (4.13 million), and Spirit Tracks (2.61 million). It has a long climb before it reaches Breath of the Wild at 35.76 million or Tears of the Kingdom at 22.71 million. [2]

The timing helps. Variety notes the launch is part of Zelda's 40th anniversary, marking the first game's 1986 release in Japan, with a worldwide concert tour beginning in 2027 and a live-action Zelda film due in theaters on April 30, 2027. Variety also says the remake was among the year's most anticipated releases, second only to Grand Theft Auto VI. [3]

There is a lesson here for anyone who thinks the past is finished. A 28-year-old adventure, retold with care, just outsold most of its own family's new chapters in four days. The old songs still draw a crowd when the singer knows the words.
