Dumont Brothers Diner had its grand opening ceremony on November 9, welcoming hundreds of people, from regulars to the borough's mayor and council members [1]. The diner, at 45 W. Madison Ave. in Dumont, New Jersey, took over the location long known as the Dumont Crystal Diner, billed as the oldest diner in Bergen County, serving customers since 1925 [1].

Brothers Gus and John Grimanis bought the building earlier in 2026 when they got word the diner was going to close [1]. A local history write-up records that the brothers purchased it in March 2026 after discovering it was advertised for sale on Facebook Marketplace [2]. "We want to stay here another 100 years," Gus Grimanis told NorthJersey.com [1]. "We wanted to step in and give it more life," he said [1].

The brothers, originally from the Greek island of Andros, have over 40 years of experience owning and operating diners throughout New Jersey, previously in Totowa, Paterson and West Orange, and first entered the restaurant business with their father [1]. The vintage feel stayed: the same memorabilia and photographs, with fresh paint and repairs [1]. The menu added Greek family recipes — homemade spanakopita, chicken souvlaki, homemade keftedes, and kolokithokeftedes — alongside New Jersey staples like the Dumont Stack, double Taylor ham, four eggs and double cheese [1]. The diner is open Monday through Sunday, 6 a.m. to 4 p.m. [1].

"Although we are new to Dumont, we already feel at home here," the owners wrote on Facebook, thanking customers and residents for the overwhelming support [1].

Print the age honestly: the 1925 date is how the diner is billed, and this paper prints it as billed, not settled. Diner historian Michael C. Gabriele has questioned the 1925 date, placing the existing diner car around 1932–1933 [2]. The reopening, the brothers, and the town's turnout are not in dispute.

No agency saved this diner. No grant, no program. Two brothers saw a closing notice, bought the building, kept the photographs on the wall, and put their family's recipes next to the Taylor ham. A town showed up in the hundreds. That is how a place survives a hundred years — somebody decides it should.

Belmont Café follow-up: The Belmont Café in Waymart, Pennsylvania — closed since a car crashed into the building on Halloween, and printed in Edition 67 as planned to reopen Tuesday, November 24 — is back in business. WNEP reported Tuesday that the café has reopened after the Halloween crash [3][4]. This paper prints the reopening as confirmed on November 24; the 6 a.m. start was the announced plan [5].
