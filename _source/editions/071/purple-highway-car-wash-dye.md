For several days, drivers shared photos and videos of a mysterious purple substance on highways across the region — Route 287 and Route 23 in Morris and Passaic counties, New Jersey, and the New York State Thruway in Rockland and Orange counties, New York [1]. The stain was also seen along Interstate 287 and 78 in New Jersey and into Pennsylvania [2]. New Jersey transportation officials received the first notifications on Saturday, November 21 [2].

The guesses ran from beet juice mixed with salt for snow prevention to potassium permanganate, ink, or diesel fuel [1]. One speculation, per local reporting, involved Barney the dinosaur having digestion issues [2].

Officials found the source. A tractor-trailer owned by freight company XPO Logistics leaked a "water-soluble organic car wash dye" on several roadways in New Jersey and New York, the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation reported [1]. The substance is not hazardous, and there is no threat to the public or the environment [1]. County hazmat units in New Jersey tested it and identified it as sorbitan monopalmitate, a compound used in food products, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals [1][2].

"DEC has been in contact with the New York State Thruway Authority and will respond if assistance is requested with any cleanup efforts," the department said [1]. In New Jersey, because there are no safety concerns, the transportation agency does not plan to clean the highway [1][2]. Motorists who encountered the substance may want to wash their vehicle, officials said [1].

So the mystery ends the way the best ones do: not aliens, not a chemical attack — a truck leaking car wash dye across three states, and two state governments agreeing the correct response is to let the weather handle it. The purple line will fade on its own schedule. The drivers who followed it for miles will be telling the story longer than that.
