Grand Theft Auto VI launched November 19 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and the live-service giants felt it immediately. Alinea Analytics estimates Call of Duty HQ's daily active users fell 42% on launch day, from an average of 7.5 million to 4.4 million across PlayStation, Xbox, and Steam, with PlayStation down 44% [1][2]. Four days later, Call of Duty was still 41% below its baseline [1].

Fortnite fell from 17.8 million to 12.8 million daily players on PlayStation and Xbox, a 28% decline, but recovered to 19% below its usual level by the end of the weekend [1]. EA Sports FC 27 fell about 27%, from 5.9 million to 4.3 million, before getting back to within 20% of baseline [1]. Call of Duty's slow recovery stands out because it had less audience overlap with GTA 6 than Fortnite did: 29% of people who played GTA 6 by the end of launch weekend had also played Fortnite that month, compared with 16% for Call of Duty HQ [1].

"All three [games] saw noticeable drops in DAUs (daily active users) as soon as GTA dropped," Alinea's Rhys Elliott wrote. "Call of Duty got it the worst of the three, dropping 42% from an average of 7.5 [million] DAUs to 4.4 [million]" [2]. Elliott added that Call of Duty has "barely bounced back after four days. It's still down 41% from its baseline" [2].

This is not a case of a publisher hiding weak sales behind reach. Take-Two Interactive reported on November 20 that GTA VI had pre-sold 32.5 million units and generated $3.79 billion on launch day [2]. Alinea's higher figure — 44.9 million copies and more than $4 billion in four days — is an analyst estimate beyond Take-Two's own numbers, and this paper prints it as an estimate [1][2].

The player behavior is measured, not spun. Circana's Mat Piscatella reported that 61% of active US PlayStation 5 players and 56% of global Xbox players engaged with GTA VI on launch day [2]. Average launch-day playtime was 513 minutes on PS5 and 494 minutes on Xbox — over eight hours [2]. Alinea counted 40.8 million people playing GTA VI on Sunday, November 22 alone [2]. The competition came from a single-player campaign, not a rival live service [1].

Print the caveats with the numbers: Alinea's figures are third-party estimates, not official counts from Activision, Sony, or Microsoft, and the combined Call of Duty figure includes Steam, where GTA 6 is not available [1]. What the estimates describe is still plain: when a game people actually want to play arrives, the daily grind loses. Call of Duty's players left fastest and came back slowest. That is the atmosphere no press release can fake.
