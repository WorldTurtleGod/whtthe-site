New York City has rats, steam and subway preachers. It also, apparently, has a sewer crew with a getaway driver.

Just before 5 a.m. on Wednesday, November 18, a witness saw five people climb out of the city sewer system through a manhole at Second Avenue and East 17th Street in Gramercy Park, according to the NYPD. [1][2] The five got into a red sedan driven by a sixth person, and all six drove west on East 17th Street. No one was hurt. [1][2] Police told 1010 WINS the group is wanted for criminal trespassing. [3]

The NYPD released photos of all six this week. ABC7 and Gothamist say the photos came out Tuesday, while 1010 WINS says Wednesday. [1][2][3] According to Gothamist, the photos show two women and four men, most carrying tools, sledgehammers among them. [2] Newsweek, citing the department, described headlamps, shovels and work gear, and said the driver was a woman. [4] There have been no arrests. [4]

**It keeps happening.** Gothamist reports that the manhole sightings have "gripped the five boroughs since at least last May," when two groups were spotted climbing out of manholes in different parts of Brooklyn. [2] Newsweek's tally includes three people in hip waders entering a sewer in Astoria, Queens, around 2 a.m. on May 5. It also counts about seven people who went down a manhole in Gravesend, Brooklyn, and came back up about three hours later, and a crew in overalls and headlamps filmed coming out at West 31st Street and Seventh Avenue near Penn Station in August. [4] In September, during the U.N. General Assembly, two men were arrested on the Upper East Side and charged with trespassing, according to Gothamist. [2] In October, police arrested five more men who allegedly tampered with manholes in Inwood. [2]

**The motive is gold, or at least the hope of it.** "We go into the sewer and look for valuables that get stuck in the storm drains and sewers, gold, anything of value," Samuel Reveron, 25, one of the two men arrested in September, told police, according to the New York Post as cited by Newsweek. [4] In a September statement, the NYPD said, "We have seen a number of incidents over the past few months of individuals going into the sewer system to look for valuables and other scrap metal after a storm." [4] Whether the Gramercy crew was after the same thing is unknown. Gothamist reports police say the group's motives are "still unclear." [2]

The city's answer is: don't. Rob Wolejsza, a spokesperson for the Department of Environmental Protection, has warned that entering the sewers is illegal and "extremely dangerous." "Sewers can contain numerous hazards, including noxious and potentially deadly gases, unstable surfaces, flooding risks, and confined spaces," he said. [4] The danger is real. A 56-year-old woman died last spring after falling into an open Con Edison manhole in Midtown, Gothamist reported. [2]

Anyone who recognizes the six can call NYPD Crime Stoppers at 1-800-577-TIPS (8477). [1][3]

The "mole people" nickname has stuck, but none of the cases in these reports involve anyone living underground. They describe crews who go down in the dark with lights and tools, come back up before dawn, and drive away. [2][4]
