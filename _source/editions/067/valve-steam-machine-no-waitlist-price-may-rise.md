For five months, buying a Steam Machine meant entering a lottery. As of this week, the 512GB model is a normal product again: click, pay, ship. The bigger model went the other way.

In an update posted Thursday, Valve said every reservation holder for the 512GB Steam Machine has been served and the unit is now in "add-to-cart" status, meaning anyone can buy one directly from the Steam store while stock lasts. [1][2] That keeps a promise Valve made in July, that everyone in the reservation queue would get a chance to buy by the end of 2026. [2]

The 2TB model is a different story. Valve said purchase invitations have gone to all remaining 2TB reservation holders, but people are still on the waitlist, and no one new may join it. [1][2] Those already waiting keep their spot. Valve invited them to buy a 512GB unit instead, but PC Gamer reported that doing so forfeits their place in the 2TB line. [1] Valve said it expects to share more on 2TB supply in January. [1][2]

The reason is the same one Valve gave at launch: parts. "We are continuing to build more Steam Machine units, but memory and storage costs continue to increase at unprecedented levels," Valve wrote. "As a result we don't yet know the number we are able to make, and at what price we'll be able to offer them. The 2TB model in particular will be especially constrained." [1][2] Valve also said that even when it can build more 2TB units, it will be "at smaller quantities than we planned." [1]

That sentence about price is the one buyers should read twice. Valve isn't announcing an increase. It is telling customers, in writing, that it can't rule one out. Ars Technica noted that the Steam Machine already shipped in June at a higher price and in smaller numbers than first planned, for the same reason. [2]

Valve has a weaker hand than its rivals here. Analysts quoted earlier this year by Ars said Valve likely lacks the supplier priority of Nintendo, Sony or Microsoft, and that the Steam Machine is "a niche product" that can't win the same procurement deals. [2] Valve hasn't published sales totals. Ars cited estimates from Steam's public charts of 12,000 to 15,000 units a week in July, against a PS5 average above 300,000 a week worldwide. [2] Those are estimates, not company numbers.

Storage matters more than it used to. PC Gamer pointed out that big games now commonly take more than 100GB, and one upcoming release asks for 300GB of free space just to pre-install. [1] On a 512GB box, that's a real limit.

**Commentary.** This is what an honest shortage notice looks like. Valve didn't hide behind "unprecedented demand." It said plainly that it doesn't know how many it can build or what it will charge. If you want the 512GB model at today's price, today is the only price anyone is promising.
