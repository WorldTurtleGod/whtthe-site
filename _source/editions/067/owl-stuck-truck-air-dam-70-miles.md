Most animals that meet a tractor-trailer at highway speed don't get a second act. This owl got a ride.

A truck driver for Old Dominion Freight Line hit a great horned owl on a Pennsylvania highway and felt the collision, but didn't know the bird had lodged headfirst in the truck's fiberglass air dam, the panel under the front bumper. [1][2] She kept driving to the company's terminal in Carlisle. When the maintenance crew found the owl there, it was alive. [1]

Workers freed the bird, which tried to fly and couldn't. [1] The company called Raven Ridge Wildlife Center in Washington Boro, which stayed open past closing to take it in after volunteers drove it over. [1][2]

"When the owl arrived, its feathers were a mess, but there were no broken wings or legs, no open wounds—just damage to its left eye," the center wrote on Facebook. "We could hardly believe it." [1] A vet exam found blood and swelling in that eye, Popular Science reported. [2]

Then came the distance. The driver had noted the mile marker where she hit the bird, and the center said the spot was 70 miles from the terminal. "The owl was stuck headfirst in the fiberglass air dam for 70 miles at 65 miles per hour," Raven Ridge wrote. "It's truly the luckiest owl we've ever seen." [1][2]

Not every account matches. The Washington Post, which named the driver as Ashley Moffitt, reported a 45-mile drive and described the owl's head as trapped above the windshield. [3] CBS Pittsburgh and Popular Science, both quoting the wildlife center, give 70 miles and the air dam. [1][2] We couldn't resolve the difference; the center's 70-mile figure is the one it attributes to the driver's own mile-marker note. [1]

"This is a very unusual case, as most animals that are hit by a tractor-trailer die due to their injuries or are killed instantly," center director Tracie Young told Popular Science. [2] It isn't Raven Ridge's first such patient: Popular Science reported that in March, the same center treated a bobcat that needed four hours of surgery on two broken legs after its head got stuck in a car's grille. [2]

The owl is healing. "The owl is doing great, and the left eye that sustained the damage is also healing," Young said. [2] The plan is to move it to an outdoor flight cage, confirm it can fly and find food, then drive it back and release it near where it was hit. [1][2]

That return trip, at least, it will make inside the vehicle.
