President Donald Trump announced a new tax policy on Friday. By his own account, he hadn't told his own staff first.

At a White House press briefing, back from a summit in China, Trump said U.S. companies that build artificial intelligence data centers abroad would face "a tremendous tax." [1][2][3] "They can, but we're going to put a tremendous tax on them," he said of firms that keep building overseas. [1] CNBC reported that he framed it as companies being blocked from building abroad, and that the announcement appeared to surprise his own staff. [1] "I don't know if that's a breaking story," Trump said. "I haven't even said that to my people. But I'm telling them now they're here." [1]

He gave no rate, no legal basis, no enforcement plan and no date. [2] Axios noted that it's unclear how he would carry it out, because Congress holds the constitutional power to impose federal taxes, and Trump didn't say whether he would ask Congress or try to use existing executive powers. [2]

His stated reason was China. "We're not allowing data centers to go into other countries because China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen," Trump said, according to Axios. [2] In the same briefing he said Chinese leader Xi Jinping had "congratulated me on how well we're doing" in AI. [1] Trump met Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin in China the day before. [1]

The companies in the crosshairs already have plenty of overseas sites. CNBC listed Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Oracle as firms with data centers in other countries. [1] Axios' Ina Fried pointed out that companies often need servers abroad for reliability, speed and to obey laws in other countries that require data to be processed locally. [2] Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Oracle did not immediately respond to CNBC. [1]

At home, the public isn't buying more data centers. CNBC's own survey found 64% of registered voters opposed an AI data center within 10 miles of their home, and 13% supported one. [1] Axios, citing Ballotpedia, reported that voters favored limiting data center development in 52 of 53 ballot measures across 13 states this year. [2] Trump's pitch to those voters was blunt: companies "have plenty of money," he said. "Take it." [2]

**Commentary.** Article I, Section 8 starts with the power "to lay and collect Taxes," and it hands that power to Congress, not the president. Madison called the purse "the most complete and effectual weapon" the people's representatives have, in Federalist No. 58. A tax announced at a podium before the Treasury has even heard of it isn't policy. It is a threat aimed at private companies, and it should worry anyone who builds in friendly places like Japan, Korea or Thailand as much as anyone who builds near Beijing. If the White House wants this tax, it can send Congress a bill and let the people's representatives vote on it in public.
