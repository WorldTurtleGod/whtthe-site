Nobody has ever proved Sasquatch is real. Next year, you will be able to stick him on an envelope anyway.

The U.S. Postal Service has previewed the stamps it plans to release in 2027, and tucked among the flags, flowers and a Willie Mays tribute is a set called Mythical Creatures. It depicts five creatures of American folklore: Champ, the Jackalope, Mothman, Sasquatch and the Squonk. [1] The artwork is by Nat Power, who drew the creatures digitally, and the set was designed by USPS art director Antonio Alcalá. [1][2] The Postal Service announced the lineup on Nov. 6 and says the designs are preliminary and could change. [1]

Most of the roster needs no introduction. Mothman is celebrated at festivals in his home state of West Virginia, where fans dress in black wings and red sunglasses. Champ is Lake Champlain's answer to Nessie. [2]

The Squonk is the deep cut. CBS Pittsburgh reports it first appeared in lumbermen's legends in the early 1900s. By the lore, it lives in Pennsylvania's forests and is so ashamed of its wrinkled, warty skin that it weeps constantly, and if someone discovers it, it dissolves into a puddle of bubbles and tears. The Postal Service's own description: "today it's a beloved symbol of anxiety." [2]

That gives Appalachia two of the five creatures, by CBS Pittsburgh's count, and puts the Squonk in the same stamp album as another western Pennsylvania icon, Fred Rogers, whose stamp first came out in 2018. [2]

The cryptids are not the only fantastical animals on the 2027 list. A separate Unicorns set offers four designs of the legendary beasts. [1] The full lineup also includes a Pat Summitt tribute, a Year of the Goat stamp, close-up photographs of vegetables, and an E Pluribus Unum stamp built from red and blue dots in the shape of the Statue of Liberty. [1]

**Commentary.** A country's stamps are a kind of family album, a record of what it chooses to put on every envelope. It says something cheerful about the national mood that, next to the founding motto, the album now has room for a shy, sobbing forest creature that only wants to be left alone. Plenty of Americans can relate.
