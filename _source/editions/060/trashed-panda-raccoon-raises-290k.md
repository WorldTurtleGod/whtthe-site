The greatest fundraiser in Hanover County animal shelter history has four legs, no liver left to speak of, and no idea any of this happened.

The backstory, from Nov. 29, 2025: a raccoon dropped through a ceiling tile into an ABC liquor store in Ashland, Virginia, in the small hours after Black Friday. He sampled rum, scotch, moonshine and eggnog, broke 14 bottles worth under $250, and was found passed out beside the employee toilet. Officers took him to Hanover County Animal Protection and Shelter to sleep it off, and he was released back into the wild with, presumably, a story he cannot tell. [1][2]

The photo of him splayed on that bathroom floor went around the world. The shelter, with apparel company Bonfire, put his face on merchandise as the "Trashed Panda." This week the final numbers landed: the Washington Post reported, and the New York Post and Dexerto confirmed on Nov. 12–13, that the raccoon merch has raised more than $290,000. [1][2][3]

Context makes that number ridiculous. The shelter's typical yearly donations run under $20,000. One drunk raccoon raised more than fourteen years' worth. [1]

And the money is going to work, not a statue — though he has earned a statue. Hanover County officials voted unanimously on Oct. 28 to fund body cameras for the department's eight animal protection officers, starting in January, with raccoon money covering roughly $39,000 of the five-year program. The rest is paying for new animal housing, toys, a temperature-controlled food shed and a community pet pantry, with about $140,000 still banked and an emergency fund for injured animals under consideration. Virginia ABC named three cocktails after him, including a Trash Panda Old Fashioned. Saturday Night Live did a sketch. The merch line itself was retired Nov. 3 — he is, officially, a limited edition. [1][2]

"We never thought a raccoon would do this for us," said Jeffrey Parker, the county's chief of animal protection. Board supervisor Faye Prichard put it better at the Oct. 28 meeting: "I don't know when we've owed a debt of gratitude to any animal as much as we do to that raccoon." [1][3]

Parker's last word on his benefactor is the right one: "Hopefully he's living a good life somewhere — and is being sober." [1]

One honest footnote: the $290,000 figure comes from the Washington Post's reporting and has been repeated by every outlet since; it has not been independently audited against the shelter's books. But the county is spending real raccoon money on real cameras, and that much is on the record. [1][2]
