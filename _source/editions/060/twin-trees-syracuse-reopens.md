On Sunday night, Nov. 8, longtime customers packed the original Twin Trees restaurant at 1100 Avery Ave. in Syracuse for what everyone believed was the last service in its 69-year history. The coolers needed fixing, the boiler needed fixing, the money was running out, and Don Rescignano — who had stepped in two years ago when his father, the owner, now 81, fell ill — had decided to close. [1][2]

Then the neighborhood answered. "With all the well wishes and everything last week, I had to give it one more chance," Rescignano said. "I just don't want to go out that easy, and I don't want to not try." On Monday, at friends' urging, he asked Onondaga County for help. [1]

On Friday, Nov. 13, County Executive Ryan McMahon stood in the restaurant and announced a $25,000 grant from the county's hospitality and tourism fund — supported by the room occupancy tax, no repayment required — for marketing and capital improvements. Twin Trees reopened the same day at 4 p.m. [1]

McMahon did not announce it like a bureaucrat. He told the room he had taken a date to Twin Trees before a ninth-grade Sadie Hawkins dance. "So many people have a story like that, or multiple stories like that of what this venue means to us," he said. "We need this anchor to stay here and serve this community." [1]

Be clear-eyed about what this is and is not. Twenty-five thousand dollars is a bridge, not a rescue. The restaurant comes back smaller: Thursday through Sunday only, a limited menu with "the favorites" kept, hours of 4 to 9 p.m. Thursdays, 4 to 10 Fridays and Saturdays, 4 to 8 Sundays. Rescignano already lost two cooks and a bartender in the five days the doors were shut and is hiring back who he can. The dwindling customer base that helped kill it is still dwindling until people actually show up — which is why part of the money goes to marketing aimed at younger customers who never built the habit. [1]

Rescignano said the quiet part out loud: "There (are) no guarantees. We need our customers. We need you greatly, and this is the only way this place will survive." [1]

That is the whole economy of the common man's culture, in one pizza joint. No grant keeps a 69-year-old restaurant alive. Neighbors do — the ones who filled it on its last night, told the owner not to quit, and now have to fill it on ordinary Thursdays. Brothers Don and Joe Rescignano and Albert "Shuffles" Palermo opened Twin Trees in 1957. It survived 69 years on Syracuse's West Side because Syracuse kept coming. On Friday, Syracuse came again. "It's been a very tough few weeks," Rescignano said, "but we've got to give it a second chance. I'm not ready to walk away." [1]
