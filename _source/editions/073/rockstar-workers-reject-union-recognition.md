The people who built Grand Theft Auto VI had a vote on whether their union gets a seat at the table. They said no, and by a lot.

Rockstar Games' UK staff voted 75% to 25% against a recognition agreement with the IWGB Game Workers Union, the union said on Thursday, November 26. [1][2][3] Polygon reported that 1,544 employees took part, a 93.2% turnout, across Rockstar's offices in Edinburgh, Leeds, Lincoln, Dundee and London. [3] Vertical Slice reported the ballot was open to about 1,600 employees below director level. [4] Voting ran from November 12 to November 26, which put it right on top of GTA VI's launch. [1][4]

**What a "no" does and doesn't do.** In Britain, workers don't need the boss's permission to join a union, which is why the Rockstar Game Workers Union, part of the IWGB, already exists. [4] Recognition is the formal step: it would have committed management to regular meetings and a bargaining framework over pay, overtime, bonuses, leave and pensions, plus consultation rights over layoffs and generative AI in the workplace. [1][4] Without it, the union "does not prevent Rockstar workers from organising with the IWGB or exercising their trade union rights, but means management will not be formally committed to regular meetings with union representatives," the IWGB said, according to Vertical Slice. [4]

**One wrinkle in the numbers.** The outlets don't describe the 25% the same way. The Verge called it "25 percent of votes," while Vertical Slice reported the IWGB's figure as 25% "of eligible workers." [1][4] With turnout above 93%, the gap is small, but it's not the same thing.

**Both sides' statements.** Rockstar head of publishing Jennifer Kolbe, in a statement The Verge said she gave to Bloomberg News: "This result reflects our team's strong support for continuing the direct relationships, collaboration and open dialogue that have helped make Rockstar Games so innovative and strong." [1][3] The union struck a defiant note: "For anyone at Rockstar who believes in a fairer workplace, a fairer sector and a fairer society - this isn't a sign to retreat, it's a sign to double down on what we stand for." [1] It added that "hundreds of our coworkers voted in favour." [1][2]

**The fight that isn't over.** Last year Rockstar fired 31 workers who were in a private union Discord channel. The IWGB called it union busting; Rockstar says they were let go for leaking confidential information about upcoming games. [2][3] That case is before a UK employment tribunal. PC Gamer reported closing submissions were made last week, and a judgment is pending. [2][3]

The vote lands days after GTA VI's record-breaking opening, which NBC News put at $3.79 billion. [5] Rockstar's workers just watched the company prove it can ship the biggest game ever made. Most of them decided they would rather keep dealing with management directly.

**Commentary.** This is how it should work. Workers held a secret ballot, nearly all of them showed up, and the result binds nobody's freedom: the union can keep organizing, and anyone who wants to join can. No government agency forced the outcome either way. The open question is the tribunal. If Rockstar fired 31 people for organizing, that is a real wrong and should be punished. If they leaked unreleased game details, that is a fireable offense anywhere. A judge will decide which, on the evidence, and that is exactly where it belongs.
