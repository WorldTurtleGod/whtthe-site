In October 2025, the Attorney General of the United States went on television and said, more or less, that she had called Apple and Apple had done what she asked. On Thursday, the government agreed that it will not do that again.

U.S. District Judge Jorge L. Alonso of the Northern District of Illinois signed an order on November 13 that turns an earlier preliminary injunction into a permanent one. It says federal officials, and anyone acting for them, "shall take no actions, formal or informal, to demand or pressure" Facebook to suppress the "ICE Sightings – Chicagoland" group, or pressure Apple over the Eyes Up app. [1][2][3]

The case, first filed as Rosado v. Bondi and now called Rosado v. Blanche after the original defendants left office, was brought in February by the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression on behalf of two Midwesterners. [1][3] Kae Rosado, who runs a jewelry business, started the Facebook group in January 2025. It grew to nearly 100,000 members during the federal enforcement surge in Chicago known as Operation Midway Blitz. [1] Mark Hodges built Eyes Up, an app that gathers and maps videos of ICE operations after he and his moderators review them. [1]

According to Reclaim The Net's account of the case, Apple pulled Eyes Up around October 2, 2025, the same day then-Attorney General Pam Bondi told Fox News her office had "reached out to Apple today demanding they remove the ICEBlock app from their App Store – and Apple did so." Bondi and then-Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem also publicly took credit for Facebook's action. [3] In April, Judge Alonso found the companies "changed their positions and removed the content immediately after Defendants contacted them about it," and called the officials' public statements "intimations of a threat." [3]

The government did not fight to the end. The plaintiffs and the government jointly asked the court on November 12 to make the injunction permanent and dismiss the case with prejudice, and the judge granted it the next day. [3] Reuters reported that the Justice Department and DHS did not immediately respond to requests for comment. [2]

Two limits are worth knowing. The order bars officials from leaning on the platforms, but Apple and Facebook are not required to restore the group or the app. And because the government settled, there is no appeals ruling to guide the next case. [3]

"It shouldn't take a lawsuit to get the government to respect our constitutional rights," Hodges said. [1]

**Commentary.** Publius warned in Federalist No. 51 that you must first enable the government to control the governed, "and in the next place oblige it to control itself." [4] The First Amendment is one of those obligations, and it does not lose force because the censor uses a phone call instead of a statute. What made this case winnable is that the officials bragged. A free people should not have to count on that. Whatever you think of watching ICE agents, the principle cuts both ways: the next administration will have its own list of apps it dislikes.
