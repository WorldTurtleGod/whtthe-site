Somebody has to know where every veteran in town is buried. In Belchertown, Massachusetts, for nearly three decades, the answer has been Vincent Viglione and Kathleen Wood.

At the Veterans Day ceremony on Wednesday, Nov. 11, the husband-and-wife team was named Belchertown's 2026 Hometown Heroes. The reporting ran Nov. 12 and was updated Nov. 13 — so frame this as Veterans Week, not breaking news: a town honouring the couple who honour everyone else. [1][2]

Both served in the U.S. Air Force Reserve, and both stayed long enough to retire as Chief Master Sergeants — E-9, the highest enlisted grade there is. Then they came home and took on the unglamorous work. Viglione has been Belchertown's Veterans Graves Officer for 27 years; Wood has assisted him for 25. For nearly 30 years they have ensured American flags are placed on veterans' graves across all six of the town's municipal cemeteries every Memorial Day. They built and maintain a database of veterans' graves and keep the maps current so every veteran is properly marked. And they secured funding from local organisations and the Community Preservation Committee to repair and restore more than 500 veterans' gravestones, some dating back to the Revolutionary War. Both help organise the town's military holiday celebrations; Wood often speaks at veterans events; Viglione has served on the Veterans Services Advisory Board since its creation. [1][2]

Retired Air Force Major Keith Poulin, in his nomination letter, refused to split the award in two: "These two, husband and wife, have together served our nation and our community of Belchertown in an extraordinary manner. It would be impossible to separate their contributions. They are a team in every aspect of their lives and the community of Belchertown has been the beneficiary of their service in honoring all veterans." [1]

Dustin Ruby, the town's Director of Veterans' Services, has only held the job a few months. It was enough: "I've only been in this role a few months, but it didn't take long to see that Vince and Kathy are the heart of how Belchertown honors its veterans... Every flag on every grave is there because they care, and our community is better for it." The ellipsis is in the source exactly as published — the words on either side of it are his. [1]

This is what common-man culture actually looks like when nobody is branding it. No audience, no monetisation, no content. A database kept current. Maps kept accurate. Five hundred stones stood back up, some of them older than the republic's second century. A flag on every grave, every year, because two enlisted people who already gave a career to the country decided the work at home was not finished either. Belchertown did not discover them this week. It just finally said, in public, what every flag in six cemeteries already said.
