Fiona the hippo survived being born six weeks early at 29 pounds. Now she has to survive a knit cap.

The Cincinnati Zoo said this week that Fiona swallowed a hat that a visitor accidentally sent into her habitat. According to WLWT, the zoo said a child in front of the habitat tossed the knit cap, which "was caught by a fan and catapulted through a 1-foot gap above the 12-foot-tall viewing window." [1] A zoo spokesperson told FOX19 that the fan "catapulted" the hat through the opening, and added: "We probably couldn't make that happen again if we tried. It was clearly an accident." [2] Axios identified the spokesperson as Michelle Curley. [3]

The zoo first announced the problem on Monday, Nov. 16, saying it had happened more than a week earlier. [1]

**What the vets have tried.** Fiona got fluids and an enema to help move the hat along, Axios reported. [3] WLWT reported that her appetite has dropped and that she has received rectal fluids while the zoo consults specialists. [1] The Columbus Zoo came down to help with a colonoscopy, but the scope found nothing. [2]

"The scope's about nine feet long, and we weren't able to reach any farther than that," Dr. Mike Wenninger, the zoo's director of animal health, told WLWT. "Ultrasound today did show that there is some bunching of intestines, so we do think she's passing something through her small intestine. And at this point, what we can do is supportive care and hope to help support her through passing it." [1] She is getting pain medicine and anti-nausea medicine. [1]

Surgery is not an option, the zoo said, which means Fiona has to pass the hat on her own. [1][2]

**Where things stand.** In its Tuesday, Nov. 17, update, the zoo said Fiona is getting 24-hour supportive care. Her care team planned to regroup Wednesday, Nov. 18, and said another procedure might be needed. "Her care team is cautiously optimistic that she will pass it and return to her usual self," the zoo wrote. [2] She is off exhibit at Hippo Cove until it's resolved. [1][2]

The zoo is asking visitors to hold on to their stuff. "Please help us keep our animals safe by securing your belongings and taking extra care near habitats," it said. "If something falls into a habitat, please notify a Zoo staff member immediately." [2]

**Why the internet cares.** Fiona was born on Jan. 24, 2017, to a 17-year-old hippo named Bibi. She was the first Nile hippo born at the Cincinnati Zoo in 75 years. She arrived about six weeks early, before her expected March due date, weighing 29 pounds, about 25 pounds lighter than the lowest recorded birth weight for her species, and was too weak to stand and nurse. [4] Zoo staff nursed her through it, and the premature calf became one of the best-known animals in the country.

Nine years later, she is a full-size hippo. A grown hippo cannot be cut open to remove a hat, so for now the job falls to Fiona's gut, a lot of fluids and a care team watching her around the clock.
