The first critics to see "The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth" posted their reactions after the film was shown to press, and Variety rounded them up late Monday, Nov. 16. The verdict was close to unanimous. [1]

The Wrap's Drew Taylor called it "an absolute triumph." Variety's own Clayton Davis called it "a wild, hilarious hangout movie" and wrote that Brad Pitt "gives his best performance since 'Once Upon a Time in…Hollywood.'" Critic Simon Thompson wrote that it "pops, throbs and crackles." [1]

The film is directed by David Fincher from a screenplay by Quentin Tarantino. Pitt returns as stuntman Cliff Booth, the role that won him the Oscar for best supporting actor. The story is set in 1977, eight years after the first film, with Cliff working as a studio fixer who gets in over his head. Elizabeth Debicki, Scott Caan, Yahya Abdul-Mateen II, Carla Gugino and Peter Weller co-star. [1] According to Variety, Fincher told Empire that Tarantino's first draft ran 230 pages and that he cut 60. Tarantino did not direct it because he plans to direct only one more feature before he retires. [1]

**The fight over the screens.** Netflix is opening the film exclusively in IMAX on Nov. 25, according to Variety (Deadline reports Nov. 29), then putting it on the streaming service on Dec. 23. [1][2][4] That short window cost it the No. 2 and No. 3 U.S. chains, as Deadline described them. [4] Variety reported on Oct. 30 that Regal and Cinemark declined to show the film on their IMAX screens. Those chains are giving their premium screens to Lionsgate's "The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping," which opens Nov. 20. [2]

Regal's Brooks LeBoeuf said premium screens are among the chain's "most valuable assets" and that "shortened theatrical windows have a direct impact on those decisions." [2] AMC, the country's biggest chain, is showing it. [4] Variety put the release at more than 500 screens worldwide, including AMC's roughly 200 IMAX screens in the U.S. [2] What's on Netflix counted 523 venues on the film's official map as of Nov. 2, with AMC accounting for 187. The site also counted 13 Aeon Cinema venues and 12 TOHO Cinemas venues in Japan, and found no listed venues in South Korea, Australia, France or India. [3]

IMAX CEO Rich Gelfond took the studio's side on his company's earnings call. "Frankly, I don't understand why anybody wouldn't play it," he said, according to Deadline. He argued that a big chain could run "Hunger Games" on many screens and "Cliff Booth" on its IMAX screens, and said some exhibitors were "standing on ceremony" over the Netflix window. [4]

Pitt won his Oscar for the 2019 original, which Variety notes earned $410 million worldwide and 10 Oscar nominations. [1] This time, the box office test is smaller and stranger: two weeks, one format, and a large part of the country's theaters sitting it out.

**Commentary.** This is how a market is supposed to work, and nobody should ask Washington to referee it. Netflix wants a short window. Regal and Cinemark think that hurts them and said no. AMC thinks it can make money and said yes. Ticket buyers will settle it with their wallets over Thanksgiving weekend. If a well-reviewed Fincher-Tarantino film sells out IMAX screens for two weeks, the chains that passed will have learned something. If it doesn't, Netflix will. Either way, nobody needs a law, a hearing or a regulator to decide which movie plays on which screen.
