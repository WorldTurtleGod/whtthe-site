The most famous leak in video game history now has a guilty plea attached to it, and the timing is almost too neat: Grand Theft Auto VI goes on sale this Thursday.

On Monday, Nov. 16, Arion Kurtaj, 21, formerly of Oxfordshire, pleaded guilty at Woolwich Crown Court to a string of hacks on Rockstar Games, BT/EE, Revolut and Uber. The Crown Prosecution Service says he will be sentenced at the same court on Nov. 23. [1][2]

The Rockstar hack is the one players remember. In 2022, Kurtaj took 90 gameplay videos from the unfinished GTA VI and posted them online under the name TeaPotUberHacker, after trying to blackmail the company. The BBC reports the clips showed nearly 60 minutes of gameplay. He then got into Rockstar's internal Slack and wrote: "If Rockstar does not contact me on Telegram within 24 hours I will start releasing the source code." Rockstar refused. Rockstar said recovery cost it $5 million plus thousands of hours of staff time, and that the leak blew a secret it was guarding for marketing: one of the game's playable characters is a woman. [1][2]

How he did it is the part that should embarrass everyone involved. Kurtaj was already on bail for other hacking offenses. Police had moved him into a Travelodge in Bicester for his own protection, because rival hackers were threatening him over stolen Bitcoin. His computer had been taken away. So he plugged an Amazon Fire Stick into the hotel room TV, added a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, and broke into one of the biggest game studios in the world. [2] The CPS describes the same setup: a mobile phone, the hotel TV's Wi-Fi and a firestick. [1]

The rest of the list is not small. Prosecutors say his BT/EE intrusion in 2021 fed a wave of unauthorized SIM swaps, which let criminals take over victims' phone numbers and, through them, bank and social media accounts. He compromised information belonging to thousands of Revolut customers. At Uber he stole and deleted data and accounts, an attack the CPS puts at more than $2.8 million in costs. [1] The BBC reports the court heard his hacks caused around £10 million in damage and that the gang stole at least $300,000 from individual crypto investors. [2] He also admitted having a mobile phone in prison in March 2026, which he used to send a mocking message to 999, Britain's emergency line. [1]

This case took a strange road to get here. In 2023, doctors judged Kurtaj unfit for a normal trial because of the severity of his autism. A jury instead decided only whether he did the acts, not whether he meant to, and in December 2023 he was ordered held in a secure hospital indefinitely. A routine assessment late last year found him fit to stand trial, he was moved to a regular prison in January, and on Monday he pleaded guilty to every count. [2]

Meanwhile, the game he stole from is days from release. According to Pixel Herald, GTA VI unlocks on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S on Nov. 19, at $79.99 for the Standard edition and $99.99 for the Ultimate. [3]

**Commentary.** Rockstar lost $5 million and a marketing reveal; players lost nothing, and arguably the leak only made the wait louder. The people who actually got hurt were the ones whose phone numbers were hijacked and whose savings were drained. That is where the sentence on Nov. 23 should land hardest. And somebody in British law enforcement should have to explain how a teenager on bail for hacking, under police protection, with his computer confiscated, still had a TV with Wi-Fi.
