After years of rumors and screen tests, the answer is Robert Pattinson.

Amazon MGM Studios announced Monday, Nov. 16, that Pattinson will play James Bond in the 26th film in the series. Denis Villeneuve, of the Dune films and Blade Runner 2049, directs from a script by Steven Knight, creator of Peaky Blinders. Amy Pascal and David Heyman produce, with Tanya Lapointe as executive producer. [1][2] The BBC reports the film is due in 2028. Its title and exact date have not been announced. [2]

Pattinson, 40 and born in London, is the seventh actor to play Bond in the official series, after Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton, Pierce Brosnan and Daniel Craig. [2] His statement: "James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honour of a lifetime." [1]

How it was announced is its own story. Pierce Brosnan was unveiled at a Mayfair hotel in 1994. Daniel Craig rode a Royal Marines speedboat down the Thames in 2005. This time, Variety reports, the official 007 Instagram account started deleting thousands of old posts early Monday. Just before 3 p.m. London time, it posted a ten-second clip reading "Identity Confirmed." Four minutes later came the video of Pattinson in a black dinner jacket and bow tie, his face half in shadow. Variety reports that even some of Amazon's own publicists were kept in the dark until last week. [3]

The bigger change is who owns the character. Variety reported that Amazon paid $8.5 billion for MGM in 2022, mostly for Bond, and a further $1 billion last year to take creative control from the Broccoli family, which had run the films since Cubby Broccoli launched them with Dr. No in 1962. [3] This is the first Bond film since Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson stepped back. When it opens, it will have been seven years since No Time to Die in 2021, the longest gap in the franchise's history. [2]

Variety reported that the Broccolis' rule was to cast an actor less famous than the character, so no one outshone 007. Amazon broke it on day one. Pattinson led Twilight, which made more than $3 billion worldwide, and The Batman, and starred in Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey this year. [2][3] He also has a long run of small independent films, including The Lighthouse and Good Time. [2] The BBC reports that Josh O'Connor, Callum Turner and Jack Lowden were said to be in the running, and that Amazon "ultimately went with a household name." [2] Buckingham Palace posted: "Welcome to His Majesty's Secret Service, Mr Bond." [2] Reports that his contract covers fewer films than the four discussed with other actors have not been confirmed. [2]

**Commentary.** Pattinson is a good actor who has spent fifteen years earning his way out of a vampire franchise, and he may make a fine Bond. The worry is not him. It is that Britain's most famous fictional servant of the Crown now answers to a shopping company in Seattle, and the first thing it did was trade a press conference for an Instagram drop. Bond survived Roger Moore in a clown suit. He can survive a quarterly earnings call. But fans are right to watch what else gets "modernized" on the way to 2028.
