Some albums flop. Glitter became shorthand for a pop star's career hitting bottom. On Sunday, the site best known for being hard to please said the music deserved better.

Pitchfork's Sunday Review on Nov. 22, by Jackson Howard, scored Mariah Carey's 2001 soundtrack 8.2 out of 10. [1][2][3] The Sunday Review is Pitchfork's weekly look back at a significant album from the past. [1]

**The verdict, in his words.** Howard separates the record from the film. "The movie is bad," he writes. The album, "over which Carey had complete creative control, unlike the film—is very good. At times, it's great. And in certain places, the soundtrack to Glitter is downright transcendent." [1] He does not oversell it. He calls Glitter "hardly Carey's masterpiece," gives that title jointly to Butterfly and The Emancipation of Mimi, and says it is better understood as "a mixtape, a loose, somewhat haphazardly sequenced collection of homages and collaborations." [1]

What he praises is the craftsmanship. Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, then at the height of their work with Janet Jackson, produced nearly the entire album. Rick James wrote and produced "All My Life," in one of his last collaborations before his death. [1] Howard calls the vocal on the ballad "Lead the Way," co-written with longtime collaborator Walter Afanasieff, "jaw-dropping." [1] "This isn't cheap retro pastiche," he writes. "It's a music obsessive receiving the resources to swing for the fences." [1]

**Why it was buried.** Glitter was Carey's first album after a $100 million deal with Virgin, which Pitchfork calls the largest recording contract of its kind at the time. [1] Carey later described what followed, in her 2020 memoir The Meaning of Mariah Carey, as a collision of bad luck, "bad timing, and sabotage," as Pitchfork quotes it. [1] Its U.S. release date was Sept. 11, 2001. [1][2]

The numbers that followed were brutal. R&B site RNBEATS0, citing Wikipedia, reported that the album opened at No. 7 on the Billboard 200 with 116,000 copies, against 323,000 for her previous album, Rainbow, and that Virgin bought out her contract within months. The lead single "Loverboy" still reached No. 2 on the Hot 100. [2]

**Why now.** A 25th-anniversary deluxe edition came out Oct. 30, putting the album back on streaming services after it was pulled in January, with 15 extra tracks, including remixes by Channel Tres and MJ Cole and a Rochelle Jordan feature, RNBEATS0 reported. [2] Pitchfork says the reissue "confirms what devoted Carey fans have long known." [1] Those fans got there first. Their #JusticeForGlitter campaign sent the album to No. 1 on iTunes in November 2018, according to RNBEATS0. [2] "I made this album with so much love and care, so it was devastating to watch it become a punchline," Carey said when the deluxe edition came out, as RNBEATS0 quoted it. [2]

**Commentary.** Glitter is a small lesson about consensus. In 2001 the critics, the late-night jokes and the sales charts all said the same thing, and they said it about a movie and an album as if they were one product. Fans who kept listening didn't wait for anyone's permission. They pushed the album up the iTunes chart 17 years later. A quarter-century on, a tastemaker has caught up with them. Trust your own ears. The crowd, and the critics, can take 25 years to come around.
