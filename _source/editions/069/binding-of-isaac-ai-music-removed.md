The run arrived in the same update that brought The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ to PS5, Xbox, and Switch 2 on November 19, a release date publisher Nicalis picked to go head-to-head with GTA 6 [1]. Entering the seed V1CE BSMT unlocks a Vice City-inspired daily run with its own soundtrack, made up of seven songs plus intros and outros [1]. Polygon describes the parody's mechanics: players drive around in a cart like it's a car, with GTA's star rating and cop chases as they kill enemies [2].

Then the players did what players do. A player on the Binding of Isaac subreddit extracted the run's music from the game files and found several tracks still carrying metadata reading "made with suno," along with links to the songs on Suno's site [1]. The metadata on one track shows it was created on October 30, 2026, less than three weeks before the update went live, and other players checked the files and confirmed the find [1]. The run itself is credited to a modder known as Mr. Headcrab, who the original poster says is "staunchly anti-AI" and had no idea the music was generated [1].

The disclosure question is the sharp edge. Valve requires developers to disclose AI-generated content on a game's Steam page, but Repentance+'s store listing makes no mention of it [1]. The DLC sat at Overwhelmingly Negative recent reviews, with only 10% of 908 reviews from the past 30 days positive at Dexerto's reporting — though the same update was also blamed for breaking mods and causing crashes, and this paper does not assign every negative review to the music [1]. One player's line, with dozens of upvotes: "They used AI when synthwave is like… the easiest genre to make" [1].

Creator Edmund McMillen addressed the backlash on X on November 21: "Woke up to a shit show, obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned" [1]. Within hours, Nicalis pushed a new build listing the "removal of generated music files" at the top of its patch notes, alongside fixes for mods broken by the launch update; the patch was live on PC, with console versions submitted to Microsoft, Nintendo, and Sony for approval [1]. Polygon confirms the music was removed in the November 21 update and replaced with tracks that presumably don't use generative AI [2]. McMillen has previously called AI-generated games "uninspired and bland," in a 2024 TikTok reported by Polygon [2]. How the music got into the build has not been explained in the reporting checked for this edition.

Commentary: Judge it by the player atmosphere, as this desk always does. Nobody review-bombed a synthwave track. They review-bombed the discovery — in the file tags, after purchase, with no disclosure on the store page — that a beloved indie game had shipped generated music its own creator says he never approved. The fix took hours once players found it. The trust question is why it took players to find it.
