The 44th Golden Joystick Awards were held in London on Wednesday, Nov. 11, hosted by Alix Wilton Regan and streamed on YouTube, Twitch, Steam, TikTok and X — and the people's choice was clear, if only barely. Resident Evil Requiem won Ultimate Game of the Year, plus Console Game of the Year, Best Game Trailer ("Evil Has Always Had a Name"), and Best Lead Performer for Angela Sant'Albano as Grace Ashcroft. Four awards, the joint-highest haul of the night. [1][2]

The vote was razor-close: Requiem took 17 percent, Phantom Blade Zero 16 percent, Crimson Desert 15 percent. Phantom Blade Zero had been nominated on Oct. 26, before its Oct. 29 release on PS5 and PC, and voting closed Oct. 30 — so it had been out barely a day when the ballots shut. [1] That is either a testament to the demo's pull or a warning that the Joysticks' voting window needs a rethink. Either way, a one-point margin between the top two is the kind of result that keeps arguments going until The Game Awards on Dec. 10.

Elsewhere on the night: Crimson Desert won PC Game of the Year, The Witcher 4 won Most Wanted Game, Capcom was named Studio of the Year, and Dispatch won Best Storytelling and Best Soundtrack. [1] GTA VI and the Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake were ineligible — GTA VI launches Nov. 19 and Zelda came out Nov. 5, both after the nomination cutoff — so both remain live for The Game Awards. [1]

But the surprise of the night was Meccha Chameleon, a viral co-op hide-and-seek game made by two developers in Japan in a couple of months. It matched Requiem's four awards: Best Multiplayer, Best Indie Game – Self-Published, Streamers' Choice, and Developers' Choice. [1] A two-person team tying Capcom's flagship horror title, award for award, at a show decided mostly by public vote — that is not a cute footnote. That is the audience telling the industry exactly what it wants: something small, strange, and fun to play with friends, made by people who clearly loved making it.

The show also premiered Conan: The Black Pyramid, a licensed action roguelite RPG from Games Farm and Grindstone, coming to PC via Steam in 2027 with consoles to follow; playtest sign-ups are open now. [3]

The Joysticks have always been the people's awards — 22 of the 25 categories are decided by public vote — and this year the people split their affection between the biggest horror game of the year and the smallest game on the list. Both deserved it. The lesson for publishers is the same one it always is: the audience will vote for the $70 blockbuster, but it will love the $5.99 oddity just as hard, and sometimes harder.
