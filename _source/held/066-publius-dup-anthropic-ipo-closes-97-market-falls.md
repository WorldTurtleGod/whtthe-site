Anthropic is a public company. Wall Street had a bad day anyway.

The maker of the Claude chatbot priced its initial public offering at $83 a share late Wednesday, Reuters reported. [1] Reuters reported that the price valued Anthropic at about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis and made it the largest stock-market debut on record, eclipsing SpaceX's in June. [2] The BBC reported the company raised a record $81 billion before its debut; SpaceX, which listed in June, raised $75 billion. [3]

The stock opened on the Nasdaq shortly before noon Eastern on Thursday at $111.50 and climbed as high as $115, 38.6% above the offer price, according to Reuters. Its market value briefly passed $2 trillion. Then it slid. Shares closed 17.1% above the offer price. [1] The BBC put the closing price at $97.17. [3]

Everything else went down. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1,101.11 points, or 2.19%, to 49,097.06. The S&P 500 lost 2.02% to 7,175.83, its sixth straight losing session, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.25%. Nvidia dropped 3.3%. AMD fell 7.7% after investigative outlet Hunterbrook said its affiliated fund had shorted the stock, Reuters reported. [1]

"The Anthropic IPO is attracting gobs of attention," Jed Ellerbroek, a portfolio manager at Argent Capital, told Reuters. "That money is coming from somewhere; investors are making changes in their portfolio to incorporate Anthropic." He said his own expectations for data-center spending have come down, citing Nvidia's troubles and the midterm elections: "Data centers are going to be more expensive to build and they're going to be built more slowly." [1]

Money is also getting more expensive. Reuters reported that futures markets are pricing a 96.4% chance that the Federal Reserve raises rates by a quarter point in December, according to CME's FedWatch tool. [1]

Here is who got rich. Each of Anthropic's seven co-founders, Dario and Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish and Chris Olah, owns roughly 240 million to 244 million shares, according to a Nov. 10 SEC filing cited by the BBC. At Thursday's close, each stake is worth $23.3 billion to $23.7 billion. [3] Amazon owns about 1.92 billion shares, or 14.7% of the company, worth about $186.7 billion at the close. Google owns about 1.79 billion shares, or 13.7%, worth about $173.9 billion, the BBC calculated. [3]

Rank-and-file employees did well too, and fast. The BBC reported that Anthropic's prospectus lets employees sell up to 10% of their shares on the first day of trading and another 35% next year, after the company reports its first full-year results. Most companies lock employees out for three to six months. [3]

**Commentary.** An IPO is the healthiest thing a private giant can do. It forces audited numbers into the open and lets a retiree in Ohio own the same share as a venture fund, instead of watching insiders trade it among themselves. But read the ownership table. Amazon and Google, two companies that sell AI of their own, together hold more than 28% of Anthropic. That's not a scandal. It is a fact anyone buying at $97 should weigh, along with a Fed expected to raise rates and an S&P 500 that has lost six sessions in a row. Buy the numbers, not the hype, and don't let anyone in Washington tell you a stock this big is too important to fall.
