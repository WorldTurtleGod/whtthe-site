The U.S. Department of Education tried to borrow the biggest game launch in history to make a policy argument. The evidence it posted undercut the argument.

On Saturday, Nov. 21, the department's official X account, @usedgov, posted a screenshot of a Google search with an AI-generated overview, captioned: "If it's not allowed in Vice City, it shouldn't be allowed here." [1][2] Vice City is the fictional Miami-style setting of Grand Theft Auto VI, which Rockstar Games released on Nov. 19.

The AI answer in the screenshot, as HuffPost quoted it, reads: "No, 'Grand Theft Auto VI' does not feature gender-segregated sports leagues where a male character can join a girls' team." [1] Newsweek quoted the same answer without the word "male." [2] Either way, the sentence does not say the game bans boys from girls' teams. It says the game has no such leagues. There is no Vice City rule to copy.

That is the whole problem. What the Fact, a fact-checking site, rated the post's implied claim false. It found the screenshot was real but said "the argument built on it reads the opposite of what it says." It also flagged an open question it could not settle: whether the department cropped a longer AI answer down to its first section. [3] As of Sunday, Nov. 22, the post was still live, according to the same check. [3]

HuffPost reported that GTA VI has no joinable sports leagues at all, and that the game includes the franchise's first nonbinary character, Cygnus, voiced by trans actor Jesse James Rose. [1] Critics piled on. "There's a literacy crisis at DoE," constitutional law professor Anthony Michael Kreis wrote on X, according to HuffPost. [1]

The policy fight behind the post is real and serious. Newsweek reported that the Trump administration argues Title IX, the 1972 law barring sex discrimination in federally funded education, requires female sports categories to be based on biological sex. In February 2025, President Trump signed an executive order directing agencies to oppose transgender women and girls competing in female categories. The department has opened investigations and threatened federal funding in states including Kansas, North Carolina, Michigan and Maryland, and transgender students and advocates are challenging those restrictions in court. [2] Newsweek said it asked the department, Rockstar and the ACLU for comment. [2] No department response appears in the coverage we reviewed.

Rockstar, for its part, has said nothing about being drafted into the Title IX debate.

**Commentary.** Set the sports question aside, because this isn't about where you land on it. A cabinet department spends taxpayer money, and its official account speaks with the authority of the United States. When it backs a policy that can strip school districts of federal funds, the least it owes the public is evidence that says what the department claims it says. Instead it posted a machine-written search summary, apparently without reading it, and treated a video game as precedent. This is a government that couldn't read its own screenshot. Agencies with the power to investigate your kid's school should be held to a higher standard than a meme account.
