At launch, this paper pointed out that Rockstar had not published a sales number for Grand Theft Auto VI. On Friday it did.

Rockstar Games, a label of Take-Two Interactive, said Friday, Nov. 20, that GTA VI's "day one retail sales" reached $3.79 billion. The company called that "a new record for entertainment across any medium" and "the biggest entertainment launch in history." [1] The release also says the game "sold 32.5 million units pre-release," which it calls a record across all platforms. [1]

**Read the fine print.** An asterisk in the Take-Two release says the $3.79 billion is "based on company estimates through end of day November 19th worldwide," the day the game launched. [1] So this is the publisher's own count, not an audit. It is also a figure for "retail sales," meaning what buyers paid in stores and digital storefronts. It is not Take-Two's revenue, which comes out later in its SEC filings.

**The trackers count lower.** Before launch, Sensor Tower estimated about 17.1 million pre-launch copies worth roughly $1.65 billion, as this paper reported at launch. After Friday's release, Reuters reported that Sensor Tower estimated 24.5 million copies and $2.36 billion in pre-order revenue. [2] That is still about 8 million copies short of Rockstar's 32.5 million. The gap may come from what each side counts, such as retail boxes, bundles and storefronts the tracker can't see. Neither side has explained it, so we can't resolve it.

**The comparisons.** Reuters and the Guardian both say $3.79 billion is nearly five times the roughly $800 million GTA V made on its first day in 2013. [2][3] The Guardian, writing with the AP and AFP, added that the one-day figure beats the whole box-office run of Avengers: Endgame, about $2.9 billion worldwide. [3] Reuters reported that Take-Two is estimated to have spent $1 billion or more producing the game, making it one of the most expensive entertainment projects ever. [2]

"We are incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world," Rockstar founder Sam Houser said in the release. [1] Before launch, Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick had been cautious. "We tend to avoid claiming success before it occurs," he said on the company's earnings call earlier this month, according to Reuters. [2]

**What's still missing.** There is still no PC version and no online mode. Reuters reported that investors are waiting on Take-Two's plans for both and that some analysts expect them in 2027. [2] TD Cowen analyst Doug Creutz told Reuters that "'when' and 'how big' the next GTA Online will be is the most important question for the stock, more so than GTA VI unit sales." [2] The game launched Nov. 19 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Its 99 on Metacritic is, by Rockstar's account, the highest rating ever. [1][3]

**Commentary.** This paper said it would print the official number when one came out, and it now has. The size is real: by Take-Two's own count, players spent billions on one game in one day without any government program or subsidy. But a company estimate with an asterisk is still marketing until it shows up in an audited SEC filing. That filing is what counts, and the difference between the two figures is the part to watch.
