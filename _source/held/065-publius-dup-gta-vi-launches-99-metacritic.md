Thirteen years after Grand Theft Auto V, the sequel is here, and the critics are close to unanimous.

Grand Theft Auto VI went on sale Thursday on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series consoles, rolling out from midnight local time around the world. [1][2] By launch it held a score of 99 out of 100 on Metacritic, based on more than 150 reviews. [1] Deadline and NME both report that ties The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time for the highest-rated game in the site's history. [3][2]

The praise is not subtle. IGN's Luke Reilly called it "the single most impressive video game I have ever played." [1] The Washington Post called it an "astonishing American epic," and The Guardian gave it five stars for "the most extraordinarily, indulgently detailed virtual world that we have yet seen," according to Deadline's roundup. [3] The New York Times review, headlined "Grand Theft Auto VI Will Steal Your Heart," said the game "largely lives up to its sky-high expectations." [3]

Not every review was a love letter. Polygon, which praised it as one of Rockstar's finest games, said the campaign is weighed down early by tutorial-heavy scene-setting. [4] NME's five-star review told readers to "temper your dream expectations," calling the game "a significant but not superhuman evolution of the franchise." [2]

The story follows Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, a Bonnie-and-Clyde pair pulled into a criminal conspiracy after an easy score goes wrong. [1] The setting is Leonida, a stand-in for Florida, and its big city is Vice City, a stand-in for Miami. [1] Like every GTA before it, it lets players steal cars, run down pedestrians and shoot it out with police. [1] Polygon's review argues the hook is less the violence than "living in a reactive place governed by intricate rules that beg to be bent and busted." [4]

The money is as big as the scores. Al Jazeera, citing the market intelligence firm Sensor Tower, reported pre-orders above an estimated 15.8 million copies as of Nov. 17, worth $1.54 billion. [1] The game's budget is estimated at more than $1 billion, and it was delayed twice from an original 2025 date. [1] Early players in New Zealand, Australia and Japan, the first time zones to get it, flooded social media with clips; USA Today noted that, yes, you can pet the dogs. [5]

It is a console launch: Polygon lists the platforms as PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X. [4]

**Commentary.** Polygon's review notes "decades of moral panic" over this series, and every few years some official promises to protect the children from it. [4] The country survived. Polygon's critic put it well: the game's satire is about who gets to break the rules in America, and the answer it gives is not flattering to the people in charge. [4] Adults buying a mature-rated game with their own money is about as basic a liberty as it gets. The market has spoken, loudly, to the tune of a billion and a half dollars before anyone pressed start.
